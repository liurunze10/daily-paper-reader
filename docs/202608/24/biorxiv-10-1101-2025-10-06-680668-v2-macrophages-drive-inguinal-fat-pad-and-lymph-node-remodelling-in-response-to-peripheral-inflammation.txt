However, the trigger for their release has not been 26 identified. Here we demonstrate that cutaneous inflammation is directly correlated with 27 rapid atrophy of perinodal fat pads and increase in embedded lymph node size. We further 28 demonstrate that the fat pad atrophy is associated with influx of a CCR2-independent, lipid 29 metabolising, macrophage population and that macrophage depletion ameliorates fat pad 30 atrophy, and lymph node expansion, downstream of inflamed sites. Our data therefore 31 identify peripheral inflammation as a trigger of downstream fat pad and lymph node 32 remodelling and contributes to the release of essential nutrients to drive the energetic 33 requirements of the adaptive immune response. 34 35 Significance statement. 36 To our knowledge, nobody has previously reported this striking correlation between 37 peripheral inflammation and reciprocal fat pad and lymph node remodelling. Our report of 38 this correlation and our mechanistic results, have clear implications for our understanding of 39 the inflammation-driven rapid release of sources of energy from fat pads to drive the 40 immune response. Our data also potentially shed light on additional aspects of the 41 functionality of pro-inflammatory vaccine adjuvants. We believe that our findings are fully 42 novel and will be of interest to immunologists, infectious disease specialists and researchers 43 interested in adipose tissue derived energetics. 44 45 46

Importantly, these events 51 must take place within the context of an inflammatory, or damage-related, challenge at the 52 site of antigen encounter(2). There is also ample evidence of other peripheral tissue-derived 53 molecules providing additional specificity to the immune response especially in terms of 54 assigning selective tissue-homing properties to the antigen-specific T cells(3, 4). This is 55 therefore a complex response which requires the simultaneous, and coordinated, interaction 56 of multiple cell types and molecules. In mammals this is achieved by integrating all these 57 processes within lymph nodes that lie downstream of all tissues and which are connected to 58 the tissues via the lymphatic vasculature(5). 59 60 In addition to requiring precise coordination of cells and molecules, the immune response is 61 highly energy-intensive and requires rapid access to local molecular energy sources including 62 free fatty acids(6). Indeed, research indicates that there is a trade-off between the high 63 metabolic costs of immune activation and the maintenance of body temperature(7). In this 64 regard, it is notable that all mammalian lymph nodes are embedded in perinodal adipose 65 tissue(8) which contains, not just adipocytes, but also a range of immune cells with 66 macrophages representing the largest resident population. Considerable research indicates a 67 key role for the perinodal adipose tissue as a source of nutrients, released through lipolysis, 68 to feed the immune response(9). However, 316 the trigger for this has remained elusive. Here we demonstrate a clear link between the 317 extent of peripheral cutaneous inflammation and remodelling of draining lymph nodes and 318 perinodal fat pads. Specifically, we see a rapid reduction in the size of the fat pads with a 319 reciprocal increase in the size of the embedded lymph node. This phenotype was seen 320 downstream of fighting wounds, in CCR2-/- male mice, and in WT female mice treated with 321 the inflammatory TLR7 agonist IMQ and was associated with the accumulation of a specific 322 macrophage population in the atrophied fat pads. Notably, this phenotype was observed in 323 both the fighting CCR2-/- males, as well as IMQ treated CCR2-/- females, demonstrating that 324 the phenotype is independent of CCR2 at the peripheral inflamed site and the draining fat 325 pad and lymph node. 326 327 Interestingly, we observed a more robust inflammatory response to fighting-associated 328 wounding in co-housed CCR2-/- male mice compared to isolated CCR2-/- male mice or WT 329 male mice. This is a counterintuitive observation given the non-redundant role for CCR2, and 330 its ligands, in monocyte egress from the bone marrow and recruitment to inflamed sites(11, 331 12, 23). Notably, similar results have been reported from other studies on inflammation in 332 CCR2-/- mice(24-27) and potentially reflect the inability to recruit a specific CCR2-dependent 333 anti-inflammatory macrophage population which would otherwise restrict the extent of the 334 inflammatory response(27).
