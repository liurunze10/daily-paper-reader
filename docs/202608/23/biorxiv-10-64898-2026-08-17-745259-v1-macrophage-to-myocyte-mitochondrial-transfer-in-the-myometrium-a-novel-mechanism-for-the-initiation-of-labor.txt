Abstract 39 

Labor initiation involves influx of peripheral monocytes into uterine tissue, where they 40 

differentiate into macrophages (Macs) and polarize toward pro-inflammatory (M1) or anti-41 

inflammatory (M2) phenotypes. Crosstalk between uterine myocytes (MYO) and Macs is 42 

implicated in myometrial activation, but the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Using 43 

immunofluorescence and live-cell imaging, we show that M1-Macs (but not M2-Macs) form 44 

tunneling nanotubes (TNT) with MYOs, enabling direct, unidirectional mitochondrial transfer. 45 

TNT-mediated mitochondrial transfer enhances ATP production and 20α -HSD expression in 46 

MYO. Functionally, M1-Macs induce intracellular and functional progesterone (P4) withdrawal 47 

in MYOs via 20α -HSD mediated P4 metabolism and increased PR-A phosphorylation. In vivo, 48 

transfer of monocytes, carrying dendra-labelled mitochondria in pregnant mice confirm their 49 

myometrial recruitment, differentiation, polarization to M1-Macs, and M1-Mac/MYO 50 

mitochondrial transfer. Increased M1-Macs and mitochondrial transfer to MYOs are observed 51 

before labor onset, implicating M1-Macs/MYO interaction as key regulator of labor initiation and 52 

potentially a therapeutic target to prevent preterm birth. 53

Introduction: 57 

The mechanisms governing the initiation of labor are complex and involve coordinated 58 

interactions between the maternal reproductive and immune systems. Our previous studies have 59 

demonstrated a strong correlation between maternal immune cell infiltration into the uterine 60 

smooth muscle (myometrium) and the initiation of labor, both at term (TL) and preterm (PTL) 1, 2,

M1-polarized Macrophages physically connect with Myocytes through tunneling nanotubes 329 

We examined cell-cell connectivity between human myometrial smooth muscle cells 330 

(hMYOs) and Macs using a “parachute assay”. Human M1 -polarized Macs (hM1-Macs), derived 331 

from peripheral blood monocytes of term pregnant women or from monocytic cell line THP-1 332 

were loaded with calcein dye and co-cultured with primary hMYOs derived from myometrial 333 

biopsies from TNL women or with myometrial cell line hTERT-HM A/B at a ratio of 1:100. 334 

Fluorescent microscopy revealed physical connections between hM1-Macs and hMYOs within 335 

two hours of co-culture. Distinct tubular membrane projections were seen extending from the 336

Discussion 499 

The molecular mechanisms driving the transition of the pregnant myometrium from 500 

quiescence to contractility during labor remains poorly understood. A substantial and growing 501 

body of evidence implicates the immune system as key contributor of both term and preterm labor. 502 

The present study demonstrates, for the first time, a novel mechanism of communication between 503 

the maternal immune and reproductive systems that triggers labor (via P4 withdrawal) and supports 504 

the energetic demands of uterine contractions. 505 

TNT-mediated open-channel connections between M1-Macs and MYO in the pregnant 506 

uterus facilitate intercellular cargo transfer. This interaction leads to myometrial P4 withdrawal, 507

The temporal pattern of these events suggests a mediating role of this interaction in the initiation 509 

of labor. Among the two classical Mac subtypes (M1 and M2), only M1‑Macs show the capability 510 

to form rapid and direct TNT connections with MYOs, as evidenced by the high expression of 511 

TNT marker TNAIP2 in M1-Macs and mitochondrial transfer exclusively from M1-Macs to MYO. 512 

In contrast, M2-Macs do not exhibit these features ( Supplemental Figure 6 ). Evidence suggests 513 

that M2 macrophages can form TNT connections in other tissues 31 ; however, in our study, we did 514 

not observe TNT formation with uterine myocytes by M2-Macs derived from PBMCs or THP-1 515 

monocytes. Recent studies have reported the presence of TNT-mediated communication among 516 

myeloid cells, including Macs, that support intercellular signaling, pathogen transfer, and the 517 

coordination of immune responses 32 . Macs have been shown to form both homotypic (Mac –Mac) 518 

and heterotypic (Mac –other cell types) TNT connections with epithelial, stromal, HIV-infected 519 

and tumor cells 32, 33, 34 however, to our knowledge, this is the first evidence demonstrating TNT 520 

formation between M1-Macs and uterine MYOs, with the functional consequence of this 521 

interaction linked to the onset of labor. 522

particularly intriguing and suggests that this process may be tightly regulated, potentially driven 524

nearby and distant MYOs suggests that even a small population of activated M1-Macs can 527 

efficiently propagate signals and provide metabolic support across the tissue within a short 528 

timeframe. This concept is further supported by the observed increase in MYO-MYO connectivity 529 

in the presence of M1-Macs, as indicated by the distribution of M1-derived cargo among myocytes 530

The increased ATP levels in MYOs suggest that functional mitochondria are transferred from 553 

M1-Macs to MYOs and can be utilized by MYOs as an energy resource to facilitate the process of 554 

labor. The increase of ATP levels in M1-Mac/MYO co-cultures is attributed to mitochondrial 555 

transfer from M1-Macs to MYO. This interpretation is supported by the experiments using 556 

pretreatment of M1-Macs with metformin, which inhibited both mitochondrial transfer and the 557 

associated rise in ATP levels in myocytes. The inhibitory effect of metformin on transcellular 558 

mitochondrial transfer may be explained by the energy dependence of intracellular mitochondrial 559 

trafficking. Mitochondrial movement relies on interactions with cytoskeletal and motor protein 560 

complexes, including kinesin/Miro1, along actin filaments within TNTs. Transport through TNTs 561 

is mediated by motor proteins such as kinesin and dynein, a process that requires ATP 35 .562 

Furthermore, TNT formation itself is driven by actin polymerization, an active and ATP‐dependent 563 

process 37 . Consequently, metformin-mediated inhibition of cellular ATP production 38 likely 564 

impairs both the formation of TNT structures and the active transport of mitochondria across these 565 

intercellular connections. 566 

Using a murine model ubiquitously expressing MitoDendra 19 , our study uniquely provides 567 

temporal tracking of monocyte recruitment and their fate in the myometrium through adoptive 568 

transfer of mitoDendra2-labelled monocytes. The detection of fluorescently labelled mitochondria 569 

enabled us to trace monocyte infiltration in the pregnant murine uterus, confirm their 570 

differentiation into tissue-resident Macs, characterize their polarization into M1 and M2 subtypes, 571 

and to directly demonstrate the mitochondrial transfer from mitoDendra-Macs to mMYOs in 572 

pregnant dams at term. 573

into Macs within 24 hours. The infiltrated Macs exhibit both M1 and M2 phenotypes; however, 575

onset. They communicate with SMCs, and transfer mitochondria via TNTs, which not only 584 

contributes to the induction of P4 withdrawal but also enhances the energetic capacity of the 585 

myometrium by increasing ATP availability, thereby supporting the high energy demands of labor. 586 

This study fills key gaps in delineating the cellular and molecular interplay between the immune 587 

system and the pregnant myometrium and defines the role of M1-Mac-MYO interactions in 588 

triggering P4 withdrawal and myometrial activation. We speculate that targeting key processes 589 

including Mac infiltration, M1-polarization, TNT formation, M1-Mac-MYO communication and 590 

cargo transfer offer novel therapeutic avenues for PTB prevention in high-risk women. 591
