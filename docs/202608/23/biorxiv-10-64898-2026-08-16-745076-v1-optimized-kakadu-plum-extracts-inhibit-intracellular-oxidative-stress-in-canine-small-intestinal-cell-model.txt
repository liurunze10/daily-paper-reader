## Abstract 16 Kakadu plum ( Terminalia ferdinandiana ), an Australian native fruit, is among the 17 richest known dietary sources of vitamin C and hydrolysable tannins, yet its capacity to 18 protect the intestinal epithelium against oxidative stress remains largely unexplored. This 19 study optimised the extraction of bioactive compounds from freeze-dried Kakadu plum 20 powder and evaluated their antioxidant activity using both chemical and cellular antioxidant 21 

in vitro assay. Phenolic compounds were extracted using three solvents (water, 80% ethanol, 22 and 80% methanol) combined with shaking, ultrasound, or microwave assistance. Solvent, 23 rather than processing technique, was the dominant determinant of antioxidant capacity: 24 ethanol and methanol maximised total phenolic content, total flavonoid content, and DPPH 25 radical-scavenging activity, whereas water extracts showed the highest ferric-reducing 26 antioxidant power. Twenty-four phenolic compounds identified by HPLC-ESI-QTOF-27 MS/MS were mapped by network pharmacology to nine core oxidative-stress targets, and 28 cross-species molecular docking predicted conserved binding of key phenolics to canine 29 orthologs of PTGS2 and MMP2. In an H2O2-induced oxidative-stress in vitro cell model 30 using canine small intestinal epithelial cells, both water ( ≤25 μg/mL) and ethanol ( ≤250 31 μg/mL) extracts significantly suppressed intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) in a 32 dose-dependent manner, with the ethanol extract effective across a wider concentration range. 33 This work demonstrated that Kakadu plum extract could be a promising natural, multi-target 34 antioxidant ingredient for canine intestinal health, and provided a reference for future in vivo 35 research. 36

## 1. Introduction 41 Kakadu plum ( Terminalia ferdinandiana ), a fruit native to northern Australia and 42 traditionally used by Aboriginal communities as both food and medicine, has attracted 43 growing scientific and commercial interest as a functional-food ingredient (Chaliha, 2018). 44 The fruit is distinguished by an exceptionally high ascorbic acid (vitamin C, 120.0-224.9 45 mg/g dry weight) content and an abundance of hydrolysable tannins, ellagitannins, and 46 phenolic acids, placing it among the most antioxidant-rich fruits characterised to date 47 (Konczak et al., 2014; J. Li et al., 2023). These phytochemicals underpin a broad range of 48 reported bioactivities and have prompted the incorporation of Kakadu plum into beverages, 49 nutraceuticals, cosmetics, and natural preservative systems, and premium pet foods (Bobasa 50 et al., 2022). Despite the potential use of Kakadu plum, its extraction and conversion into 51 bioactive-enriched ingredients is underdeveloped. The recovery of phenolics from plant 52 matrices is strongly influenced by the extraction solvent and the assisting technology, which 53 together govern the release of free and bound phenolics (Hossain et al., 2021). Techniques 54 such as ultrasound- and microwave-assisted extraction have been used to release tannins and 55 other bound phenolics from related matrices (Hernández-Corroto et al., 2022). However, how 56 solvent polarity and processing technique jointly affect the antioxidant profile of Kakadu 57 plum across its free, bound, and total phenolic fractions has not been systematically studied. 58 Oxidative stress, arising from an imbalance between reactive oxygen species (ROS) 59 production and antioxidant defence, is a central driver of intestinal epithelial injury, barrier 60 dysfunction, and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) (Sies et al., 2022). Diet-derived 61 polyphenols can counteract this imbalance by scavenging radicals, chelating transition metals, 62 and modulating redox-sensitive signalling. Kakadu plum extracts have shown antioxidant, 63 anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, anticancer, and hepatoprotective activities across a range of 64 chemical and cellular models (Akter et al., 2021; Alwis et al., 2025; Ramadhania et al., 2022), 65 and their phenolics are extensively biotransformed during colonic fermentation, implying 66 direct interaction with the gut epithelium (Adiamo et al., 2024). Nonetheless, most of the 67 evidence in vitro at cellular level derives from cancer or non-intestinal cell lines, and the 68 ability of Kakadu plum phenolics to protect normal intestinal epithelium from oxidative stress 69 remains to be explored. Furthermore, to the best of our knowledge, no H O-induced 70 oxidative stress model has been established for canine intestinal epithelial cells. 71 To address these gaps, the present study aimed to 1. optimise the solvent and 72 processing conditions for extracting phenolics from Kakadu plum and 2. characterise their 73

2.4. Network pharmacology analysis 182 A small molecule pharmacology workflow was used to link the identified compounds 183 to oxidative-stress biology and to prioritise compound-target pairs for structural validation. 184 The analysis was proceeded in five stages: 1. compound-target prediction and disease-185 oriented filtering; 2. Network pharmacology analysis including compound-target interaction, 186 protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction and functional enrichment analysis; 3. 187 multi-stage ADMET and developability screening; 4. cross-species ortholog mapping and 188 comparative validation; and 5. pharmacokinetic compartment screening followed by 189 molecular docking of the most feasible pairs. A pipeline of the forementioned in silico 190 analysis has been established by our group and will be made available on PangenomeAI 191 platform https://github.com/PangenomeAI. 192

2.4.2. Multi-stage ADMET and developability screening 209 The pharmacokinetic and toxicity profiles of the network-derived candidates were 210 evaluated using an integrated ADMET screening funnel. Physicochemical properties and 211 drug-likeness (Lipinski’s Rule of Five and Veber’s criteria) were calculated using RDKit 212 (v2023.09.1). Mechanistic toxicokinetic parameters, including steady-state 216 plasma concentration and total clearance, were estimated via the httk (v2.2.1) R package 217 using a three-compartment model. 218 

2.4.3. Pharmacokinetic compartment screening and pair feasibility 219 To ensure physiological relevance, compound-target pairs were subjected to a route-220 specific compartment screening protocol using the pair_compartment_screening module 221 developed by our group. The principles of screening are described as below: for the oral 222 administration route, compounds were filtered based on a multi-parameter pharmacokinetic 223 gate: Human Intestinal Absorption (HIA) > 30%, Caco-2 permeability (log Papp) > -5.15, 224 and a preference for non-substrate status regarding P-glycoprotein (P-gp) efflux to ensure 225 sufficient systemic exposure. 226 A compartment-matching algorithm was then applied to verify that the predicted 227 systemic distribution of the compound aligned with the anatomical localization of the target 228 protein. Target localization was retrieved from UniProt and the Human Protein Atlas, while 229 compound accessibility was determined by predicted Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) penetration 230 (for CNS targets) and volume of distribution (Vss). Only "pharmacokinetically feasible" pairs, 231 in which the compound demonstrated both high oral bioavailability and the ability to reach 232 the target's physiological compartment, were carried forward for structural validation. 233 234

## 3. Results and Discussion 314 

3.1 Physicochemical properties of Kakadu plum extract 315 

3.1.1 Chemical antioxidant assay 316 To determine the effect of extraction solvents and extraction technologies on the 317 physicochemical characteristics of Kakadu plum, the freeze-dried KP fruit powder was 318 extracted using different solvents assisted with different processing technologies. 319 Figure 1 revealed that the extraction solvent was the dominant factor influencing 320 chemical antioxidant properties, with no effects of extraction technique or solvent–technique 321 interaction (p > 0.05). This limited impact of extraction technique may be attributed to the 322 exceptionally fine particle size of the Kakadu plum powder (filtered through a 1000-mesh 323 screen), as milling-induced cell wall disruption facilitates efficient mass transfer and near-324 complete release of bioactive compounds. Smaller particle sizes have been shown to enhance 325 the antioxidant capacity of plant extracts like tea and ginger (Makanjuola, 2017), and the 326 thorough release of phenolics likely renders additional extraction techniques redundant. 327 Therefore, subsequent analysis focused on comparing antioxidant capacity across the three 328 extraction solvents. 329

## 4. Conclusions 764 Kakadu plum extracts demonstrated suppressing effects on intracellular oxidative 765 stress in the canine intestinal epithelial cell model. Extraction solvent (hydroalcoholic 766 solvents such as ethanol and methanol), instead of processing technique, was the principal 767 determinant of antioxidant capacity. By integrating chemical profiling, network 768 pharmacology, ADMET profiling, cross-species ortholog mapping, and molecular docking, 769 the identified phenolics were linked to ROS related targets, and the binding of key 770 constituents to canine orthologs of PTGS2 and MMP2 were confirmed in silico . In the 771 HO-induced oxidative-stress cell model, both the water and ethanol extracts of Kakadu 772 plum significantly suppressed intracellular ROS in a dose dependent manner, with the ethanol 773 extract effective across a wider concentration range. The chemical, computational, and 774 cellular findings provide a mechanistic and translational rationale for developing Kakadu 775 plum as a natural, multi-target antioxidant ingredient for canine intestinal health. These 776 conclusions are based on commercial Kakadu plum powder, the phenolic profile was 777 characterised qualitatively without individual quantification due to lack of individual 778 standards, and the network-pharmacology and docking predictions were not experimentally 779 validated; these limitations should be addressed in future work. Future research would aim at 780 experimentally validating the predicted molecular interactions, elucidating the underlying 781 molecular mechanisms through whole-transcriptome analysis, and evaluating the effects on 782 intestinal barrier function, inflammatory responses, and in vivo efficacy in animal models. 783 784
