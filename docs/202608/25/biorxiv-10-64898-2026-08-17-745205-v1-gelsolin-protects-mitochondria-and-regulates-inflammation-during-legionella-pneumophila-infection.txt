Abstract 21 

Legionella pneumophila (L. pneumophila ) is the causative agent of Legionnaires’ disease, a severe 22 

bacterial pneumonia. Difficulty in diagnosing Legionnaires’ disease leads to an underreporting of cases 23 

and delayed treatment. Rapid-acting, broad-spectrum therapies are needed to treat pathology while 24 

avoiding antibiotic resistance. We showed that gelsolin knockout ( gsn -/-) mice succumb more quickly to 25 

severe L. pneumophila infection despite no difference in bacterial loads in the lung compared to wild type 26 

mice. There is an increase in CXCL1/KC production from macrophages from gsn -/- mice, which is 27 

accompanied by increased neutrophils and apoptosis in their lungs. Neutrophils lacking gelsolin produce 28 

fewer neutrophil extracellular traps, and their mitochondrial capacity is diminished in response to L. 29 

pneumophila . Gelsolin is required for maintaining mitochondrial network morphology and respiration in 30 

L. pneumophila infected macrophages. When given recombinant gelsolin protein, gsn -/- mice survive 31 

significantly longer during severe L. pneumophila infection, with reduced lung pathology, and the 32 

inflammatory signature of their macrophages was reduced in vitro . Together, gelsolin protects mice during 33 

severe L. pneumophila infection, dampens inflammation, promotes mitochondrial health, and maintains 34 

neutrophil function. 35

Introduction 37 

Legionella pneumophila (L. pneumophila ) is a gram-negative respiratory intracellular pathogen that is the 38 

causative agent of the severe atypical pneumonia Legionnaires’ disease. Contamination of domestic or 39 

industrial water systems, such as air conditioning systems or water-cooling towers, and formation of 40 

biofilms can lead to aerosolization and subsequent inhalation of L. pneumophila (De Filippis et al., 2018; 41 

Fang et al., 2023; Van Heijnsbergen et al., 2015). L. pneumophila is phagocytosed by alveolar macrophages 42 

and secretes effector proteins to halt phagosome-lysosome fusion, creating the L. pneumophila 43 

containing vacuole (LCV) (Horwitz, 1983). During a successful infection cycle, L. pneumophila replicates 44

to the severity of Legionnaires disease. Individuals who are elderly, immunodeficient, or have a history of 53

Gelsolin (GSN) is a calcium-dependent actin severing and capping protein with two isoforms, plasma 57 

(pGSN) and cellular gelsolin (cGSN), that arise through alternative splicing of a single gsn gene (Bryan, 58 

1988; Stella et al., 1994). cGSN regulates cellular processes including actin dynamics, apoptosis, and 59 

mitochondrial homeostasis (García-Bartolomé et al., 2017; Geng et al., 1998; Janmey et al., 1985; 60 

Kothakota et al., 1997a; Koya et al., 2000; Kusano et al., 2000). pGSN contains a 25 amino acid N-terminal 61 

secretion signal that facilitates its secretion, primarily from muscle cells, into the blood, where it 62 

contributes to the clearance of circulating actin and inactivation of inflammatory molecules (Bucki et al., 63 

2005, 2010, 2014; Kwiatkowski et al., 1988; Lind et al., 1986). In humans, decreased pGSN (and increased 64 

F-actin) concentrations in the blood correlate with worsened disease severity and outcome in patients 65 

with a broad range of infections, injuries, and diseases (DiNubile et al., 2002; Esawy et al., 2020; P. S. Lee 66 

et al., 2006, 2008; Self et al., 2019; Shi et al., 2014). Gelsolin is integral to the immune function of 67 

neutrophils and macrophages, and recombinant human gelsolin (rGSN) has been shown to reduce 68

Human caspase-4 and its murine ortholog caspase-11 mediate non-canonical inflammasome activity, a 77 

central innate immune pathway in the response to many intracellular pathogens including L. pneumophila 78 

(Akhter et al., 2012a; Kayagaki et al., 2011). The activation of caspase-4/11 or caspase-1, through non-79 

canonical and canonical inflammasome activation respectively, coordinates a highly inflammatory cell 80 

death termed pyroptosis that is vital for the control of such pathogens (Cerqueira et al., 2015; Kayagaki 81 

et al., 2015). While the role of caspase-1 has been studied in detail in many diseases, novel functions and 82 

substrates of caspase-4/11 continue to be discovered and remain incompletely understood. We have 83 

previously shown that caspase-4/11 mediates several cellular processes including actin organization and 84 

mitochondrial function during infection (Akhter et al., 2012a; Caution et al., 2015a; Krause et al., 2019). 85 

Here we establish gelsolin as a possible substrate candidate of noncanonical inflammasome activation, 86 

given its susceptibility to cleavage by caspase-4, and demonstrate its role in regulating Legionnaires 87 

disease pathology and severity. 88

Gelsolin is required for neutrophil NET formation and mitochondrial function in response to L. 168 

pneumophila 169 

Given the increased expression of the neutrophil-attracting chemokine in the macrophages of gsn -/- mice, 170 

as well as the increased neutrophils in the lung, we next investigated the role of gelsolin regarding intrinsic 171 

neutrophil function in response to L. pneumophila infection. It was previously reported that gelsolin 172 

contributes to NET formation by neutrophils stimulated with ionomycin (Mannherz et al., 2024). We 173 

isolated neutrophils from WT and gsn -/- mice and treated them with L. pneumophila, PMA, or media, and 174 

measured NET formation by confocal microscopy. We found that gsn -/- neutrophils produced significantly 175 

fewer NETs in all conditions (Fig 4A,B). Mitochondrial integrity and function are required for producing 176 

reactive oxygen species (ROS) and ROS-dependent NETs in response to pathogens (Douda et al., 2015). To 177 

determine if gelsolin regulates neutrophil respiration during L. pneumophila infection, we measured the 178 

mitochondrial respiration of WT and gsn -/- neutrophils infected with L. pneumophila by seahorse assay 179 

using the mitochondrial stress test. We found that gsn -/- neutrophils had a significantly lower spare 180 

respiratory capacity at rest and with L. pneumophila infection compared to WT neutrophils (Fig 4C). 181 

Therefore, gelsolin is required for NET formation and maintaining mitochondrial respiration in neutrophils 182 

during L. pneumophila infection. 183

Gelsolin interacts directly with mitochondria and mediates mitochondrial functions including gating 186 

voltage dependent anion channel (VDAC) on the mitochondrial membrane (Kusano et al., 2000). During 187 

L. pneumophila infection, maintenance and augmentation of mitochondria is integral for pathogen 188 

defense and cellular function (Kajiwara et al., 2018). L. pneumophila can target the host mitochondria and 189 

shift metabolism to favor pathogen growth (Escoll et al., 2017; Fu et al., 2022). We investigated the role 190 

of gelsolin in maintaining mitochondrial network connectivity. Macrophages lacking gelsolin have a 191 

significantly higher frequency of mitochondrial network fracturing and rounding compared to WT 192 

macrophages (Fig 5A-C, supplemental movie S1 & S2), indicating a higher degree of stress and dysfunction. 193 

TEM imaging of these cells revealed fewer mitochondria near the bacterium in gsn -/- macrophages 194 

compared to WT macrophages (5D). We analyzed the mitochondrial function of these macrophages by 195 

seahorse assay and observed a significantly decreased spare respiratory capacity in gsn -/- macrophages 196 

compared to WT (5E). Therefore, gelsolin maintains mitochondrial network shape and overall 197 

mitochondrial function during L. pneumophila infection in macrophages. 198

Gelsolin is a canonical substrate of caspase-3, which once activated cleaves gelsolin at the S3-S4 linker to 202 

release the N-terminal half from the C-terminal (Kothakota et al., 1997a). The free N-terminal can sever 203 

actin in a calcium-independent manner and help execute the apoptotic functions of caspase-3 (Geng et 204 

al., 1998; Kothakota et al., 1997b). We have previously shown that caspase-11 activation alters actin 205 

dynamics in macrophages during L. pneumophila infection (Akhter et al., 2012a; Caution et al., 2015a). 206 

Due to the proteolytic nature of caspase-4/11 and gelsolin’s susceptibility to cleavage by other caspases, 207 

we sought to determine if human caspase-4 could cleave human recombinant gelsolin protein. We 208 

incubated recombinant human gelsolin with or without either recombinant human caspase-4, caspase-1, 209

Discussion 220 

L. pneumophila causes the atypical community acquired pneumonia known as Legionnaires’ disease, a 221 

severe pneumonia that can be fatal in vulnerable populations (Dartevel et al., 2025; Lupia et al., 2023). Of 222 

particular concern is that L. pneumophila outbreaks are most prevalent in artificial water systems and 223 

buildings with complex water systems, including hospitals, retirement homes, and residential buildings, 224 

where vulnerable populations are exposed (De Filippis et al., 2018; Fang et al., 2023; Van Heijnsbergen et 225 

al., 2015). This necessitates the development of effective host-targeting treatments that are rapid-acting, 226 

broad spectrum, and non-immunogenic without contributing to antibiotic resistance. Additionally, L. 227 

pneumophila is a professional intracellular pathogen that alters cell biological pathways including actin 228 

machinery, metabolism, and inflammatory signaling to establish infection. To this effect, we show that 229 

rGSN administration improves the lung pathology of L. pneumophila infected mice at early timepoints, 230 

highlighting the potential of rGSN as a rapid-acting treatment for acutely infected patients. rGSN 231 

administration also improved the survival and lung function of infected mice, suggesting its potential to 232 

improve overall patient outcomes during severe Legionnaires’ disease. 233

Although rGSN administration did not contribute to bacterial clearance in our study, intravenous or 235 

respiratory administration of rGSN at higher, more frequent doses may allow for improved delivery of 236 

rGSN to the lung. Additionally, while rGSN has been shown to bind to lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and 237 

improve bacterial killing in some animal models of infection, it is possible that the effects on L. 238 

pneumophila may differ, due to its intracellular life cycle or atypical LPS structure (Bucki et al., 2005; Piktel 239 

et al., 2020; Zaehringer et al., 1995) . Legionnaires’ disease is often a difficult and neglected diagnosis, 240 

hence its classification as an atypical pneumonia (Mulazimoglu & Yu, 2001; Shimada et al., 2009; Vergis et 241 

al., 2000). Broad-spectrum host-directed therapeutics may provide an option for immediate treatment of 242 

such severe pneumonias when diagnosis is pending. Administering rGSN alongside antibiotics may be 243 

effective in limiting organ damage and disease progression while improving recovery. 244

Plasma gelsolin is abundant in the blood (Smith DB, Janmey PA, Herbert TJ, 1987). During an infection or 246 

injury, pGSN is consumed at the site of insult where it rapidly clears inflammatory and cytotoxic molecules, 247 

primarily filamentous actin released from dead cells, resulting in a measurable decrease in circulating 248 

pGSN (Bucki et al., 2005; Haddad et al., 1990; Osborn et al., 2008). In several clinical studies examining 249 

patients with a wide variety of diseases and injuries, pGSN has been shown to be decreased in diseased 250 

patients compared to healthy individuals (Abers et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2011; P. S. Lee et al., 2008; Lind 251 

SE, Smith DB, Janmey PA, 1988; Overmyer et al., 2021). The extent of the decrease can exist on a spectrum, 252 

where critical disease severity correlates with even further reduction in pGSN than mild disease 253 

(Overmyer et al., 2021). Here we show that mice infected with L. pneumophila have decreased pGSN 254 

concentrations in the serum, corresponding with an increase in the lungs, highlighting its potential as a 255 

biomarker for Legionnaires’ disease severity. It remains to be determined if individuals with low pGSN 256 

concentrations at baseline are more susceptible to increased disease severity, as pGSN concentration can 257

A clinical trial investigating the efficacy of recombinant gelsolin administration in treating severe COVID-264 

19 patients found mixed results: gelsolin reduced the frequency of adverse events but did not decrease 265 

the length of hospital stay (Dinubile et al., 2022). Varying results of such clinical trials may be due to 266 

delivery, dose, timeline of treatment, and genetic variability of the subjects. Additionally, a report of rGSN 267 

administration in a severe COVID-19 patient suggests that rGSN administration may improve the disease 268 

severity and outcome of these patients (Catteeuw & DiNubile, 2021). Although our study corroborates 269 

such anecdotal findings with systemic gelsolin delivery, better targeting rGSN to the lungs, such as through 270 

aerosolization, may allow gelsolin to be more concentrated in the lungs. This might overcome issues with 271 

dosing and dilution in the body, as well as decreasing the time for the protein to reach the lungs, and 272 

potential off target effects. However, the systemic effects of rGSN may be decreased due to the localized 273 

delivery to the lungs compared to intravenous delivery. We also show direct treatment of macrophages 274 

with rGSN results in a decreased inflammatory cytokine signature in the supernatant of L. pneumophila -275 

infected macrophages, further cementing the potential of rGSN as a potential treatment for reducing 276 

harmful inflammation during infection. 277

al., 2022). Gelsolin binds to voltage dependent anion channel, a mitochondrial pore protein important for 302 

regulating mitochondrial membrane permeability, signal transduction, and cell death (García-Bartolomé 303

the L. pneumophila infected lung (Tateda, Moore, Deng, et al., 2001; Tateda, Moore, Newstead, et al., 309 

2001). Upon stimulation, neutrophils can engulf and kill L. pneumophila by generating a ROS burst or eject 310 

NETs to trap and kill extracellular bacteria (Koutantou et al., 2025; Price et al., 2021; Ziltener et al., 2016). 311 

Although neutrophils are important for pathogen defense, they can cause pathogenic inflammation if they 312 

are not properly regulated or resolved after infection (Mayadas et al., 2014). Our data suggests that 313 

gelsolin deficiency causes neutrophils to accumulate in the lungs during L. pneumophila infection, which 314 

correlates with worsened lung pathology. Interestingly, when exposed to L. pneumophila ex vivo , gelsolin 315 

deficient neutrophils struggle to produce NETs. This may suggest a failure of neutrophils to react to the 316 

pathogen, in agreement with our seahorse data, that causes pathogenic inflammation to persist instead 317 

of resolve. 318 

319 

Identifying substrates and pathways downstream of non-canonical inflammasome activation will help 320 

elucidate its functions and give insight as to how to modulate it to treat diseases. We show that caspase-321 

4 can cleave gelsolin, creating a unique cleavage pattern compared to that of caspase-3 cleaved gelsolin, 322 

yet similar to the cleavage products produced by caspase-1. As caspase-3 is an apoptotic executioner 323 

caspase, its gelsolin products work to promote apoptosis by collapsing the cytoskeleton and releasing DNA 324 

(Geng et al., 1998; Kothakota et al., 1997a). Due to the difference in gelsolin cleavage signatures between 325 

caspase-4 and caspase-3, and the ability of caspase-4 to cleave caspase-3 generated gelsolin products, it 326 

is possible that caspase-4 can oppose caspase-3 induced apoptosis by creating gelsolin derivatives that 327 

are nonfunctional or not apoptotic. Interestingly, caspase-1 cleaved gelsolin produces similar bands to 328
