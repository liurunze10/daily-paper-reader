# Abstract 12 Inflammatory activation and lipid remodeling are linked features of microglial states, but how inflammatory 13 transcription factors shape microglial lipid handling is unclear. Here we show that STAT1 sets neutral-lipid 14 content in microglia through a route not predicted by lipid-handling transcription. Acute STAT1 depletion in 15 primary microglia lowered neutral-lipid content while lipid-uptake and lipid-storage programs were induced, and 16 interferon-γ activation moved inflammatory transcription in the opposite direction yet lowered lipid content alike. 17 Single-cell transcriptomic and chromatin profiling of Stat1- and Irf1-deficient mice showed that STAT1 and 18 IRF1 organize overlapping inflammatory and lipid-handling programs, with genome-wide accessibility changes 19 that did not predict transcriptional output at individual lipid-handling loci. Microglia co-expressing STAT1 and 20 APOE recurred across Alzheimer's disease and multiple sclerosis datasets. Transcriptional program 21 engagement is therefore separable from cellular lipid state, and lipid-handling gene expression cannot be read 22 as a proxy for microglial lipid content. 23 .CC-BY 4.0 International license available under a (which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. # Introduction 24 Microglia are the resident immune cells of the central nervous system, maintaining tissue homeostasis and 25 coordinating responses to injury, infection, and neurodegeneration 1,2 . Under inflammatory or degenerative 26 stress they transition into distinct cellular states through coordinated gene reprogramming 3,4 , and these 27 transitions are accompanied by metabolic remodeling, including changes in lipid uptake, storage, and 28 utilization 5–7 . Lipid droplets, which are intracellular organelles that store neutral lipids such as triglycerides and 29 cholesteryl esters, accumulate in macrophages and microglia during infection, aging, and neurodegenerative 30 disease, and their accumulation coincides with altered lipid metabolism, phagocytosis, and inflammatory output 31 8–11 . Lipid handling is therefore a regulated component of activated microglial states rather than a passive 32 consequence of lipid excess 12,13 .33 Single-cell transcriptomic studies have identified multiple microglial states enriched for lipid-handling 34 programs 1,14 , which commonly express Apoe , Lpl , and other genes involved in lipid uptake, trafficking, 35 processing, and turnover 15,16 . Inflammatory signaling and lipid handling are directly coupled in at least some of 36 these states. In the median eminence, an Apoe -positive microglial population engages lipid-handling, 37 interferon, and stress-response programs, Western diet increases both interferon signaling and lipid 38 accumulation in these cells, and microglial Apoe deletion attenuates the coupling 17 . The transcriptional 39 regulators that coordinate inflammatory and lipid-handling programs in adult microglia nonetheless remain 40 largely undefined. 41 STAT1 is a central regulator of cytokine and innate immune signaling and a best known transcription factor for 42 activating interferon-stimulated genes that drive antimicrobial and inflammatory responses 18–21 . Its activity 43 extends beyond canonical interferon-responsive transcription: STAT1 can reshape broader cellular programs 44 and influence chromatin accessibility, positioning it to establish the regulatory context in which inflammatory 45 responses occur 22,23 Because inflammatory signaling and lipid handling are tightly coordinated in myeloid cells, 46 STAT1 is well placed to connect inflammatory state with lipid-handling transcription 3,24 . Interferon regulatory 47 factor 1 (IRF1) is induced downstream of STAT1 and mediates a substantial component of interferon-48 responsive transcription 18,25,26 , making the canonical STAT1-IRF1 hierarchy a natural framework for asking 49 whether its output extends to lipid-handling programs. Other IRF-family members already show that interferon-50 associated factors can act on microglial identity and lipid biology: IRF8 configures postnatal microglial 51 enhancer landscapes and transcriptional programs 27 , and IRF7 regulates microglial lipid-droplet accumulation 52 after ischemic stroke through impaired lipophagy 28 . Whether STAT1 and IRF1 shape lipid-handling programs in 53 adult microglia under basal in vivo conditions has not been systematically examined. 54 Here we show that STAT1 and IRF1 converge on inflammatory and lipid-handling programs in adult microglia, 55 and that engaging these programs does not specify neutral-lipid content in a fixed direction. Combining single-56 cell transcriptomic and chromatin-accessibility profiling of Stat1 - and Irf1 -deficient mice with reanalysis of 57 human and mouse disease datasets and acute STAT1 depletion in primary mouse microglia and human 58 microglial-like cells, we find that chromatin accessibility, lipid-handling transcription, and neutral-lipid content 59 can move independently of one another. 60 .CC-BY 4.0 International license available under a (which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. # STAT1 loss reshapes adult microglial states and reorganizes lipid-handling 62 

# programs 63 To determine whether STAT1 shapes microglial transcriptional states under basal conditions, we performed 64 single-cell RNA sequencing of cerebral cortical cells from 8-week-old wild-type (WT) and Stat1 -deficient (Stat1 -65 /-) mice (Fig. 1A). The dataset resolved the major neural, glial, vascular, and immune populations of the adult 66 brain, each identified by canonical lineage markers (Fig. S1A, B). Stat1 deficiency altered transcription in 67 astrocytes, endothelial cells, and neurons, with distinct responses in each cell type (Fig. S1C-K; Tables S1-3). 68 In microglia, unsupervised clustering resolved three states with homeostatic, disease-associated microglia 69 (DAM)-like, and interferon-responsive features (Fig. 1B, C). Homeostatic microglia expressed P2ry12 ,70 

Tmem119 , Hexb , and Olfml3 ; the DAM-like population expressed Trem2 , Apoe , Cd9 , and Cst7 ; and a smaller 71 interferon-responsive population expressed Ifit3 , Oasl2 , Isg15 , and Bst2 (Fig. 1C). Stat1 deficiency reduced 72 both the interferon-responsive and DAM-like fractions, with a corresponding increase in the homeostatic 73 fraction (Fig. 1B, D). STAT1 therefore contributes to the organization of adult microglial states under basal 74 conditions. 75 Differential-expression analysis of the microglial compartment identified 211 decreased and 505 increased 76 genes in Stat1 -deficient microglia (Fig. 1E; Table S4). Decreased genes were enriched for type II interferon 77 responses, amyloid-beta formation, glutathione metabolism, and leukocyte activation (Fig. 1F). Increased 78 genes were enriched for small GTPase signaling, cell-junction organization, glycoprotein biosynthesis, and 79 myeloid differentiation (Fig. 1G). Stat1 deficiency therefore reconfigures the adult microglial transcriptome 80 beyond canonical interferon-responsive genes. 81 Many of these genes act in lipid handling. Stat1 -deficient microglia showed reduced expression of Apoe , Lpl ,82 

Lipa , Plin2 , Plin3 , Nceh1 , and Sorl1 , which function in lipoprotein trafficking, lysosomal lipid processing, lipid-83 droplet organization, and cholesterol handling (Fig. 1H). Other lipid-handling genes, including Scarb1 , Pltp , and 84 

Abcb4 , increased (Fig. 1H). Genes involved in lipid biosynthesis and lipid-responsive regulation likewise 85 changed in opposing directions (Fig. 1I). 86 These changes did not arise solely from the smaller interferon-responsive fraction. State-resolved analysis 87 confirmed the expected decrease in Ifit3 , Oasl2 , Isg15 , Bst2 , and Irf7 , but lipid-handling genes also changed 88 within the homeostatic and DAM-like compartments, with magnitude and direction varying by gene and state 89 (Fig. S2A). Stat1 deficiency therefore alters transcription within established microglial states as well as their 90 relative representation. Stat1 deficiency also altered microglial genes at Alzheimer's disease-associated loci: 91 

Apoe , Sorl1 , and Trem2 decreased, whereas Inpp5d increased (Fig. 1J-M, S2B). 92 Transcriptome-derived metabolic-task analysis predicted changes across lipid, amino-acid, carbohydrate, 93 energy, and vitamin and cofactor metabolism (Fig. S2C-F). The largest lipid-associated effects were reduced 94 phospholipid, sphingolipid, ceramide, and cardiolipin synthesis, alongside changes in glutathione and redox 95 processes. These predictions reflect transcriptional potential and do not establish metabolite abundance or 96 biochemical flux. 97 Together, these data show that Stat1 deficiency reshapes adult microglial state organization and reorganizes 98 lipid-handling transcription under basal in vivo conditions. Loss of either factor reduced the interferon-responsive and 111 DAM-like fractions, with a corresponding increase in the homeostatic fraction (Fig. 2C). The two differed in 112 degree: the interferon-responsive state was absent in Stat1 -deficient microglia but reduced and retained in Irf1 -113 deficient microglia (Fig. 2C). Convergence was not uniform: Apoe decreased after loss of either factor, but more strongly in Stat1 -119 deficient microglia (Fig. 2F). 120 Direct comparison of Irf1 -deficient and Stat1 -deficient microglia identified 130 genes expressed at lower and 121 131 at higher levels in Irf1 -deficient cells (Fig. 2G; Table S9). Genes lower in Irf1 -deficient microglia were 125 enriched for cytoplasmic translation, signal transduction, rRNA processing, and ribonucleoprotein-complex 126 biogenesis. STAT1 and IRF1 therefore produce strongly overlapping but nonidentical transcriptional outputs in 127 adult microglia. 128 We next asked whether this convergence extended to chromatin accessibility. Several genes 137 with reduced RNA expression, including lipid-handling genes, showed increased promoter or distal 138 accessibility in Stat1 -deficient or Irf1 -deficient microglia, so accessibility did not predict the direction of 139 transcriptional change. Stat1 -targeting siRNAs, siRNA #1 and siRNA #3 reduced Stat1 expression most effectively and siRNA #2 less 200 so (Fig. 4B); we carried siRNA #1 and #3 forward. 201 

Stat1 depletion did not affect baseline viability or microglial morphology (Fig. S7A, B), and LPS induced 202 marked morphological activation in both control and depleted cultures, confirming that the cells remained 203 responsive (Fig. S7A). Both Stat1 -targeting siRNAs reduced neutral-lipid LipidTOX fluorescence relative to 204 control siRNA (Fig. 4C, D; S7C). The same direction of change was obtained with BODIPY (data not shown). 205 Acute STAT1 depletion therefore lowers neutral-lipid content in primary microglia without overt cytotoxicity. 206 Bulk RNA sequencing confirmed disruption of the STAT1-dependent program. Psmb8 , Rtp4 , and Zbp1 , although a small number of genes, including Ifi204 and Ccnd1 , increased (Fig. 4E; 209 S7D; Table S11). Lipid-process sets moved differently: 215 lipid-droplet storage and lipid uptake or processing were positively enriched, lipolysis and fatty-acid oxidation 216 trended positive, and lysosome or lipophagy sets were modestly negative. Oxidative phosphorylation 217 decreased with both siRNAs, whereas glycolysis and iron, redox, and glutathione sets responded in opposite 218 directions between the two reagents and were not interpreted further. Lipid-storage and lipid-uptake 219 transcription therefore rose while neutral-lipid content fell. 220 Gene-level responses matched this split (Fig. S7D). Gene-level effects correlated positively between acute depletion and constitutive Stat1 loss, more 228 strongly for siRNA #3 than for siRNA #1 (Fig. S8G, H). Genes reduced or increased in Stat1 -deficient adult 232 microglia shifted in the same direction after acute knockdown, and the in vivo IFN/STAT1 module showed the 233 strongest directional concordance. Cpt1a , and the coordinated PPAR γ and adipogenic-lipid program remained inducible. # Discussion 317 We identify STAT1 and IRF1 as overlapping regulators of the transcriptional programs that organize adult 318 microglial states and lipid handling, and we find that engagement of those programs does not specify neutral-319 lipid content in a fixed direction. Loss of either factor in vivo reshaped microglial states and reorganized lipid-320 handling transcription, with concordant genome-wide accessibility changes that nonetheless failed to predict 321 transcriptional output at individual lipid-handling loci. In primary microglia, acute STAT1 depletion lowered 322 neutral-lipid content while lipid-storage and lipid-uptake programs were positively enriched, and IFN γ activation 323 lowered it in the same direction while moving inflammatory transcription the opposite way. HMC3 cells showed 324 the reverse lipid phenotype. Together these results place STAT1 and IRF1 within a context-dependent 325 framework linking inflammatory state, lipid-handling transcription, and neutral-lipid content. 326 Lipid droplets are dynamic organelles that integrate neutral-lipid storage with lipid mobilization, membrane 327 homeostasis, and cellular stress responses 32,33 . In inflammatory myeloid cells, droplet formation and turnover 328 are coupled to inflammatory signaling, and perturbing triglyceride storage or mobilization alters immune 329 output 10,34,35 . Lipid-droplet-rich microglial states arise during aging and neurodegenerative disease, although 330 their functional consequences vary across cellular and pathological contexts 8,9,11 . Neutral-lipid abundance is 331 therefore a regulated cellular property rather than a passive consequence of lipid excess. 332 

In vivo , Stat1 deficiency altered the representation of homeostatic, DAM-like, and interferon-responsive states 333 and moved lipid-handling genes in both directions, reducing a group involved in lipoprotein trafficking, 334 lysosomal lipid processing, cholesterol handling, and lipid-droplet organization while increasing others. STAT1 335 therefore does not uniformly activate or suppress lipid-handling transcription; it reorganizes distinct 336 components of the program. These changes were not attributable solely to the reduced interferon-responsive 337 fraction, since lipid-handling genes also changed within the homeostatic and DAM-like compartments. Lipid-338 handling transcription is thus integrated with microglial state organization rather than controlled as a single 339 uniform metabolic module. 340 Canonical models position STAT1 upstream of IRF1 in an interferon-responsive transcriptional cascade 36,37 .341 Our results are compatible with that hierarchy and extend its functional output to microglial lipid-handling 342 transcription: loss of either factor reduced interferon-associated and lipid-handling module expression, and 343 several lipid-handling genes responded similarly in the two genotypes. The outputs were not identical, 344 however. Apoe expression fell after loss of either factor but more strongly after Stat1 loss. Because state 345 representation also differed between genotypes, this aggregate comparison reflects both compositional and 346 within-state effects, and it does not establish direct regulation of the Apoe locus or a STAT1-exclusive 347 mechanism. Individual genes may depend differently on STAT1, IRF1, other IRF-family factors, cooperating 348 transcription factors, cofactor availability, and the underlying microglial state. 349 Chromatin accessibility adds a further regulatory dimension. Accessibility changes were concordant genome-350 wide between the two knockouts, yet individual lipid-handling loci carried different combinations of shared and 351 factor-biased candidate peaks, and several genes with reduced transcript abundance showed increased 352 promoter or distal accessibility. Accessibility alone therefore did not explain the observed transcriptional 353 responses, consistent with contributions from transcription-factor occupancy, cofactor recruitment, enhancer-354 promoter communication, polymerase engagement, and three-dimensional chromatin organization. The 355 candidate regions identified here provide a basis for subsequent direct-binding and locus-specific perturbation 356 studies but do not themselves establish direct STAT1 or IRF1 regulation. 357
