Abstract 6

Background: Microglia drive neuroinflammation in Alzheimer's disease (AD), yet no 7

approved therapy targets this compartment. Human genome -wide association studies 8

consistently implicate innate immune loci in AD risk, establishing microglial 9

transcriptional programs as therapeutically relevant but pharmacologically 10 

underexploited targets. 11 

Objective: We sought to identify transcription factors (TFs) governing microglial state 12 

transitions computationally and to nominate structurally tractable drug repurposing 13 

candidates. 14 

Methods: We applied trajectory inference (PAGA), pseudobulk DESeq2, pySCENIC 15 

gene regulatory network (GRN) inference, CellChat, and virtual screening of 1,962 16 

approved compounds to 236,002 microglial nuclei from 84 donors (SEA -AD atlas). 17 

Results: IKZF1 was the sole target TF retained under cisTarget v10 motif constraints, 18 

with peak regulon activity in LateAD -DAM (pseudotime ρ = +0.309) and replication in an 19 

independent bulk cohort (GSE95587; adjusted P value = .004). CellChat identified 20 

SLIT2→ROBO2 from multiple neuron subtypes (predominantly inhibitory interneurons) 21

Introduction 31 

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is the leading cause of dementia, affecting tens of millions of 32 

people worldwide with prevalence expected to increase substantially as global 33 

populations age. Despite approval of amyloid -targeting immunotherapies, disease -34 

modifying options remain limited and do not engage the neuroinflammatory component 35 

of disease [1] . Human genome -wide association studies have consistently implicated 36 

innate immune loci — including TREM2 , CLU , BIN1 , and INPP5D — in AD risk [2,3] ,37 

establishing the microglial compartment as a therapeutically relevant but 38 

pharmacologically underexploited target. 39 

Microglia, the brain’s resident immune sentinels, undergo complex state transitions in 40 

Alzheimer’s disease. They shift from homeostatic surveillance to a spectrum of disease -41 

associated states, including interferon -responsive (IRM), disease -associated (DAM), 42 

lipid -associated (LAM), and terminal late -disease phenotypes [2,4] . Identifying the 43

and potentially reversing — pathological microglial activation. 45 

Due to significant species -specific divergence in microglial signatures [3] , we focus 46 

exclusively on human -derived single -nucleus data. Master regulators like SPI1 /PU.1 ,47 

IRF8 , and PPARG have been implicated in various microglial states [3,5] , but prioritizing 48 

these candidates for pharmacological intervention remains a significant challenge. 49 

These TFs possess structurally defined DNA -binding domains that are in principle 50 

targetable by small molecules [6,7] .51 

Gene regulatory network (GRN) inference offers a principled route to TF prioritisation by 52 

identifying TFs that are causally active rather than merely differentially expressed [8] .53 

Combined with structural druggability assessment and virtual screening, GRN analysis 54 

can narrow the hypothesis space from hundreds of candidate regulators to a handful of 55 

mechanistically credible, structurally tractable targets [9] .56 

Here we apply the DAM -DRUG framework to the SEA -AD multi -regional atlas [10] , the 57 

largest publicly available single -nucleus microglial dataset. The novelty of this work lies 58 

in the multi -modality convergence logic that links established tools —pySCENIC, 59 

CellChat, AutoDock Vina, and CellOracle —into a ranked evidence hierarchy. This 60 

framework requires a candidate TF to be supported across trajectory, regulon, 61 

chromatin, bulk replication, and perturbation evidence before advancing to structural 62 

screening. 63

CellOracle in -silico TF perturbation 137 

In -silico transcription factor (TF) knockouts (KO) were performed using CellOracle v0.12 138 

[25] ; on a 20,000 -cell stratified subsample of the microglial atlas. The base gene 139 

regulatory network (GRN) was constructed from the 5 -seed pySCENIC consensus 140 

adjacency, restricted to the top 500 targets per TF based on importance scores. 141 

Simulated KO was executed by setting the expression of the target TF to zero, with 142 

signal propagation depth set to n propagation =3. 143 

The magnitude of the resulting state transition was quantified as the magnitude of the 144 

mean displacement vector per substate in the UMAP embedding (mean |ΔUMAP|). It is 145 

important to note that because UMAP is a non -linear manifold learning technique that 146 

prioritizes local connectivity over global distance preservation [26] , distances in this 147 

embedding space are often distorted and do not faithfully represent biological distances 148 

[27] . Consequently, these perturbation metrics are utilized exclusively for the ordinal 149 

ranking of TF candidates within specific substates rather than as linear quantitative 150 

measurements of biological effect size. 151

Gene regulatory network inference identifies IKZF1 as the primary disease regulon 239 

pySCENIC (5 -seed GRNBoost2 consensus; 1.46 M co -expression edges; RcisTarget 240 

against cisTarget v10/HOCOMOCO v11; Supplementary Methods SM1 ) identified 46 241 

regulons above the 80% gene -mapping threshold. Of the nine target TFs, only IKZF1 242 

was retained; the remaining eight failed motif -database coverage —a bottleneck of 243 

cisTarget v10 annotation, not GRNBoost2 stochasticity (confirmed across 5 seeds; 244 

Supplementary Methods SM2 ; Supplementary Table S 7). IKZF1 prioritisation is 245 

therefore conditional on this database and does not imply other TFs lack regulatory 246 

roles. A motif -agnostic pseudobulk co -expression rescue analysis for BHLHE40 /41 247 

found no enrichment of DAM/LAM markers in either TF’s top co -expressed genes 248 

(Fisher’s exact OR = 0.00, P value = 1.0), consistent with these TFs acting within cell -249 

state transitions rather than as donor -level axes ( Supplementary Figs . S2, S 3). The 250 

IKZF1 (+) regulon peaked in LateAD -DAM (AUC = 0.153) and showed the only positive 251 

pseudotime correlation among the 46 retained regulons (Spearman ρ = +0.309; anti -252 

conservative CI due to within -donor clustering; Figure 2; Supplementary Fi g. S4). 253

Discussion 361 

The central finding —that IKZF1 is a computationally prioritised stage -specific candidate 362 

in the terminal LateAD -DAM microglial state —extends prior work by Ballasch et al. [30] ,363 

who demonstrated microglial -specific IKZF1 expression in mice, a DAM -like profile in 364 

IKZF1 -deficient microglia, and IKZF1 protein elevation in human AD hippocampus. The 365 

present study adds: (1) stage -specific association within a human single -nucleus 366 

trajectory across 236,002 cells and 84 donors; (2) multi -modality convergence ranking 367 

of IKZF1 relative to eight other candidate TFs; and (3) nomination of the CRBN -ZF2 368 

interface as a structural entry point via virtual screening. The SEA -AD atlas identification 369 

of IKZF1 as an early GRN driver [10] is also prior; our contribution is the stage -resolved 370 

trajectory context and multi -modality framework applied here. 371 

IKZF1 encodes Ikaros, a zinc -finger transcription factor best characterised as a master 372 

regulator of lymphoid development in haematopoiesis [40,41] , where it serves as a 373 

critical tumor suppressor in B -cell malignancies [42] . Its expression in tissue -resident 374 

macrophages and microglia is less studied, but emerging evidence positions IKZF1 as a 375 

regulator of innate immune quiescence: IKZF1 -deficient microglia adopt a DAM -like 376 

transcriptional profile with increased TREM2 and reduced P2RY12 [30] , the trajectory -377 

resolved transition from Homeostatic to DAM states observed in our human single -378 

nucleus analysis . Furthermore, the protein -level elevation of IKZF1 in late -stage 5xFAD 379 

mice and human AD hippocampi reported by Ballasch et al. provides biological 380 

validation for our finding that IKZF1 regulon activity peaks in the terminal LateAD -DAM 381 

state [30] . The multi -modality convergence framework applied in this study — requiring 382 

support across GRN regulon activity, pseudotime trajectory, chromatin accessibility, 383

SPR or TSA with recombinant IRF8 DBD is the required experimental next step; no 394 

translational inference is warranted without experimental triage. 395 

Separately, the cereblon (CRBN) molecular glue track identified in our virtual screen is 396 

noteworthy: approved CELMoDs (cereblon -E3 ligase modulators) including 397 

lenalidomide and pomalidomide already achieve IKZF1 protein degradation in 398 

haematopoietic malignancies. Whether this drug class could selectively modulate IKZF1 399 

activity in microglia without systemic immunosuppression is an open question, 400 

particularly given the immunocompromised state already associated with advanced AD. 401 

The six CRBN -track hits identified here — celecoxib, teriflunomide, amlexanox ,402 

capmatinib, lasmiditan, and risdiplam — are structurally distinct from classical 403 

CELMoDs and require CRBN -displacement or TR -FRET assays to confirm cereblon 404 

engagement before further characterisation. 405

The SLIT2→ROBO2 signal from CellChat adds an intercellular dimension absent from 406 

most computational target -identification studies. It is an MTG -specific prediction that 407 

requires cross -dataset and experimental validation (e.g., co -culture or optogenetic 408 

perturbation) before any novelty claim; SLIT -ROBO signalling has been shown to 409 

regulate microglial process extension [44] , making the prediction biologically plausible 410 

but unconfirmed. The SLIT -ROBO axis is best characterised in neuronal axon guidance 411 

during development; ROBO2 expression on microglia may mediate process extension 412 

and chemotactic responses toward sites of neuronal injury. In AD cortex, the 413 

predominant SLIT2 -expressing populations identified here — primarily inhibitory 414 

interneuron subtypes (Pvalb, Lamp5 Lhx6, Chandelier, Sst, Lamp5) with minor 415 

excitatory neuron contribution (ExcNeuron ) — include cell types that are selectively 416 

vulnerable and progressively depleted with disease stage [10,45] . While functional 417 

evidence for SLIT -ROBO signaling in microglial process extension is currently limited to 418 

murine models [44] , pathway -level analyses of human AD transcriptomes consistently 419 

nominate 'axon guidance' as a critical neuro -immune axis [46] . Furthermore, the 420 

reported downregulation of guidance cues during disease progression [47] mirrors the 421 

depletion of SLIT2 -expressing interneurons observed here, suggesting that a loss of 422 

inhibitory guidance signaling may contribute to microglial dysfunction in the LateAD -423 

DAM state. This loss, particularly within somatostatin -positive subpopulations, has been 424 

validated across independent cohorts using both single -nucleus transcriptomics and 425 

quantitative histology [48,49] . Declining SLIT2 from dying interneurons could plausibly 426 

alter microglial process dynamics or phagocytic capacity, representing a mechanism by 427 

which neuronal loss propagates microglial dysfunction; this remains speculative. 428

Limitations 429 

All virtual screening hits, CellChat predictions, and CellOracle perturbation results are 430 

exploratory computational predictions. Internal analyses 1 –5 for IKZF1 are non -431 

independent (same SEA -AD RNA data; not pre -registered); analysis 6 (GSE95587) is 432 

the sole external replication, in a single cohort and brain region. The absence of region -433 

stratified pseudobulk modelling means some transcriptional differences may reflect 434 

regional composition rather than disease -state transitions. GRN inference is limited by 435 

motif database coverage: BHLHE40 /41 could not be evaluated because HOCOMOCO 436 

v11 lacks their atypical E -box motifs, and the IKZF1 finding is therefore conditional on 437 

the cisTarget v10/HOCOMOCO v11 combination. The SLIT2 →ROBO2 pathway derives 438 

from a single brain region (MTG) and has not been replicated across the other nine 439 

SEA -AD regions. Virtual screening with implicit -solvent MM -GBSA provides relative 440 

compound rankings, not binding affinities; ligand egress in MD simulations for both top 441 

compounds underscores the necessity of biophysical triage. The SEA -AD cohort 442 

includes sex and age as covariates in differential expression models, but ancestry 443 

composition was not formally characterised; generalisability of the IKZF1 trajectory 444 

association across ethnically diverse populations remains to be established. Sex was 445 

controlled as a covariate throughout but was not analysed as a primary biological 446 

variable; sex -stratified substate composition, sex -specific 447 

regulon dynamics, and potential sex -by -disease interactions in microglial state 448 

transitions were not examined. Given emerging evidence for sex -dimorphic microglial 449 

responses in AD, this represents a limitation and a priority for future stratified analyses. 450

The DAM -DRUG pipeline is transferable to any disease -relevant cell type with atlas -452

decision logic (cell -state trajectory → TF candidate set → GRN inference → multi -454

communication context) and advancement criteria (≥4/6 evidence dimensions) are 456 

documented with substitution guidance in the GitHub repository README. 457 

Immediate experimental priorities for validating the computational findings reported here 458 

include: (a) surface plasmon resonance (SPR) or thermal shift assay (TSA) to confirm 459 

tafamidis binding to recombinant IRF8 DBD; (b) CRISPR -mediated knockout of IKZF1 in 460 

iPSC -derived or primary human microglia followed by transcriptomic profiling to 461 

determine whether IKZF1 loss reproduces or accelerates the LateAD -DAM signature; (c) 462 

cross -dataset replication of the SLIT2→ROBO2 pathway in an independent snRNA -seq 463 

dataset with paired interneuron and microglia populations; and (d) region -stratified 464 

pseudobulk modelling across the ten SEA -AD brain regions to determine whether the 465 

IKZF1 trajectory association is cortex -wide or region -specific. 466 

Conclusion 467 

Applying a six -step multi -modality convergence framework to 236,002 microglial nuclei 468 

from 84 postmortem AD donors, we nominate IKZF1 as the primary computationally 469 

prioritised transcription factor in the terminal LateAD -DAM state. This nomination rests 470 

on concordant evidence across pseudobulk differential expression, GRN regulon activity, 471 

diffusion pseudotime trajectory, chromatin accessibility motif enrichment, independent 472 

bulk RNA -seq replication (GSE95587), and in -silico perturbation — with external 473 

replication in a single independent cohort representing the sole non -SEA -AD evidence 474
