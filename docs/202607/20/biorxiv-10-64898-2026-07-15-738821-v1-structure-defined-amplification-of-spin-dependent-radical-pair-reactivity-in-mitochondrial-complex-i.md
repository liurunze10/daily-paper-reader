## 摘要
线粒体复合物 I 是活性氧（ROS）的主要来源，但自由基对自旋动力学是否参与调节 ROS 相关反应仍不清楚。在这里，我们将冷冻电子显微镜结构引导的氧采样与自由基对量子动力学相结合，以确定黄素单核苷酸（FMN）周围的分子结构如何塑造模拟的自旋相关自由基对反应产率。蒙特卡洛采样揭示了广泛的空间可及氧构型系综，而自旋敏感性则集中在距离 FMN 参考中心约 3.3-3.4 Å 的受限近接触区域。这种定位是由结构可及性与磁场和自旋去相位敏感性的整合所定义的，并且在空间上与有利于单重态-三重态相互转换的交换-超精细交叉区域重叠。结构波动的模拟进一步表明，与热点区域外相比，热点区域内的等效扰动在单重态反应产率中产生了更大的变异性，从而确定了一个增强结构响应性的局部区域。这些结果表明，FMN 结合口袋可能充当一个结构依赖性的放大层，将自由基对几何结构的微小变化转化为异质的自旋相关反应结果。我们的研究结果建立了一个将实验解析的蛋白质结构与自由基对自旋动力学联系起来的框架，并确定了可能塑造线粒体复合物 I 中自旋相关 ROS 化学过程的结构约束。

## Abstract
Mitochondrial complex I is a major source of reactive oxygen species (ROS), but whether radical pair spin dynamics contribute to the regulation of ROS-associated reactions remains unknown. Here we integrate cryo-electron microscopy structure-guided oxygen sampling with radical pair quantum dynamics to determine how the molecular architecture surrounding flavin mononucleotide (FMN) shapes modelled spin-dependent radical-pair reaction yields. Monte Carlo sampling revealed a broad ensemble of sterically accessible oxygen configurations, whereas spin sensitivity was concentrated within a restricted near-contact region centred at approximately 3.3-3.4 [A] from the FMN reference centre. This localisation was defined by the integration of structural accessibility with magnetic field and spin dephasing sensitivities and spatially overlapped with an exchange-hyperfine crossover regime favourable for singlet-triplet interconversion. Simulations of structural fluctuations further show that equivalent perturbations generated greater variability in singlet reaction yields within a hotspot than outside it, identifying a localized regime of enhanced structural responsiveness. These results suggest that the FMN binding pocket may act as a structure-dependent amplification layer that converts small changes in radical pair geometry into heterogeneous spin-dependent reaction outcomes. Our findings establish a framework linking experimentally resolved protein architecture to radical pair spin dynamics and identify structural constraints that may shape spin-dependent ROS chemistry in mitochondrial complex I.
