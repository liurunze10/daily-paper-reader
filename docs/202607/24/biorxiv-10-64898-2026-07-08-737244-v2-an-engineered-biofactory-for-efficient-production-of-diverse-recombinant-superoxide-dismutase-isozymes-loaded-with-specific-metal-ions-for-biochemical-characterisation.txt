Abstract 19 

Background: Biochemical, biophysical and structural characterisation of isozymes from the 20 

ubiquitous family of iron- or manganese-dependent superoxide dismutases (SodFMs) requires 21 

the purification of high-quality preparations of recombinant enzymes. Determination of their key 22 

biochemical parameter, their catalytic metal-preference, requires the comparison of the catalytic 23 

turnover of samples loaded exclusively with iron versus samples loaded exclusively with 24 

manganese. Both of these aims are inhibited by the potential contamination of recombinant 25 

preparations of SodFMs, prepared by heterologous overexpression inside Escherichia coli cells, 26 

by even low levels of endogenous SodFMs from the host, both of which show very high turnover 27 

with either manganese ( E. coli MnSOD) or iron (FeSOD). To overcome this problem, we created 28 

a strain of E. coli lacking the endogenous SodFMs. Here, we characterised this E. coli BL21 (DE3) 29 

DsodA DsodB strain, determining the physiological effects of SodFM deletion and demonstrating 30 

its utility for producing recombinant SodFMs for in vitro characterisation and use. 31 

Results: Genomic analysis verified the targeted gene deletions, without off-target effects. Growth, 32 

expression, elemental analysis, and proteomic data confirmed a lack of physiological defects of 33 

the strain except for a known inability to grow on glucose, which is overcome by heterologous 34 

SodFM expression. We demonstrate the utility of the strain for the efficient production of diverse 35 

recombinant SodFMs, including highly divergent, understudied isozymes, including the ability to 36 

precisely control the metal-loading of the heterologously expressed protein. 37 

Conclusions: The E. coli strain described herein is a useful microbial cell factory for production 38 

of recombinant SodFMs, which should find widespread utility as expression host of choice, 39 

enabling more efficient production of protein for studies of the biochemical, biophysical and 40 

structural properties of this remarkable family of metalloenzymes. 41

Background 42 

Since the first expression of a recombinant protein inside cells of Escherichia coli was reported in 43 

1977 [1] and the first recombinant therapeutic, human insulin, in 1979 [2], demand for recombinant 44 

protein production for academic study and for commercial, medical or industrial applications has 45 

grown dramatically. Although alternatives are available, E. coli remains the cell factory of choice 46 

for producing recombinant proteins [3,4]. It offers several advantages for recombinant protein 47 

synthesis. It has been extensively studied, with well-characterised physiology and genetics and 48 

easy-to-use tools available [5–10]. These include inducible expression vectors [6,7,11] and the 49 

host strain, BL21 (DE3). This strain lacks Lon and OmpT proteases, limiting degradation of foreign 50 

protein, and incorporates RNA polymerase from T7 bacteriophage in its genome [12], enabling it 51 

to express transcripts from the strong T7 promoter [5]. 52 

Derivatives of E. coli BL21 (DE3) have been constructed to improve its production of 53 

proteins for specific applications. Strains were created that are optimised for production of 54 

proteins containing a poly-histidine tag to facilitate their purification. These strains have mutations 55 

in five endogenous E. coli genes whose products commonly contaminate protein preparations 56 

after purification by immobilised metal affinity chromatography (IMAC), reducing their affinity for 57 

IMAC or enabling a pre-clearance step using chitin [13]. Other modified BL21 strains eliminate 58 

contamination of recombinant protein preparations by the chaperone DnaK [14], improve 59 

expression of outer membrane proteins [15], facilitate site-specific incorporation of non-natural or 60 

isotopically labelled amino acids [16,17], produce glycosylated eukaryotic proteins [18], or 61 

improve production of specific metabolites such as N -acetyl -D-neuraminic acid (Neu5Ac) [19], 62 

lycopene [8], or phenylpyruvic acid [20]. 63 

For the last decade, we have been studying the iron- or manganese-dependent 64 

superoxide dismutase (SOD) family of metalloenzymes (SodFM) [21–27]. SODs are the primary 65 

cellular defence against superoxide radicals, key antioxidant enzymes that maintain redox 66

balance, and in higher eukaryotes have been implicated in signalling, immune function and 67 

disease [28]. The SodFMs are the most common and most widely distributed class of SODs, 68 

present in diverse organisms including some obligate anaerobes [21]. SodFMs exhibit a spectrum 69 

of metal specificities, ranging from highly manganese-preferring to highly iron-preferring enzymes 70 

[21,29,30]. Between these extremes are isozymes that can catalyse their reaction with either 71 

metal cofactor, termed cambialism [21,24,31], a rare biochemical property among redox 72 

metalloenzymes [32]. All SodFMs share identical folds and remarkably similar active site 73 

architectures [21,28,33,34], leading to questions about how different isozymes control their 74 

distinct catalytic metal-preferences [22,23,28,35]. The detailed biochemical, biophysical and 75 

structural characterisation of SodFMs needed to answer such questions requires purified 76 

preparations of recombinant enzymes, free of contamination by the endogenous SodFMs that are 77 

expressed by the host cell. E. coli possesses two SodFMs, strongly manganese-preferring 78 

Ec SodA and iron-preferring Ec SodB [36–38], which are highly active enzymes with their cognate 79 

metal cofactor [21,29,30]. This makes even very low levels of their contamination in recombinant 80 

preparations of heterologous SodFMs highly problematic for quantifying the catalytic turnover of 81 

an isozyme with each metal, especially as highly metal-preferring isozymes can have very low 82 

catalytic efficiency when loaded with the ‘wrong’ metal cofactor [21,23,29,30]. 83 

To overcome this bottleneck, we created a strain of E. coli BL21 (DE3) in which both genes 84 

encoding SodFMs were deleted from the genome [21]. The strain grows normally in aerobic 85 

culture when used for recombinant protein expression [21,39]. The BL21 (DE3) DsodA DsodB 86 

strain enables large-scale preparation of recombinant SodFMs, free of contamination by native 87 

E. coli SodFMs, and with specific metal-loading despite the BL21 parental strain being known to 88 

have defects in metal homeostasis [40]. Using this strain, we developed medium throughput in-89 

gel assays to assess the catalytic metal-preference of SodFM isozymes, which enabled a study 90 

of the evolution of the SodFM family [21]. Here, we have characterised this E. coli mutant strain, 91 

indicated its lack of physiological growth defects under standard culture conditions, and 92

Results 229 

The genome of the BL21-derived host strain shows no off-target mutations 230 

The E. coli BL21 strain is known to be vulnerable to genetic mutations, showing an increased 231 

mutation rate relative to other Type B E. coli [47]. The Lambda Red method for genetic 232 

modification of the E. coli genome has also been suggested to be vulnerable to secondary 233 

mutations due to the use of low and high temperature selection steps [48,49]. Further, deleting 234 

genes encoding superoxide dismutases, which are involved in cellular defence against 235 

exogenous oxidative stress, also makes the host cell vulnerable to secondary mutations [50]. For 236 

these reasons, we began our study of the BL21 DsodA DsodB strain by sequencing its genome to 237 

identify any mutations that were present in addition to the targeted deletions. 238 

We used Illumina sequencing to compare the genome sequences of both the DsodA DsodB 239 

strain and the parental BL21 (DE3) strain from which it was made (herein ‘wild type’, WT) to the 240 

reference genome for E. coli BL21 (DE3) [51]. The WT strain showed no deviations from that of 241

Previous reports showed that a DsodA DsodB strain of Type A E. coli , the K-12 strain MC4100, 257 

exhibited a severe growth defect under certain growth conditions. Specifically, this strain was 258 

unable to grow in media containing glucose as the sole carbon source, but was able to grow on 259 

alternative carbon sources such as amino acids [39]. Subsequent studies have indicated that 260 

superoxide stress strongly affects enzymes containing exposed iron-sulphur clusters, including 261 

enzymes in central carbon metabolism and the biosynthesis of branched-chain amino acids [54– 262 

56]. Therefore, this failure of the E. coli MC4100 DsodA DsodB strain to grow in glucose was 263 

proposed to be a result of a superoxide-induced metabolic block [39,55]. 264

Discussion 431 

We previously constructed a strain of E. coli BL21 for the purpose of creating a microbial cell 432 

factory for the efficient synthesis of recombinant heterologous superoxide dismutase enzymes 433 

free of contamination by its endogenous cytosolic SodFM isozymes. The native E. coli SodFMs 434 

exhibit very high metal-preference of their catalysis, with Ec SodA being active only with 435 

manganese and Ec SodB only with iron, each with very high catalytic turnover [30,65]. Therefore, 436 

even low levels of contamination by the E. coli SodFMs can contribute to the measured SOD 437

activity of recombinant protein preparations, especially when biochemically characterising an 438 

isozyme with low catalytic turnover (for example, when measuring turnover from a manganese-439 

preferring isozyme when wrongly loaded with iron and vice versa ). Additionally, while the 440 

expression of the iron-dependent E. coli SOD, Ec SodB, is largely constitutive, the manganese-441 

dependent E. coli SOD, Ec SodA, is induced under stress conditions [66]. Furthermore, it is 442 

feasible that the endogenous E. coli SODs might also assemble into heterodimeric forms with the 443 

heterologous SodFM, which would result in contamination even after purification using a protein-444 

tagging strategy. Thus, it is unavoidably unclear to what extent each of these isozymes will 445 

contribute through their contamination to a given extract, interfering not only with the 446 

measurement of catalytic activity but also to elemental analysis to determine the metal content of 447 

recombinant protein preparations of heterologously expressed SodFMs. Our E. coli BL21 448 

DsodA DsodB strain was designed to overcome this contamination problem, enabling us to 449 

produce large quantities of the purified recombinant enzymes for detailed biochemical and 450 

biophysical characterisation [21,22]. Use of this strain enables the production of contaminant-free 451 

recombinant SodFMs without the use of protein tags to facilitate the purification process. It also 452 

created a system for screening the activity of SodFMs in a higher throughput manner, avoiding 453 

the need for full purification of target isozymes in each metal form by assaying the recombinant 454 

enzyme direct from crude bacterial lysates, either in solution or in gel activity assays. The latter 455 

provided us with a tool that enabled us to survey a diverse set of 60 SodFM isozymes from across 456 

the entire phylogenetic tree, as well as more than 100 different mutated variants of those 457 

isozymes, to determine the evolutionary history of this widely distributed metalloenzyme family 458 

[21]. 459 

In this study, we sought to characterise this mutant strain of E. coli BL21 to identify any 460 

physiological defects which it might exhibit and to validate it as a new tool for use in the study of 461 

the remarkable SodFM family of metalloenzymes. Based on prior observations made with a 462 

mutated strain of Type A E. coli , strain K-12, we anticipated that we would observe physiological 463

We demonstrated the usefulness of this E. coli BL21 (DE3) DsodA DsodB strain for 474 

production of recombinant SodFM enzymes. First and foremost, the strain can produce large 475 

quantities of heterologous isozymes from across the SodFM phylogenetic tree, assuring that 476 

preparations are free of contamination by Ec SodA and Ec SodB. The resulting samples can be 477 

readily purified to near-homogeneity by liquid chromatography, without need for affinity tags, 478 

resulting in samples that free of contamination by E. coli SODs, which are suited for biochemical 479 

characterisation or structural determination [21–24]. Metal-loading control can be achieved to a 480 

significant extent by culturing the strain under carefully defined metal conditions in M9 minimal 481 

medium. Unfortunately, some mixed metal loading of heterologous proteins are unavoidable in-482 

cell: both of the contaminating metals, Fe and Zn, are needed for growth so cannot be omitted, 483 

and therefore are also present in low-levels in the purified preparations. Nonetheless, the strain 484 

can make protein predominantly loaded with a target metal ion. We have optimised these 485 

conditions specifically for our extensive studies of the SodFM1 isozymes from S. aureus , and 486 

these conditions produce highly selective metal loading with either manganese or iron based on 487 

supplementation of the growth medium with these metals. Importantly, the strain also enables a 488

Conclusions 498 

Here, we have characterised the physiology and utility of an E. coli BL21 DsodA DsodB strain for 499 

production of recombinant SodFM proteins for in vitro studies. Genomic analysis verified the 500 

targeted gene deletions, without significant off-target effects. Growth, expression, elemental 501 

analysis, and proteomic data confirmed a lack of physiological defects of the mutant strain, except 502 

for a known inability to grow on glucose, which we have demonstrated can be overcome by 503 

heterologous expression of a SodFM isozyme. We demonstrate the strain can efficiently produce 504 

diverse recombinant SodFMs, including precise control of metal-loading of the heterologously 505 

expressed protein. The E. coli strain is thus a useful microbial cell factory for production of 506 

recombinant SodFMs, which should find widespread utility as expression host of choise, enabling 507 

improved production of protein for studies of the biochemical, biophysical and structural properties 508 

of this remarkable family of metalloenzymes. 509
