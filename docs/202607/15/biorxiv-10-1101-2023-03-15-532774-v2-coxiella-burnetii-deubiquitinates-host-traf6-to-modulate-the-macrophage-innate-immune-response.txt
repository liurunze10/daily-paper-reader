Abstract 20 

Coxiella  burnetii  is a highly infectious, aerosol -transmitted  obligate  intracellular bacterium that 21 

causes human Q fever and replicates within alveolar macrophages during pulmonary infection .22 

Successful infection re quires the C. burnetii Type IVB secretion system (T4BSS) , which delivers 23 

bacterial  effector proteins into the host cytoplasm to  remodel host processes and suppress the 24 

innate immune response. In the lung, IL -17 promotes antibacterial immunity by activating ACT1 -25 

TRAF6 -dependent pathways that drive inflammatory gene expression, reactive oxygen species 26 

production , and neutrophil recruitment. However, how intracellular pathogens subvert IL -17 27 

signaling pathways to promote survival within macrophages remains poorly understood. Here, we 28 

show that  C.  burnetii  evades IL -17 -mediated host defense by targeting TRAF6, a central 29 

ubiquitin -dependent regulator of innate immune signaling. IL -17 signaling through ACT1 -TRAF6 30 

restricts  C.  burnetii  viability  in  macrophages; however, C. burnetii impairs IL -17 -induced NF -κB31 

and MAPK activation, particularly JNK phosphorylation. C.  burnetii  T4BSS activity suppresses  IL -32 

17 -driven reactive oxygen species production and neutrophil recruitment. We  identif y the C. 33 

burnetii T4BSS effector  EmcB  as a TRAF6 -targeting  deubiquitinase  that disrupts IL -17 -34 

dependent antimicrobial and chemotactic responses. Together these findings reveal that C. 35 

burnetii downregulates intracellular innate immune signaling by targeting TRAF6, enabling 36 

evasion of the macrophage antimicrobial response and limiting neutrophil -mediated immunity. 37

Significance Statement 39 

Coxiella burnetii causes Q fever and survives inside macrophages, immune cells that normally 40 

help eliminate inhaled pathogens. This study identifies a mechanism by which C. burnetii weakens 41 

macrophage defenses. We show that the bacterium disrupts macrophage IL -17 signaling , an 42 

immune pathway that promotes inflammatory gene expression, reactive oxygen species 43 

production, and neutrophil recruitment. Mechanistically, C. burnetii uses the secreted enzyme 44 

EmcB to target TRAF6, a central signaling protein required for IL -17 -depen dent antimicrobial 45 

responses. These findings reveal how an intracellular bacterial pathogen disables a key immune 46 

signaling hub to promote survival and limit innate immune activation, providing broader insight 47 

into how pathogens manipulate host immunity. 48

C. burnetii suppresses IL -17 -ACT1 -TRAF6 dependent transcriptional activation. 96 

Upon IL -17 receptor engagement, ACT1 recruits TRAF6 and promotes K63 -linked TRAF6 97 

polyubiquitination, thereby activating downstream NF -κB an d MAPK signaling cascades 17 . Given 98 

the central role of this pathway in coordinating pro -inflammatory host -responses and our previous 99 

finding that C. burnetii targets IL -17 intracellular signaling early during macrophage infection 2, we 100 

hypothesized that C. burnetii suppresses IL -17 activation of N F-κB and/or MAPK pathways. 101 

Infected alveolar macrophages were stimulated with IL -17 and phosphorylation levels of NF -κB102 

p65, JNK/SAPK, and p38 MAPK were quantified. In mock - and T4BSS -deficient (ΔdotA ) C. 103 

burnetii mutant -infected cells, IL -17 stimulation robustly increased phosphorylation of p38 MAPK, 104 

and JNK/SAPK, with a modest increase observed for NF -κB p65 ( Figure 1A -C). In contrast, wild -105 

type C. burnetii infection significantly attenuated signaling, reducing p65 and p 38 phosphorylation 106 

by approximately 40% and 60% (Figure 1A and B). Notably, IL -17 -induced JNK/SAPK 107 

phosphorylation levels were nearly eliminated in WT -infected cells (Figure 1C ). 108

The IL17R -ACT1 -TRAF6 pathway is required for IL -17 -mediated restriction of C. burnetii 121 

infection. 122 

Our prior work established that IL -17 stimulation enhances macrophage -mediated killing of C. 123 

burnetii 2. Given our data that the C. burnetii T4BSS suppresses IL -17 -dependent activation of the 124 

ACT1 -TRAF6 pathway, we hypothesized that the IL -17R -ACT1 -TRAF6 pathway is required for 125 

IL -17 -mediated restriction of C. burnetii infection . To directly test the requirement for IL -17 126 

receptor and TRAF6 -dependent signaling, IL -17RA and TRAF6 macrophage knockouts (Δil -17ra 127 

and Δtraf6 ) were generated using CRISPR/Cas9 ( Supplemental Figure 1A -B). Because we 128 

were unable to generate an ACT1 knockout in macrophages, siRNA was used to deplete ACT1 129 

protein ( Supplemental Figure 1C ). As expected, IL -17 stimulation of wild -type macrophages 130 

reduced C. burnetii replication over six days and significantly limited CCV size ( Figure 2A -C). 131 

However, IL -17 treatment failed to restrict bacterial replication or CCV size in Δil -17ra and Δtraf6 132 

macrophages , as well as siRNA -depleted ACT1 macrophages (Figure 2D and E ). These data 133 

indicat e that IL -17 -mediated antimicrobial activity requires both receptor engagement and 134 

downstream ACT1 and TRAF6 signaling. 135

Interestingly , C. burnetii replication improved in Δtraf6 macrophages even in the absence of IL -17 137

Together, these findings establish that the IL -17R -ACT1 -TRAF6 axis is required for IL -17 -140 

mediated restriction of C. burnetii infection and further identify TRAF6 as a key determinant of 141

dependent activation of NADPH -oxidases (NOX) 19 . Because elevated ROS restricts C. burnetii 146 

replication 20 , we hypothesized that C. burnetii T4BSS -mediated inhibition of TRAF6 suppresses 147 

IL -17 -induced oxidative stress in infected macrophages. We first confirmed that IL -17 stimulates 148 

NOX activity in alveolar macrophages and observed a significant increase in NOX activity in wild -149 

type macrophages following IL -17 treatment, compared to untreated controls. In contrast, IL -17 150 

failed to increase NOX activity in Δil -17ra cells, demonstrating that IL -17 -ind uced ROS required 151 

the IL -17 receptor ( Supplemental Figure 2 A). 152

Discussion 261 

The innate immune response relies on proinflammatory cytokines to trigger and regulate an 262 

effective response to invading pathogens. The proinflammatory cytokine IL -17 promotes 263 

protective innate immunity by inducing antim icrobial peptides, stimulating ROS produc tion ,264 

recruiting neutrophils , and promoting IFN -γ responses 24 . Our previous studies revealed that C. 265 

burnetii downregulates intracellular IL -17 signaling in macrophages in a T4BSS -dependent 266 

manner, implicating one or more T4BSS secreted effector proteins 2. Here, we demonstrate that 267 

IL -17 -mediated restriction of C. burnetii requires IL -17R, ACT1, and TRAF6 , and that C. burnetii 268 

disrupts this pathway by promoting TRAF6 deubiquitination , thereby attenuating NF -κB and 269 

MAPK signaling . We further show that EmcB, a C. burnetii T4BSS effector and deubiquitinase, 270 

targets TRAF6 to inhibit IL -17 -mediated oxidative stress and IL -17 -induced neutrophil 271 

chemotaxis. Together, th ese findings identi fy TRAF6 as a pathogen -targeted signaling hub in the 272 

IL -17 antimicrobial response and define EmcB as a bacterial effector that disrupts TRAF6 -273 

dependent oxidative and chemotactic responses. 274

Alveolar macrophages play a key role in immune surveillance of the airway and phagocytosis of 276 

inhaled bacteria, limiting pathogen replication while preserving pulmonary function 25 . However, 277 

intracellular bacteria l pathogens , including Mycobacterium tuberculosis and Yersinia pestis , have 278 

evolved mechanisms to subvert macrophage antimicrobial response s, including inflammatory 279 

signaling and oxidative stress pathways 26,27 . One common mechanism to evade the early host 280 

immune response is by modulating key inflammatory signaling pathways. We found that C. 281 

burnetii blocks IL -17 -induced activation of NF -κB and MAPK pathways , dampen ing the immune 282 

response. Although C. burnetii has previously been shown to modulate NF -κB and MAPK in other 283 

contexts 13 -15 , this study identifies the upstream IL -17R -ACT1 -TRAF6 axis as a specific pathway 284 

targete d during infection. 285

signaling comple xes, including TAK1 , NF -κB, and MAPK 28,29 . Our finding that EmcB 289

Pathogen -mediated disruption of TRAF6 ubiquitin signaling is not unique to C. burnetii . Host 298 

deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs) , such as A20, CYLD and USP family members , modulate NF -299 

κB signaling by removing ubiquitin chains from TRAF6 30 . Bacterial pathogens have evolved 300 

analogous strategies to in hibit inflammatory signa ling. The Yersinia Type III effector protein YopJ 301 

inhibits TRAF6 and TRAF2 ubiquitination to block p38 MAPK, JNK, and NF -κB activation 31 , while 302 

the Shigella flexneri effector OspI modifies UBC13, thereby inhibiting TRAF6 activation and 303 

inflammatory signaling 32 . EmcB appears to act through a distinct biochemical mechanism with 304 

direct deubiquitination of TRAF6, but converges on the same host vulnerability: the requirement 305 

for TRAF6 ubiquitin scaffolds in innate immune signaling. Prior work identified EmcB as a 306 

deubiquit inase that targets RIG -I and suggested that the DUB has limited substrate specificity 307 

based on min imal changes in global K63 -linked ubiquitination 22 . However, deubiquitinases can 308 

act on multiple substrates since they recognize ubiquitin itself in addition to the modif ied protein 309 

substrate 33 . Recent structural analyses further suggest that the EmcB deubiquitinase domain may 310 

allow recognition of multiple host substrates 34 .311

IL -17 activates NADPH oxidases in an ACT1 - and TRAF6 -dependent manner 19 . Our data indicate 313 

that C. burnetii targets the IL -17R -ACT1 -TRAF6 pathway to block ROS production, likely as a 314 

strategy to promote bacterial survival. This interference highlights TRAF6 as a critical regulatory 315 

node that pathogens may exploit to dampen host oxidative defenses. Such a strate gy is not 316 

without precedent, as the Salmonella Typhimurium effector protein SopB inhibit s mitochondrial 317 

recruitment of TRAF6, effectively reducing mitochondrial ROS generation 35 . In this context, 318 

TRAF6 emerges as a convergent target for distinct intracellular pathogens seeking to evade ROS -319 

mediated killing. C. burnetii may also employ additional mechanisms to counteract ROS 320 

production. For example, RpoS, a stationary -phase sigma factor  required  for bacterial survival 321 

during environmental stress,  plays  an important role  in ROS resistance 36 . In addition,  a recent 322

C. burnetii may delay neutrophil recruitment during early pulmonary infection. 334

EmcB -me diated TRAF6 deubiquitination may have broader consequences for C. burnetii 336 

infection . TRAF6 is involved in autophagy, inflammasome activation, and the adaptive immune 337 

respons e, and TRAF6 -mediated ubiquitination of BECN1/Beclin 1 and Rab7 has been implicated 338 

in autophagy induction and autophagosome -lysosome fusion 42 -44 . Because EmcB has also been 339 

linked to autophag y45 , TRAF6 de ubiquitination could contribute to additional immune defects 340 

during C. burnetii infection. These possibilities remain to be tested but suggest that EmcB may 341 

broadly remodel TRAF6 -dependent host defense pathways. 342 

343 

Although our data support a model in which EmcB disrupts TRAF6 -dependent IL -17 signaling, 344 

defining the specific ubiquitin linkages removed from TRAF6 during infection will be important. In 345 

addition, EmcB has been implicated in targeting other ubiquitinated host proteins, including RIG -346 

I, raising the possibility that its effects on macrophage activation reflect coordinated regulation of 347 

multiple immune substrates. Future studies using linkage -specific ubiquitin approaches, 348 

substrate -trapping EmcB mutants, an d infection models expressing catalytically inactive EmcB 349 

will be important to determine how TRAF6 deubiquitination contributes to pathogenesis in vivo .350 

351 

Together, our findings identify TRAF6 -dependent ubiquitin signaling as a key vulnerability in the 352 

IL -17 antimicrobial response and reveal EmcB as a bacterial effector that exploits this node to 353 

suppress macrophage ROS production and neutrophil recruitment. These results expand the 354 

known functions of C. burnetii T4BSS effectors and support a model in which targeted disruption 355 

of TRAF6 allows C. burnetii to blunt early innate immune responses in the lung. 356
