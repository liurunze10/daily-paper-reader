Abstract 37 

38 

Type I interferons (IFN-I) and IFNγ exert divergent effects during tuberculosis, but the 39 

mechanisms that determine whether macrophage activation promotes host defense or 40 

inflammatory pathology remain incompletely understood. Here, we dissect the interplay between 41 

IFN-I and IFNγ in macrophage activation using genetically susceptible B6.Sst1S macrophages. 42 

We show that, during tumor necrosis factor (TNF) stimulation, susceptible macrophages enter a 43 

persistent pathological activation state (pPAS) characterized by sustained lipid peroxidation and 44 

super-induction of IFN-I responses. This pathological state is maintained by autocrine IFN-I 45 

signaling. In contrast, IFNγ priming prevents pPAS development by enhancing macrophage 46 

resilience to oxidative stress, in part through regulation of iron metabolism and induction of ferritin 47 

expression. Computational cell state transition assessment and regulation (cSTAR) analysis 48 

identified pathways and small molecules predicted to promote the transition of susceptible 49 

macrophages toward an IFNγ-induced, Mtb-resistant state. Consistent with these predictions, the 50 

CDK4/6 inhibitor trilaciclib reduced lipid peroxidation by regulating iron metabolism, whereas 51 

retinoic acid signaling enhanced GPX4 expression and lipid biosynthesis programs. Combined 52 

CDK4/6 inhibition and retinoic acid receptor activation efficiently prevented the pathological 53 

activation state. Together, these findings delineate a mechanism of IFN-I/IFNγ crosstalk during 54 

macrophage activation and identify pharmacologic strategies to prevent IFN-I-dominant, lipid 55 

peroxidation-driven macrophage pathology. 56

Introduction 59 

60 

Tuberculosis (TB), caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb), remains the leading cause of 61 

death by a single infectious agent worldwide, with 10.7 million new cases and 1.23 million deaths 62 

reported in 2024. Despite the availability of antimicrobial therapy , treatment failures occurred in 63 

12% of TB patients (WHO, 2024), primarily attributed to the emergence of multidrug-resistant 64 

strains, HIV co-infections and host immune dysregulation (Furin et al., 2019; Pai et al., 2016). 65 

Remarkably, about a quarter of the global human population has been infected with Mtb, but only 66 

a fraction of those develop TB disease and transmits the bacteria, suggesting that the majority of 67 

immunocompetent humans are capable of spontaneously controlling and, possibly, eradicating 68 

Mtb (Behr et al., 2024). In contrast, the susceptible individuals, even immunocompetent, are not 69 

sufficiently protected by existing vaccines. Therefore, better understanding mechanisms of TB 70 

susceptibility are necessary to improve TB prevention and treatment outcomes in the susceptible 71 

individuals. 72 

73 

Among central unanswered questions are the mechanisms of dysregulation of macrophages that 74 

may either eradicate the bacteria or serve as the major host cell niche for the Mtb intracellular 75 

growth and persistence. Interferons are among the main known host factors that regulate 76 

macrophage interactions with virulent Mtb (Russell et al., 2025; Vance, 2025). Despite significant 77 

overlap of the transcriptional targets and signaling pathways of type I (IFN-I) and type II 78 

(interferon-gamma) interferons (Ravi Sundar Jose Geetha et al., 2024), their effects on TB are 79 

strikingly divergent: IFN-gamma (IFN 𝛾 ) is essential for Mtb resistance, while IFN-I may induce 80 

a dysfunctional macrophage activation state associated with TB susceptibility (Moreira-Teixeira 81 

et al., 2018). IFN 𝛾 is produced by antigen-stimulated Th1 cells and natural killer (NK) cells and 82 

activates macrophages enabling them to control or kill the bacteria. It plays an essential role in 83 

host resistance to mycobacterial infections, including Mtb, as well as avirulent vaccine strain M. 84 

bovis BCG and opportunistic environmental mycobacteria in mice (Cooper et al., 1993; Flynn et 85 

al., 1993) and in humans (Fortin et al., 2007; Rosain et al., 2019). Among pleiotropic effects of 86 

IFN 𝛾 relevant to TB are the upregulation of macrophage effector functions, such as production of 87 

reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, phagocytosis, autophagy, phagosome maturation and 88 

targeting the bacteria for lysosomal degradation (Flynn et al., 1993; Gutierrez et al., 2004; 89

The type I interferons (IFN-I) are mediators of innate immunity produced by multiple cells in 98 

response to viral infections, cytokines and stress (Boccuni et al., 2022). Compared to IFN 𝛾 , their 99 

roles in TB pathogenesis are more nuanced (Moreira-Teixeira et al., 2018; Munson & Kaushal, 100 

2025). Thus, several experimental studies demonstrate protective effects of IFN-I pathway using 101 

macrophage infection with Mtb in vitro (Roberts et al., 2025) or during an early stage of Mtb 102 

infection in mice (Desvignes et al., 2012; Moreira-Teixeira et al., 2016). However, the overall 103 

impact of IFN-I on TB progression in vivo is negative. In human patients, transcriptomic studies 104 

identified an IFN-I inducible gene signature in the peripheral blood of active TB patients, which 105 

correlated with the disease severity and the risk of progression from latent to active disease (Berry 106 

et al., 2010; Cliff et al., 2013; Maertzdorf et al., 2011; Scriba et al., 2017). The upregulation of 107 

IFN-I pathways in human TB lesions was associated with TB severity (Fonseca et al., 2025). In 108 

animal TB models, more virulent Mtb strains induced higher levels of IFN-I that was associated 109 

with reduced Th1 immunity and decreased survival (Manca et al., 2005). Accordingly, inhibition 110 

of the IFN-I signaling using either the IFN-I receptor knockout or blocking antibodies reduced the 111 

inflammatory lung damage and increased the animal survival in several mouse models (Dorhoi et 112 

al., 2014; Ji et al., 2019; Moreira-Teixeira et al., 2020; Wiens & Ernst, 2016). Several mechanisms 113 

by which the IFN-I hyperactivity contributes to TB pathogenesis have been described. In vitro 114 

IFN-I suppressed macrophage responses to IFN 𝛾 (Teles et al., 2013) and exacerbated Mtb-induced 115 

macrophage death (Lee & Nathan, 2024). In vivo, IFN-I counters essential TB protective cytokine 116 

pathways: inhibits IL-12 and promotes the immunosuppressive IL-10 production (McNab et al., 117 

2014); promotes macrophage polarization towards anti-inflammatory state (Benard et al., 2018); 118 

induces IL-1Ra, which blocks IL-1 (Ji et al., 2019), an essential protective cytokine (Mayer-Barber 119 

et al., 2010); promotes the neutrophil-driven lung damage (Chowdhury et al., 2024; Moreira-120

Our studies demonstrated a major role of the IFN-I pathway hyperactivity in the context of TB 125 

susceptible host – the sst1 -susceptible mouse model of human-like pulmonary TB, which is 126 

characterized by the development of necrotic TB lesions (Kramnik et al., 2000; Pichugin et al., 127 

2009; S. M. Yabaji, S. Lata, I. Gavrish, et al., 2025; S. M. Yabaji, S. Lata, A. E. Tseng, et al., 128 

2025). In vitro the sst1 -susceptible macrophages displayed an aberrant activation characterized by 129 

the superinduction of IFN b that led to the development of the integrated stress response 130 

(Bhattacharya et al., 2021; He et al., 2013). The critical role of the IFN-I-driven ISR in vivo was 131 

further supported by the administration of the Integrated Stress Response Inhibitor (ISRIB) or 132 

blockade of the IFN-I pathway that reduced lung pathology and Mtb burdens in the sst1 -133 

susceptible congenic B6.Sst1S mice (Bhattacharya et al., 2021; Ji et al., 2019; Krug et al., 2023). 134 

Next, we demonstrated that the aberrant activation of the sst1 -susceptible macrophages was 135 

primarily driven by coupling of the IFN-I pathway to dysfunctional antioxidant response, leading 136 

to the accumulation of lipid peroxidation products, unresolving stress and compromised Mtb 137 

resistance (Shivraj M. Yabaji et al., 2025). 138 

139 

Several lines of evidence suggest that boosting Th1-mediated adaptive immunity may correct the 140 

sst1 -susceptible phenotype: subcutaneous immunization with BCG or Mtb delayed the pulmonary 141 

TB progression in the sst1 -susceptible mice (Gern et al., 2025; Yan et al., 2007). It has been 142 

established that BCG vaccination induces Th1 response in the genetically resistant and susceptible 143 

mice (Kramnik et al., 1994) and in non-human primates (Simonson et al., 2025). The BCG-induced 144 

T lymphocytes restored the ability of the sst1 -susceptible macrophages to control Mtb in vitro 145 

(Shivraj M. Yabaji et al., 2025). Therefore, we hypothesized that IFN 𝛾 may counter pathogenic 146 

effects of the IFN-I hyperactivity that drive TB progression. Moreover, an IFN 𝛾 -mimetic, 147 

rocaglamide A, identified in our previous studies, also prevented the aberrant macrophage 148 

activation and IFN b superinduction (Bhattacharya et al., 2021; Chatterjee et al., 2021; Yabaji et 149 

al., 2023). 150

Here, we demonstrate that type I and type II interferons (IFN-I and IFN 𝛾 ) antagonize each other 152 

in activated macrophages through differential regulation of iron metabolism and lipid peroxidation 153 

and propose small-molecule modulation of these pathways as a strategy to prevent pathogenic 154 

macrophage activation. 155

Results 157 

158 

1. IFN-I autocrine signaling sustains pathological macrophage activation in a TNF-159 

independent manner 160 

The IFN-I and lipid peroxidation pathways underlying the aberrant activation of the sst1 -161 

susceptible macrophages by TNF are prone to auto-amplification. Therefore, we hypothesized that 162 

their coupling maintained this pathological activation state (PAS). To test this hypothesis, first we 163 

compared the activation states of the wild type B6 and the mutant B6.Sst1S bone marrow-derived 164 

macrophages (BMDMs) after TNF withdrawal. BMDMs were initially stimulated with TNF for 165 

24 h, washed and cultured in fresh medium without exogenous TNF for an additional 16 h (Figure 166 

1A). After this period, ROS production and Ifnb1 mRNA expression in B6 BMDMs returned to 167 

baseline. In contrast, in B6.Sst1S macrophages we observed continuous ROS production, lipid 168 

peroxidation and Ifnb1 mRNA upregulation (Figure 1B and 1C, Figure 1-figure supplement 1A, 169 

1B). Consistent with the hyperactivity of the IFN-I pathway, we also observed the upregulation of 170 

Rsad2 mRNA and downregulation of genes involved in lipid and cholesterol biosynthesis Srebf2 ,171 

Scd2 , and Dhcr24 (Figure 1D, Figure 1-figure supplement 1C, 1D, and 1E) and unresponsiveness 172 

to IFN 𝛾 (Figure 1E). After blocking TNF signaling at 24 h, the level of intracellular 4-HNE adducts 173 

remained elevated exclusively in the B6.Sst1S macrophages (Figure 1-figure supplement 1J). 174 

Restimulation of B6.Sst1S macrophages with TNF at 24 h (Figure 1A) resulted in further 175 

upregulation of Ifnb1 mRNA (Figure 1F, Figure 1-figure supplement 1F), increased levels of ROS 176 

and 4-HNE adducts (Figure 1G, Figure 1-figure supplement 1G and 1H) and persistent suppression 177 

of lipid biosynthesis genes (Figure 1H, Figure 1-figure supplement 1I). Thus, after initial TNF 178 

stimulation, the sst1 -susceptible macrophages develop a self-sustained persistent pathological 179 

activation state (pPAS), which is characterized by continuous ROS production, dysregulated lipid 180

To test the role of IFN-I in the pPAS maintenance, we blocked IFN-I signaling using IFNAR1-183 

specific antibodies after 24 h of TNF stimulation (Figure 2A). This blockade restored the B6.Sst1S 184 

macrophage responsiveness to IFN 𝛾 (Figure 2B) and prevented the 4-HNE adducts accumulation 185

reduced the rates of lipid peroxidation (LAA biosynthesis) at 12 and 24 h of TNF stimulation, 188 

respectively (Figure 2D, Figure 2-figure supplement 1B and 1C). These data suggest that IFN-I 189 

signaling was a major driver of lipid peroxidation in TNF-activated B6.Sst1S macrophages at both 190 

the pPAS initiation and maintenance stages. 191 

Accordingly, treatment of naïve B6 and B6.Sst1S BMDMs with IFN 𝛽 alone induced lipid 192 

peroxidation, as determined by the rate of LAA production (Figure 2E) and the intracellular 4-193 

HNE adducts accumulation (Figure 2F), which occurred in a sst1 -independent manner. We 194 

hypothesized that this effect could be mediated by itaconate, an electrophilic metabolite produced 195 

by the IFN-inducible enzyme Acod1 (Lampropoulou et al., 2016; Peace & O'Neill, 2022). Indeed, 196 

treatment of naïve BMDMs with 4-octyl itaconate (4-OI) led to the 4-HNE adducts accumulation 197 

(Figure 2G, Figure 2-figure supplement 1D). 198 

Considering other factors involved in lipid peroxidation in TNF-stimulated macrophages, IFN-I 199 

signaling neither affected the levels of the intracellular labile iron pool (Figure 2H and 2I, 200 

respectively), nor did it appeared to be a major driver of ROS production (Figure 2-figure 201 

supplement 1F and 1G). In these settings, however, IFN-I suppressed lipid biosynthesis genes, as 202 

evidenced by their upregulation after the IFN-I receptor blockade (Figure 2J, Figure 2-figure 203 

supplement 1E). 204 

Taken together, these data suggest that persistent IFN-I signaling maintains pPAS by stimulating 205 

itaconate biosynthesis and suppressing lipid biosynthesis genes, thus promoting the accumulation 206 

of toxic lipid peroxidation products. 207

2. IFN 𝛾 priming prevents pPAS by limiting intracellular iron availability 209 

Previous studies demonstrated that the induction of Th1-mediated immunity partially corrected the 210 

sst1 -mediated Mtb susceptibility in vivo (Gern et al., 2025). Accordingly, co-stimulation of the 211 

B6.Sst1S BMDMs with IFN 𝛾 and TNF significantly increased their ability to control the 212 

intracellular Mtb growth in vitro (Figure 3A). Therefore, we tested whether macrophage priming 213 

with IFN 𝛾 could prevent or ameliorate pPAS induced by TNF in the susceptible macrophages 214 

(Figure 3-figure supplement 1A). 215 

Although IFN 𝛾 alone moderately stimulated ROS production and LPO synthesis, the IFN 𝛾 216 

priming significantly reduced LPO synthesis and accumulation induced by TNF (Figure 3B, Figure 217 

3-figure supplement 1B). Moreover, IFN 𝛾 priming prevented the Ifnb1 super-induction (Figure 218 

3C), and reduced the upregulation of IFN-I-driven pPAS markers, such as Rsad2 , the integrated 219 

stress response (ISR) genes, Trib3 and Chac1 , and Acod1 (Figure 3C, 3D, 3F). Of note, IFN 𝛾 220 

priming also suppressed the 4-HNE adduct accumulation caused by synthetic itaconate 4-OI 221 

(Figure 3G, Figure 3-figure supplement 1C). The latter observations suggested that IFN 𝛾 priming 222 

prevented the LPO accumulation independently of the IFN-I - Acod1 – itaconate downregulation. 223 

In addition, the IFN 𝛾 priming suppressed the expression of lipid biosynthesis genes, similarly to 224 

the IFN-I mediated effect of TNF described above (Figure 3H, Figure 3-figure supplement 1D). 225 

Also, IFN 𝛾 priming did not enhance the GPX4 expression in B6.Sss1S BMDMs (Figure 3-figure 226 

supplement 1E), which is maintained at lower levels compared to the B6 BMDMs during TNF 227 

stimulation (Shivraj M. Yabaji et al., 2025). The latter observations suggested that IFN 𝛾 prevented 228 

the LPO accumulation independently of the downregulation of the IFN-I - Acod1 – itaconate axis 229 

and lipid biosynthesis. 230 

To reveal the preventive effect of IFN 𝛾 on pPAS development, we compared transcriptional 231 

responses of the B6 and B6.Sss1S BMDMs to IFN 𝛾 , TNF and their combination at the pPAS 232 

incipient stage, i.e. at 12 h after the cytokine stimulation, using bulk RNA-seq. The co-stimulation 233 

with IFN 𝛾 and TNF induced similar shifts in cell states and reduced the PC1 distance between the 234 

resistant and susceptible macrophages, as evidenced by the Principal Component Analysis (PCA) 235 

(Figure 3I), and by the similarities of their transcriptomes (Figure 3J). Thus, the MHC class II 236 

genes were upregulated, and the genes related to tissue remodeling, alternative macrophage 237

iron pool, IFN 𝛾 prevents lipid peroxidation and promotes Hif1a-mediated metabolic 263 

reprogramming of macrophages, thereby increasing their resilience to oxidative stress and 264 

preventing self-sustained pathological activation. 265

3. IFN 

𝛾 mimetics prevent pPAS 266 

To determine how to mimic the effect of IFN 𝛾 using small molecules, we used cell State Transition 267 

Assessment and Regulation (cSTAR) analysis, a recently developed systems biology approach that 268 

integrates multi-omics data to map distinct cell states and predict perturbations controlling 269 

transitions between them (Kholodenko et al., 2023; Rukhlenko et al., 2022). 270 

We used RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) data to map the cytokine priming states of B6 and B6.Sst1S 271 

macrophages following a 12 h stimulation with TNF, IFN 𝛾 , and their combination (Figure 4A). To 272 

capture these dynamics, we built a State Transition Vector (STV TB ). Unlike previous works relying 273 

on classification methods (Rukhlenko et al., 2022; Yabaji et al., 2023), we utilized regression 274 

methods to achieve higher quantitative precision. This STV TB determines the direction of gene 275 

expression changes towards the most TB-resistant state while integrating both genetic background 276 

(B6 vs. B6.Sst1S) and cytokine priming effects. Dynamic phenotype descriptor (DPD TB ) scores 277 

were then calculated to quantify the phenotypic shifts induced by each perturbation (Rukhlenko et 278 

al., 2022; Yabaji et al., 2023). It can be seen (Figure 4B) that TNF stimulation of B6.Sst1S 279 

macrophages decreased their DPD TB score, in agreement with their pathological activation state. 280 

In contrast, TNF and IFN 𝛾 stimulation of B6 macrophages increased their DPD TB scores, with the 281 

highest score obtained by combining both cytokines, reflecting their known synergistic effect on 282 

macrophage activation. Although IFN 𝛾 and IFN 𝛾 + TNF stimulation of B6.Sst1S macrophages 283 

resulted in increased DPD TB scores, these responses were significantly weaker than those observed 284 

in B6 macrophages (Figure 4A). The calculated DPD TB scores strongly correlated with Mtb fold 285 

change data (Figure 4B). Thus, in these settings, a negative DPD TB is indicative of Mtb-permissive 286 

macrophage cell state, whereas an increase in DPD TB score is indicative of increased Mtb 287 

resistance. 288 

By analyzing transcriptional responses to perturbations using publicly available perturbation 289 

transcriptomics datasets, cSTAR enables reconstruction of the protein signaling network wiring 290 

(Deng et al., 2025). Here, we applied cSTAR to reconstruct the network that controls the transition 291 

between the susceptible and resistant macrophage activation states and identified key signaling 292 

pathways (nodes) that govern macrophage priming for Mtb resistance. Predictions of how 293 

activation of each node by 1% will change DPD TB score are shown in Figure 4C. As anticipated, 294

Discussion 388 

389 

Our findings reveal a fundamental divergence between type I and type II interferon programs in 390 

macrophages. They suggest that the conflicting roles of the type I and type II interferons during 391 

chronic TB can be accounted for by their opposing effects on lipid peroxidation: IFN-I enhances, 392 

while IFN 𝛾 prevents it (Table 1). We found that persistent IFN-I signaling promotes a self-393 

amplifying cycle of ROS production and lipid peroxidation that impairs host resistance to Mtb, 394 

whereas IFN 𝛾 primes macrophages for antimicrobial activation while simultaneously increasing 395 

their resilience to oxidative damage through iron sequestration, thereby limiting the conversion of 396 

ROS into damaging lipid peroxidation products. Thus, beyond differences in downstream effector 397 

pathways, IFN-I and IFN 𝛾 differ in their ability to couple inflammatory activation to cyto-398 

protection, a distinction with important biological causes and pathological consequences. 399 

400 

We demonstrate that persistent IFN-I signaling and autocatalytic lipid peroxidation form a self-401 

reinforcing pathological circuit, in which ROS-driven lipid damage further amplifies inflammatory 402 

signaling leading to the IFN-I pathway hyperactivity. This circuit is sufficient to maintain the 403 

persistent pathological activation state (pPAS) of macrophages in an autocrine manner after 404 

removal of TNF, the initial PAS trigger in our in vitro model. The pPAS is characterized by the 405 

continuous ROS generation, the accumulation of lipid peroxidation products, suppression of lipid 406 

biosynthesis, and further escalation of the IFN-I pathway upon repeated TNF stimulation. 407 

Mechanistically, this vicious cycle develops in sst1 susceptible macrophages because of their 408 

failure to upregulate the sst1 -encoded IFN-inducible proteins SP110 and SP140, which serve as 409 

feedback regulators of the IFN-I pathway either directly (Ji et al., 2021; Witt et al., 2025) or 410 

indirectly, downstream of Myc hyperactivity (Shivraj M. Yabaji et al., 2025). We propose that other 411

molecular pathologies characterized by persistent cellular stress and IFN-I hyperactivity, such as 412 

interferonopathies, may be driven by similar IFN-I/lipid peroxidation amplification loops. 413 

414 

Because type I interferons are produced by virus-infected cells, as well as by cells undergoing 415 

chronic stress or damage (Boccuni et al., 2022), we hypothesize that the IFN-I/lipid peroxidation 416 

amplification loop may represent an element of an evolutionarily conserved host-defense strategy 417 

for containing or eliminating infected, damaged, or otherwise aberrant cells. Consistent with this 418 

hypothesis, toxic aldehydes generated by lipid peroxidation have been shown to limit the 419 

replication of viruses and intracellular pathogens (Anaya-Sanchez et al., 2025), and IFN-I 420 

signaling has been implicated in the elimination of epigenetically altered or premalignant cells in 421 

vitro (Leonova et al., 2013; Zhao et al., 2021). Similarly, lipid peroxidation-induced cellular 422 

senescence could restrict aberrant cell proliferation. We found that IFN-I promotes lipid 423 

peroxidation by inducing ROS without iron sequestration. This mode permits ROS amplification 424 

and may be more efficient in containing acute infection. However, during chronic infection or 425 

persistent inflammation, sustained activation of this pathway becomes maladaptive. In chronic TB 426 

lesions, this response is particularly detrimental because the lipid-rich cell wall and non-replicative 427 

persistence programs of Mtb allow the bacterium to withstand the initial host attack, whereas host 428 

macrophages are vulnerable to self-inflicted oxidative injury. The resulting macrophage damage 429 

impairs their antimicrobial effector functions and antigen presentation thereby creating a430 

permissive niche for the bacterial persistence and growth. This framework provides a plausible 431 

explanation for the dual role of type I interferon in TB (Desvignes et al., 2012; Munson & Kaushal, 432 

2025). 433 

434 

In contrast, IFN 𝛾 is produced not by the infected or stressed cells themselves, but by specialized 435 

immune cells, primarily T lymphocytes and NK cells, upon recognition of specific targets. Thus, 436 

IFN 𝛾 functions as a paracrine messenger that alerts and preconditions host tissues before or during 437 

pathogen encounter. Its local and systemic pleiotropic effects include priming macrophages for 438 

subsequent activation through epigenetic and metabolic reprogramming, enabling them to 439 

reallocate cellular resources toward antimicrobial effector functions. Our current studies reveal 440 

that, in parallel with boosting ROS production, IFN 𝛾 enhances macrophage resilience to oxidative 441 

stress by limiting the labile iron pool, thereby preventing the amplification of ROS-mediated host-442

Both type I and type II interferon pathways evolved as host defense mechanisms against 449 

taxonomically diverse pathogens. Conceivably, their cross-regulation and mutual antagonism help 450 

shape responses appropriate for viral versus intracellular bacterial infections, respectively. 451 

However, during Mtb infection lesion dynamics and architecture critically influence the local IFN 452 

balance. The IFN-I-mediated protection is transient and limited to the early stages of infection, 453 

when and where macrophage - Mtb interactions occur primarily in a cell autonomous manner. 454 

Following adequate T cell activation, IFN 𝛾 -mediated macrophage reprogramming provides a more 455 

efficient coordinated response, enabling both control of intracellular Mtb and tissue protection. 456 

Consistent with this model, BCG vaccination (Yan et al., 2007) or prior Mtb infection (Gern et al., 457 

2025) accelerate the formation of IFN 𝛾 -dominated protective lesions, even in the genetically 458 

susceptible but immunocompetent mice. 459

However, in the susceptible animals, preexisting immunity delays, but does not prevent lesion 461 

progression (Cohen et al., 2022; S. M. Yabaji, S. Lata, A. E. Tseng, et al., 2025; Yan et al., 2007). 462 

Myeloid cells increasingly dominate TB lesions, whereas T cells are relegated to the lesion 463 

periphery (Branchett et al., 2025; McCaffrey et al., 2026; McCaffrey et al., 2022; S. M. Yabaji, S. 464 

Lata, A. E. Tseng, et al., 2025). As expanding myeloid compartments spatially segregate infected 465 

macrophages from IFN 𝛾 -producing lymphocytes, the local interferon balance shifts toward IFN-I 466 

dominance, because Mtb-infected and aberrantly activated stressed macrophages produce IFN b in 467 

a cell autonomous manner. This shift leads to gradual loss of protection within individual lesions 468 

and promotes hyperinflammatory tissue damage. Eventually, bacteria resume intracellular 469 

replication, destroy host cells, and begin growing extracellularly. Subsequent neutrophil 470 

recruitment expands local tissue damage, promotes bacterial expansion (Eruslanov et al., 2005), 471 

and further stimulates the IFN-I pathway (Kotov et al., 2023). In addition, systemic IFN-I 472 

hyperactivity compromises IFN 𝛾 -mediated hematopoietic reprogramming (Kaufmann et al., 2018; 473

Preventing macrophages from transitioning into an IFN-I-dominant pathological activation state 477 

may therefore represent a logical therapeutic strategy. We searched for small molecules that could 478 

recapitulate protective components of IFN 𝛾 -mediated macrophage reprogramming and promote 479 

an oxidative-stress-resilient, Mtb-resistant state. 480 

Using cSTAR analysis of resistant and susceptible macrophage states, we found that IFN 𝛾 priming 481 

shifted B6.Sst1S susceptible macrophages toward the transcriptional state of B6 resistant 482 

macrophages. We then confirmed that this transcriptome shift is closely correlated with an 483 

increased ability of susceptible macrophages to control Mtb replication. Thus, using transcriptomic 484 

data, we have built DPD TB score as a novel in vitro metric for macrophages priming to be either 485 

susceptible or resistant to Mtb infection, consistent with the in vivo phenotype. 486 

487 

Next, we used publicly available data to identify perturbations that could regulate the transition of 488 

susceptible macrophages toward the IFN 𝛾 -mediated Mtb-resistant state. This analysis identified 489 

several candidate pathways predicted to either mimic or antagonize the effects of IFN 𝛾 . However, 490 

the pathways that increased macrophage resistance only partially recapitulated the potent effect of 491 

IFN 𝛾 priming. Therefore, we assessed interactions among the candidate pathways and identified 492 

two mutually antagonistic regulatory clusters. The Mtb resistance-promoting cluster comprised 493 

retinoic acid receptor (RAR), mTOR, and IFN 𝛾 signaling modules, which showed mutual intra-494 

cluster activation. The antagonistic cluster, consisting of PAK, CDK1/2, and CDK4/6 nodes, was 495 

predicted to promote Mtb susceptibility. We focused on the interplay between CDK4/6, the 496 

strongest contributor to the Mtb-susceptible phenotype, and RAR, a key contributor to the Mtb-497 

resistant phenotype. Network topology analysis suggested that simultaneous CDK4/6 inhibition 498 

and RAR stimulation could synergize to induce an Mtb-resistant macrophage activation state. 499

The importance of retinoic acid (RA) in host resistance to tuberculosis has been demonstrated in 501 

humans and animal models (Faye Lanni, 2025; Podell et al., 2022). However, the specific 502 

mechanisms underlying RA-mediated protection in TB remain incompletely understood. RA has 503

cSTAR analysis 683 

684 

To build State Transition Vector STV TB we have applied ordinary least squares linear 685 

regression of transcriptomic data for cytokine stimulation (Figure 4A) over Mtb fold change data 686 

(Figure 3A). Since the available perturbation transcriptomics data from CMAP dataset had only 687 

978 genes measured directly (Subramanian et al., 2017), we have built STV TB in the space of these 688 

978 genes. To reflect increase of TB resistance in response to the increase of appropriate DPD 689 

score, DPD TB , we have multiplied STV TB on -1, so the increase in DPD TB corresponds to the 690 

decrease in Mtb fold change, and consequently, increase in TB-resistance. 691 

Next, we utilized publicly available perturbation data to understand how priming of 692 

macrophages towards Mtb resistance can be controlled. Particularly, we used a cSTAR pipeline 693 

adapted for perturbation transcriptomics data, that allows reconstruction of the protein signaling 694 

network wiring from transcriptomics responses perturbation data (Deng et al., 2025). This 695 

approach allows us to access how pharmacological interventions targeting different druggable 696 

proteins move macrophage states along the STV TB , by changing DPD TB score. Among cellular 697 

models that are considered relevant for the task of controlling macrophage cell states, THP1 cell 698 

line has the highest amount of perturbation data. Accordingly, we have collected perturbation data 699 

for this cell line to run our cSTAR pipeline (Deng et al., 2025). While most of perturbation data 700 

that we used originated from CMAP dataset (Subramanian et al., 2017), this dataset lacks 701 

perturbations to some critically important nodes, such as RAR receptors and receptors of IFN 𝛾 .702 

For these nodes we have taken data from the following GEO accession numbers: GSE46268, 703 

GSE244129 and GSE229710. Since THP1 cells do not directly resemble sst1 -driven susceptibility 704 

phenotype, we excluded IFN type I and TNF from the network, and focused on other key signaling 705 

nodes. 706 

In brief, we collated from different publicly available sources (see above) a dataset of perturbation 707 

responses, combining log fold changes of gene expression levels with respect to appropriate 708 

control. For each data point, we have calculated DPD TB responses to specific perturbation. Next, 709 

we have selected core network components based on ranking of DPD TB responses, as well as on 710 

our prior knowledge of key pathways involved. Then, we inferred module activity coefficients, 711
