Abstract 
26 
Background: Janus kinase (JAK)-Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription (STAT) 
27 
pathway is a key regulator of inflammatory signaling in ulcerative colitis (UC). While most 
28 
studies have focused on JAK/tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2) inhibitors effects on immune cell-
29 
mediated responses, their direct epithelial impact remains less known. We investigated 
30 
epithelial-specific transcriptional responses to JAK/TYK2 inhibitors using patient-derived 
31 
intestinal epithelial organoids (IEOs) under UC-relevant conditions. 
32 
Methods: Colonic IEOs from UC patients were pretreated with various concentrations of 
33 
tofacitinib, upadacitinib, filgotinib, brepocitinib, and deucravacitinib for 1 hour prior to 
34 
stimulation with IFNβ, IFNγ, or IFNλ1 for Western blot analysis of STAT1/3 and TYK2 
35 
phosphorylation. For transcriptomic profiling, IEOs were pretreated with upadacitinib or 
36 
deucravacitinib for 16 hours, followed by 8-hour stimulation with IFNγ, IFNλ1, TNF, or IFNγ 
37 
+ TNF. Bulk RNA sequencing assessed differential gene expression, and multiplex assays 
38 
quantified chemokine secretion. Ki67 immunohistochemistry on colonic biopsies from healthy 
39 
controls, and UC patients with and without JAK inhibitors-treatment were assessed for 
40 
epithelial proliferation. 
41 
Results: IFNs induced distinct STAT1/3 and TYK2 activation, with IFNβ/γ eliciting stronger 
42 
phosphorylation than IFNλ1. All JAK/TYK2 inhibitors regulated pSTAT1/3 and pTYK2, with 
43 
upadacitinib most strongly inhibiting pSTAT1/3 and deucravacitinib selectively targeting 
44 
pTYK2. Transcriptomic analysis revealed extensive cytokine-driven gene regulation, with 
45 
IFNγ + TNF eliciting the strongest response. Enrichment analysis highlighted upregulation of 
46 
IFN signaling, antigen presentation, and innate immune pathways, alongside downregulation 
47 
of cell-cycle processes. Drug-response profiling showed minimal transcriptional changes with 
48 
upadacitinib and deucravacitinib alone. Upadacitinib broadly modulated IFNs and IFNγ + 
49 
.

Background 
61 
Ulcerative colitis (UC), a major subtype of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), is characterized 
62 
by chronic, relapsing inflammation of the colonic mucosa, with multifactorial etiology (1). The 
63 
pathogenesis of UC is likely driven by a complex interplay between genetic susceptibility, 
64 
environmental factors and dysregulated microbiome-host interactions, with epithelial barrier 
65 
dysfunction emerging as a central contributor to disease progression (2). Neutrophil infiltration 
66 
and elevated secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines including interferon-gamma (IFNγ), 
67 
tumor necrosis factor (TNF), and interleukins (IL-5, IL-6, IL-13, IL-17, and IL-22), 
68 
significantly contributes to the mucosal damage and impairs epithelial homeostasis (3, 4). 
69 
Current therapeutic strategies include conventional immunosuppressants, targeted biologic 
70 
agents, and small molecule therapies such as Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors and Sphingosine 1-
71 
phosphate (S1P) modulators (5-8). These therapies are designed to inhibit inflammation rather 
72 
than promoting mucosal healing and restore intestinal homeostasis (9). However, a substantial 
73 
subset of patients remains refractory to these interventions. Despite accumulating evidence 
74 
indicating epithelial dysfunction in both disease onset and persistence (10, 11), drugs 
75 
specifically targeting epithelial regeneration and barrier restoration are still underdeveloped 
76 
(12, 13). 
77 
JAK inhibitors have emerged as a promising therapeutic modality due to their capacity to 
78 
simultaneously modulate multiple cytokines (14). The JAK-Signal Transducer and Activator 
79 
of Transcription (STAT) pathway is an evolutionarily conserved signaling pathway and a 
80 
crucial mediator in IBD pathogenesis (15). This pathway comprises four intracellular non-
81 
receptor tyrosine kinases (JAK1, JAK, JAK3 and tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2)) and seven STAT 
82 
family members (STAT1, STAT2, STAT3, STAT4, STAT5A, STAT5B, and STAT6) (16). 
83 
Activation of the JAK-STAT pathway occurs through inflammatory cytokines including IFNs 
84 
.

6 
and the interleukin families IL-6, IL-9, IL-10, and IL-12/23 (17-20). Interferons have 
85 
multifaceted role in the intestine, acting as a critical regulatory node for antiviral defense, 
86 
maintenance of intestinal homeostasis and modulation of inflammatory responses in IBD 
87 
pathophysiology (19, 21). Type II IFN (IFNγ) serves as an immune modulator important for 
88 
resistance to many microbial infections, while type I (e.g., IFNα, IFNβ) and type III (IFNλ 
89 
subtypes) share similar induction mechanisms, stimulate interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), 
90 
and are mostly recognized for their ability to restrict virus infections (22). However, type-I 
91 
IFNs can activate nearly all nucleated cells, whereas type III IFN responses are limited to few 
92 
cell types, including epithelial cells (21, 22). The JAK-STAT transduction cascade is initiated 
93 
when each IFN type binds to its specific receptor complex, each of which is associated with 
94 
distinct combination of JAK kinase. Interferon signaling in intestinal epithelial cells (IECs) has 
95 
been reported to exert both protective and detrimental effects (21, 23, 24). 
96 
Currently, several JAK/TYK2 inhibitors are approved or in various stages of clinical 
97 
development for IBD. Among these, tofacitinib (25) (oral pan-JAK inhibitor), upadacitinib (26) 
98 
and filgotinib (27) (oral JAK1-specific inhibitors) have received regulatory approval for 
99 
moderate-to-severe UC patients. Given the potentially reduced toxicity profile compared to 
100 
JAK1-3 inhibitors, the more recently developed TYK2 inhibitors such as deucravacitinib (28) 
101 
(TYK2 inhibitor) and brepocitinib (29) (JAK1/TYK2 inhibitor) are under investigation as 
102 
potential therapeutic options. The different IFN types differ in their requirements of JAKs and 
103 
STATs indicating that the pan-JAK and the JAK-specific inhibitors will have different effects 
104 
on the cell depending on which ligand-receptor complex is activated (19, 30). This differential 
105 
selectivity confers potential therapeutic advantages; JAK1-specific inhibition may effectively 
106 
suppress IFNγ-mediated inflammatory cascades while TYK2 inhibition could preserve 
107 
beneficial epithelial repair mechanisms mediated through alternative JAK pathways. Further, 
108 
these small molecules may exhibit different affinity profiles and function by inhibiting specific 
109 
.

JAKs, thereby selectively modulating specific inflammatory arms of epithelial JAK-STAT 
110 
signaling while preserving essential homeostatic functions. The IECs play a critical role in IBD 
111 
pathophysiology (31) and are reported to respond to diverse pro-inflammatory stimuli by 
112 
inducing inflammatory genes in the JAK-STAT and NFκB pathways, antigen presentation 
113 
genes (32, 33), cell death (34-38), antiviral protection system (34), barrier function disruption 
114 
(39, 40), and regulation of mucosal healing (39, 41, 42). JAK inhibitors can modulate these 
115 
inflammatory pathways mediated by cytokines in immune as well as non-immune cell types 
116 
(7, 43). Furthermore, multiple IBD-associated genes encode or regulate components of this 
117 
JAK pathway, highlighting its genetic significance in disease etiology (44-46). However, 
118 
inhibition of IFN signaling may compromise innate antiviral defense mechanisms, as 
119 
evidenced by increased infection rates observed with JAK inhibitors in therapies (47). 
120 
Despite the central roles of the intestinal epithelium in mucosal healing, the cellular and 
121 
molecular mechanisms underlying its responses to JAK inhibitors remain incompletely 
122 
understood. Patient-derived intestinal epithelial organoids (IEOs) closely mimic three-
123 
dimensional intestinal epithelial architecture and maintain the genetic profiles of the donor, 
124 
facilitating investigation of patient-specific responses and drug mechanisms of action (48, 49). 
125 
Therefore, this study aims to delineate the effects of first- and next-generation JAK/TYK2-
126 
specific inhibitors using patient-derived colonic IEOs treated with UC-relevant cytokines.

Culturing and experiments in HT-29 cells  
132

136

137

138

139

140 
Dulbecco’s Phosphate Buffered Saline (DPBS, #8537, Sigma-Aldrich) before detaching using 
141 
cell scraper and 80µl lysis buffer/well, and stored at -20°C for measurement of protein 
142 
concentration and immunoblot.

557

558

559

560

561

562

563

564

565

566

567

Discussion 
659 
A central finding of our study is that JAK/TYK2 inhibitors exert a strong impact on IFNs/TNF-
660 
induced genes associated with JAK-STAT and NFκB signaling cascades, modulation of innate 
661 
antiviral defense, attenuation of cell death pathways and finally regulation of cell cycle, DNA 
662 
replication, and developmental signaling processes which are involved in induction of mucosal 
663 
healing (9, 12). IEOs are increasingly used as a tool in ex vivo drug screening and therapeutic 
664 
testing, for evaluating drug efficacy, and toxicity in patient-specific manner (89). However, 
665 
previous studies of JAK inhibitors effects on IEOs have largely focused on cytokine-induced 
666 
barrier dysfunction, cell death or cytokine release (32, 34, 37, 38, 40, 90-93), and have not 
667 
systematically examined how these drugs influence epithelial response at the transcriptional 
668 
level in a cytokine specific context. Our study addresses this by evaluating the receptor-specific 
669 
transcriptional response to JAK/TYK2 inhibitors at a defined time point (8 hours post cytokine 
670 
stimulation following 16 hours of drug pretreatment). For instance, under IFNγ/TNF 
671 
stimulation, upadacitinib excerpted broader transcriptional regulation of inflammatory 
672 
pathways compared to deucravacitinib. IFNλ1-induced gene expression was strongly 
673 
modulated by both inhibitors, while TNF-driven transcriptional changes were minimally 
674 
affected consistent with TNF signaling through a JAK-independent mechanism. To our 
675 
knowledge, this is the first study that provides insights into the multifaceted roles of JAK 
676 
inhibition in modulating intestinal epithelial responses to interferons and TNF, extending 
677 
beyond their established role in immune cell modulation in IBD. 
678 
Inhibitors with different JAK selectivity are expected to exert distinct cellular effects depending 
679 
on the receptor-ligand context (19, 30), providing a mechanistic basis for the different 
680 
transcriptional responses observed. Our comparative analysis revealed clear differences with 
681 
JAK inhibitors (upadacitinib > brepocitinib > tofacitinib > filgotinib) significantly inhibited

.

703

Cell death is a critical determinant of epithelial turnover and mucosal inflammation in IBD. 
706 
Our transcriptome analysis revealed that IFNγ, IFNλ1, IFNγ + TNF stimulation of IEOs 
707 
recapitulated cell death signatures observed in UC patients (37, 97-100). Our data indicate a 
708 
more prominent role of necroptotic, and pyroptotic cell death mechanisms compared to 
709 
apoptosis at the concentration used in our study (IFNγ at 10 ng/mL + TNF at 50 ng/mL), 
710 
whereas higher doses of IFNγ + TNF (50 ng/mL each) have been reported to favor 
711 
mitochondrial apoptosis alongside contribution from necroptotic or pyroptosis (37). Consistent 
712 
with the inhibitor-specific patterns observed for antiviral responses, upadacitinib broadly 
713 
attenuated IFNs/TNF-induced cell death programs. Deucravacitinib on the other hand 
714 
displayed selective modulation, predominantly affecting IFNλ1-induced cell death with 
715 
minimal effects on IFNγ + TNF-induced responses. Consistent with our findings, the capacity 
716 
of JAK inhibitors to mitigate cytokine-induced cell death in IECs has been previously 
717 
documented in UC and CD colonoids (97) and in CD enteroids (36). Mechanistically, 
718 
upadacitinib-mediated suppression of IFNs/TNF-induced NOS2 expression aligns with the 
719 
proposed regulation of PANoptosis via the JAK-STAT-IRF1-iNOS-NO pathway (101). These 
720 
findings extend previous observation on JAK inhibitors effects on IFNγ (50 ng/mL) induced 
721 
iNOS expression (90), highlight the molecular mechanism by which the JAK inhibitors may 
722 
attenuate inflammatory epithelial damage. 
723 
Beyond suppressing cell death, JAK inhibition also influenced epithelial proliferation. 
724 
Cytokines exert opposing effects on intestinal epithelial proliferation (102). IL22 has shown to 
725 
drive epithelial proliferation via STAT3 activation (103), while IFNγ, essentially in 
726 
combination with TNF, inhibits stem cell proliferation and increases apoptosis (104). 
727 
Consistent with this, our transcriptional analysis also revealed that IFNγ and IFNγ + TNF 
728 
conditions substantially downregulated genes involved in cell cycle/DNA replication related 
729 
pathways that upadacitinib reversed this suppression. Clinically, the OCTAVE trial of

757 
Conclusions 
758 
In summary, our comprehensive analysis of JAK/TYK2 inhibitors effect on patient-derived 
759 
IEOs challenges the current paradigm of JAK inhibitors as solely immune-modulatory agents 
760 
and reveals their capacity to directly promote genes involved in epithelial repair and 
761 
restoration. By elucidating how different JAK inhibitors differentially regulate epithelial 
762 
responses to inflammatory stimuli, our study provides insights for optimizing therapeutic 
763 
efficacy while minimizing adverse effects, ultimately advancing toward personalized JAK 
764 
inhibitor therapy that addresses both immune dysfunction and epithelial restoration in UC 
765 
patients.
