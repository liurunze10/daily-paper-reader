ABSTRACT 
26 
Background Macrophages in breast cancer are highly heterogeneous and targeting both 
27 
tissue-resident and bone marrow-derived macrophages represents a high-potential strategy in 
28 
breast cancer immunotherapy. 
29 
Methods Myeloid-specific Egr2 knockout (KO) mice were used to determine roles of 
30 
macrophage Egr2 signaling in tumor progression and anti-tumor immune responses. RNA-seq 
31 
analysis and Flow cytometry were used to examine regulation of Egr2 in tumor-associated 
32 
macrophages (TAMs) and fatty acid-stimulated macrophages. Tumor cell and macrophage 
33 
admixture study was used to evaluate effects of fatty acid educated macrophages on anti-tumor 
34 
responses.   
35 
Results Here we showed that early growth response 2 (Egr2) was highly expressed in 
36 
mammary tissue macrophages and recruited macrophages upon tumor progression. Depletion 
37 
of Egr2 in myeloid cells significantly decreased the immunosuppressive function of polarized 
38 
M2-like macrophages and TAMs. Tumor progression was delayed in myeloid cell Egr2 knockout 
39 
mice, which was associated with enhanced function of effector CD8+ T cells and NK cells. 
40 
Mechanistically, we showed that retinol and nicotinamide metabolism in TAMs were impaired 
41 
after Egr2 depletion.  Further, tumor cell-derived stearic acid (SA) was identified as an important 
42 
fatty acid inducing Egr2 expression. SA stimulation induced macrophage-mediated 
43 
immunosuppression in an Egr2 dependent manner. Addition of oleic acid (OA) uniquely 
44 
repressed SA-induced Egr2 expression and immunosuppressive activity in macrophages.  
45 
Conclusions Our study thus uncovers a novel SA-Egr2 pathway driving macrophage 
46 
immunosuppression and novel molecular mechanism of OA mediated anti-tumor effect.

INTRODUCTION 
67 
  Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) play essential roles at each stage of breast cancer 
68 
progression and metastasis, and targeting TAMs is an attractive approach for cancer 
69 
immunotherapy. However, the current strategies targeting macrophage-associated molecules 
70 
show limited success. Targeting CCL2-CCR2 axis to inhibit macrophage recruitment is largely 
71 
disappointing1-4. These can be explained in part by the fact that TAMs have dual origins and 
72 
distinct functions. Previous studies have mainly focused on monocyte-derived macrophages 
73 
(MDMs) due to the accumulation with an increased tumor burden. The roles of tissue-resident 
74 
macrophages in breast cancer are not yet fully understood4-7. Mammary tissue macrophages

(MTMs) are tissue-resident cells that display M2-like macrophage phenotype4. A recent study 
76 
has demonstrated that MTMs contribute to early breast cancer development8. We postulate that 
77 
targeting both tissue-resident and monocyte-derived macrophages provide a powerful approach 
78 
to reprogram TAMs in breast cancer. 
79 
  Lipid-associated macrophages (LAMs) have been identified in the tumor microenvironment 
80 
of human breast cancer9-11. These LAMs can accumulate lipids and promote immune 
81 
suppression, tumor growth and metastasis in breast cancers. Fatty acids (FAs) from tumor 
82 
synthesis and diet play a dual, complex role in tumor immunity, acting as both pro-tumorigenic 
83 
and anti-tumorigenic effects, which are dependent on the specific FA type and metabolism 
84 
pathways in both cancer and immune cells12-18. However, molecular mechanisms underlying 
85 
fatty acid modulated macrophages and their roles in tumor remain not fully elucidated. 
86 
  Transcription factor early growth response 2 (Egr2) is a zinc finger transcription factor of the 
87 
early growth response gene family. Previous data have demonstrated that Egr2 signaling in 
88 
tumor-infiltrating T cells negatively regulates anti-tumor immunity19-21. Recent studies suggest 
89 
that macrophage Egr2 signaling exhibits pro-tumor effect22,23. However, the function of Egr2 in 
90 
macrophage heterogeneity in mammary gland and breast cancer are largely unexplored. Tumor 
91 
factors inducing Egr2 expression and involvement of Egr2 in fatty acid modulated macrophages 
92 
in tumor remain unknown. In this study, we demonstrate that Egr2 is essential for the 
93 
immunosuppressive function of IL-4 polarized M2-like macrophages and TAMs. Further, tumor 
94 
cell-derived stearic acid (SA) was identified as an important fatty acid inducing Egr2 expression 
95 
and immunosuppression in macrophages. Adding oleic acid (OA) uniquely represses SA-
96 
induced Egr2 expression and immunosuppression. These findings support our hypothesis that 
97 
SA-Egr2 axis is a novel pathway driving macrophage immunosuppression, and OA treatment 
98 
enhances macrophage anti-tumor immunity via inhibiting Egr2 signaling and 
99 
immunosuppression.

RESULTS  
206 
Egr2 expression in macrophages 
207 
Previous studies have shown that Egr2 is a novel M2 macrophage marker and highly expressed 
208 
in IL-4+IL-13 polarized M2 macrophages28. We further analyzed the data from The 
209 
Immunological Genome Project (http://www.immgen.org) and found that Egr2 was highly 
210 
expressed in tissue-resident MTMs, but not in bone marrow macrophages (BMMφ), splenic 
211 
macrophages (SpMφ) and peritoneal macrophages (PecMφ) (figure 1B). Flow cytometry 
212 
analysis confirmed that Egr2 was expressed in MTMs (CD45+F4/80+CD11b+) under steady-state 
213 
conditions (figure 1C). Expression of Egr2 in M2 macrophages and MTMs was remarkably 
214 
downregulated in conditional myeloid cell Egr2 KO (LyzM-cre-Egr2f/f) mice (figure 1A and C). 
215 
Tissue-resident macrophages usually express low levels of CCR2 and MHC II compared to BM 
216 
monocyte-derived macrophages. We examined Egr2 expressions in TAMs from MMTV-PyMT 
217 
transgenic mice and found that Egr2 was expressed in both BM-derived CCR2+MHC IIhigh and 
218 
tissue-resident CCR2-MHC IIlow TAM subsets, but not neutrophils (figure 1D).  
219 
      To demonstrate potential clinical significance, we analyzed Egr2 expression in human 
220 
macrophages from single cell RNA sequencing dataset GSE176078, which identified six 
221 
macrophage subsets from breast cancer patients including two clusters of lipid-associated

DISCUSSION 
384 
Identifying novel immunological targets in tissue-macrophages for controlling early-stage breast 
385 
cancer formation is essential. Targeting both tissue-resident macrophages and recruited 
386 
monocyte-derived macrophages can elicit powerful anti-tumor immunity in breast cancer. Egr2 
387 
signaling in tumor-infiltrating T cells negatively regulates anti-tumor immunity19-21. The current 
388 
study further reveals that Egr2 is expressed in both mammary tissue resident and BM 
389 
monocyte-derived macrophages. Egr2 expression is associated with immunosuppression of 
390 
macrophages. Targeting myeloid cell Egr2 can significantly enhance anti-tumor immunity in 
391 
breast cancer. 
392 
     The tumor microenvironment is rich in a diverse array of fatty acids and other lipids. Lipid-
393 
associated macrophages have been isolated in the tumor microenvironment of mouse tumor 
394 
models and human breast cancer9,10,26. Targeting macrophage lipid metabolism is a promising 
395 
strategy for tumor immunotherapy9,10,12,40. Studies also demonstrate that the type and 
396 
composition of dietary fats can influence tumor growth and the effectiveness of anti-tumor 
397 
immune responses41. A recent study discovered that tumor-derived arachidonic acid (AA) 
398 
reprograms neutrophils to promote immune suppression17. Although previous studies have 
399 
.

shown that Egr2 regulates lipid biosynthesis in peripheral nerve myelination42 and 
400

401 
remains unknown. PPARγ is crucial for fatty acid metabolic reprogramming and fatty acid 
402 
oxidation (FAO) in T cells44,45. The current study demonstrates that IL-4 induced PPARγ 
403 
expression is controlled by Egr2 and responsible for Egr2 mediated M2-like macrophage 
404 
immunosuppressive activity. We further demonstrate a previously unidentified mechanism by 
405 
which tumor-derived SA induces Egr2 expression and immunosuppression in macrophages. 
406 
Egr2 signaling can regulate fatty acid metabolism in TAMs.      
407 
      Obesity is associated with an increased risk of developing and worse prognosis in breast 
408 
cancer patients. It is known that obesity reprograms mammary adipose tissue macrophages that 
409 
support breast cancer formation46. In addition to the tumor microenvironment, SA is also found 
410 
in various foods, such as beef fat, pork fat, cocoa butter, and processed foods. A recent study 
411 
showed that  the source of dietary fat influences anti-tumor immunity in obese mice41. 
412 
Specifically, high-fat diets derived from lard, beef tallow or butter, but not from coconut oil, palm 
413 
oil or olive oil, accelerate tumor growth. SA is significantly higher in butter high-fat diet41. SA 
414 
associated metabolite stearoyl-carnitine (CAR18:0) is increased in the butter enriched high-fat-
415 
diet and directly inhibits CD8 T cell cytotoxicity41. These data strongly suggest that SA is an 
416 
important pro-tumor fatty acid in the tumor microenvironment and high-fat diet induced obese. 
417 
The current study further demonstrates that SA-induced macrophage immunosuppression is 
418 
particularly dependent on Egr2 signaling. We will investigate whether Egr2 signaling contributes 
419 
to pro-tumor effects of obesity reprogrammed macrophages in future studies.  
420 
      OA is the most abundant monounsaturated fatty acid in the human diet and highly enriched 
421 
in olive oil47. Olive oil is a main component of the Mediterranean diet, and consumption of olive 
422 
oil is inversely related to cancer prevalence48. It is generally thought that the beneficial effects of 
423 
olive oil are due to its high proportion of OA, and studies have suggested that OA has potential 
424 
.
