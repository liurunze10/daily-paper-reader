ABSTRACT (149/150 words) 18 

Antigen -presenting cell (APC) interactions with cytotoxic T cells are critical for anti -tumour 19 

immunity and response to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB), yet context -specific regulators 20 

in the tumour microenvironment remain not fully defined. Here we identify the lectin receptor 21 

SIGLEC1 as a key mediator of macrophage –T cell interactions in human melanoma. In a well -22 

characterized patient cohort, SIGLEC1 was selectively upregulated in inflammatory 23 

macrophages physically associated with activate d/ exhausted CD8 ⁺ T cells. Imaging and 24 

functional analyses revealed that SIGLEC1 accumulates at the macrophage –T cell interface 25 

and promotes cell clustering. SIGLEC1 ligands were enriched on activated T cells, and their 26 

blockade reduced cytotoxic cytokine production ex vivo . Single -cell (spatial ) transcriptomics 27 

across independent ICB -treated melanoma cohorts showed that SIGLEC1 ⁺ macrophages 28 

localize near CD8 ⁺ T cells and are enriched in responders, where they also associate with T 29 

cells expressing activatio n/ exhaustion markers. These findings define a SIGLEC1 -dependent 30 

macrophage –T cell niche linked to effective immunotherapy. 31

INTRODUCTION 33 

Immunotherapies, such as immune checkpoint blockade (ICB), have notably improved the 34 

clinical outcome of melanoma , y et, a subset of patients still do es not show benefit 1. Effective 35 

immunotherapy requires the infiltration of tumour s by effec tor immune cells, including cytotoxic 36 

T cells and antigen presenting cells (APCs). Priming of T cells by APCs is essential for the 37 

acquisition of cytotoxic function , enabling their migration into the tumour microenvironment 38 

(TME) where they can recognize cognate cancer antigens and promote tumour cell killing . The 39 

abundance of APC s and T cell s in tumour s is a well -established predictive and prognostic 40 

marker s of ICB response 2–7. However , infiltration alone is insufficient; the phenotypic state, 41 

activation status and spatial distribution of immune cells within the TME are also critical 42 

determinants of therapy efficacy 8,9 .43 

44 

APCs are a heterogeneous group of immune cells comprising macrophages, dendritic cells 45 

(DCs) and B cells, which process and present antige ns to T cells , leading to their activation ,46 

often linked to effective ICB treatments . However, APCs can also act as suppressors of 47 

cytotoxic T cell activity. For example, CXCL9/10 -producing inflammatory macrophages are 48 

associated with positive ICB responses 10 , whereas SPP1+ immunosuppressive macrophages 49 

correlate with poor clinical prognosis 11,12 . Thus, macrophage populations modulate anti tumour 50 

immunity through direct stimulatory or inhibitory interactions with T cells. Prognostically, the 51 

abundance and activation state of infiltrating T cells is as important as APC infiltration 8. Certain 52 

T cell exhausted or dysfunctional states have been linked to improved anti tumour responses, 53 

whereas T cells in these states can also display reduced cytotoxic capacity 8,13,14 . T cell 54 

phenotype s and functionalities are shaped by interactions with neighboring cells , and thereby 55 

highly dependent on their spatial organization within the TME. 56 

57 

Recent advances in spatial transcriptomics have reve aled that cell -cell proximity in tumour s is 58 

a strong predictive marker for immunotherapy. For instance, inflammatory macrophages in 59 

close proximity to T cells correlate with positive ICB response 13 , whereas T cell s near SPP1+ 60 

immunosuppressive macrophages are link ed to unfavorable outcomes in multiple cancer 61 

types 7,12,15 . Furthermore, the se immunosuppressive macrophages can sequester T cells at the 62 

tumour margin, restricting their access to cancer cells 16,17 . These observations underscore the 63 

importance of understanding not only the abundance of APC and T cells , but also how and 64 

where they communicate within the TME. 65 

66

analyze physically interacting cell clusters from melanoma tumours 18 . Unlike earlier studies 68 

that inferred interactions from cell –cell proximity in two -dimensional histological sections, this 69

SIGLEC1 re -localizes to the immune interface between inflammatory macrophages and 108 

CD8+ T cells and facilitates their cluster formation 109 

SIGLEC1 ( also known as CD169) is an immunoglobulin -like lectin receptor expressed on 110 

inflammatory macrophages that binds sialylated cell surface glycans 19,20 . Upon antigen 111 

recognition, APCs and T cells form an immunological synapse, defined by re -localization of 112 

membrane receptors and reduced T cell motility 21 –25 . Given the enrichment of SIGLEC1 in 113 

macrophages engaged with CD8 ⁺ T cells, we examined whether it similarly re -localized to the 114 

synapse to stabilize these interactions. 115 

116 

Monocytes isolated from healthy donor peripheral blood mononuclear cells ( PBMCs ) were 117 

polarized into inflammatory and suppress ive macrophages, or non -polarized; and 118 

subsequently co -cultured with autologous CD8 ⁺ T cells for 1 h our (Fig. 2a ). Localization of 119 

SIGLEC1 was assessed by imaging flow cytometry ( Fig. 2b , Extended Data Fig . 2a , b ). 120 

Consistent with our patient -derived data, inflammatory macrophages exhibited higher baseline 121 

SIGLEC1 expression ( Fig. 2c ). Upon co -culture with CD8 ⁺ T cells, SIGLEC1 in inflammatory 122 

macrophages re -localized to the intercellular interface, whereas no such re -localization was 123 

observed in suppressor or non -polarized macrophages (Fig. 2 d). These findings indicate that 124 

SIGLEC1 is recruited to the immune synapse between inflammatory macrophages and CD8 ⁺125 

T cells, suggesting a potential role in synapse formation and/or maintenance. 126 

127 

Next, we assessed whether SIGLEC1 contributed to maintaining macrophage -T cell 128 

interaction. Macrophage –T cell co -cultures were treated with SIGLEC1 -blocking antibody, and 129 

cluster formation was quantified as a measure of cell –cell interaction. Inflammatory 130 

macrophages formed the highest proportion of clusters, confirming our patient -derived 131 

observations (Fig. 2e, f ). Upon SIGLEC1 blocking, macrophage -T cell clusters were reduced 132 

for inflammatory macrophages , but no significan t difference s were detected in suppressor or 133 

non -polarized macrophages, likely due to their low baseline interaction frequencies (Fig. 2e, 134 

f). These results indicate that SIGLEC1 promotes stable contacts between inflammatory 135 

macrophages and CD8 ⁺ T cells .136

SIGLEC1 has been primarily studied for its role in antigen capture and presentation, 219 

particularly in lymphoid tissues, and has been leveraged for antigen -targeting vaccine 220 

strategies 26,27 . However, its function in mediating direct APC –T cell interactions within tumour s221 

has remained poorly defined. Given its extended extracellular domain and affinity for sialylated 222 

glycans, SIGLEC1 is well suited to mediate glycan -dependent adhesion, potentially prolonging 223 

cell interactions 28,29 .224 

225 

We found that SIGLEC1 ligand expression was associated with T cell activation /exhaustion ,226 

defined by cell surface markers including CD137 and PD -1. T hese data suggest that sialylation 227 

patterns on activated T cells may promote engagement with SIGLEC1 ⁺ macrophages, 228 

reinforcing physical interactions within immune niches. Such interactions may facilitate 229 

sustained antigen presentation but could also contribute to chronic stimulation and the 230 

emergence of exhausted T cell states. This is in line with recent findings that show that the 231 

sialylation profile of T cells can determine their phenotype and functions 30 .232 

233 

Functional perturbation experiments further highlight the complexity of the SIGLEC1 –ligand 234 

axis. Blockade of SIGLEC1 ligands, but not SIGLEC1 itself, reduced TNF and IFNγ production 235 

by CD8 ⁺ T cells ex vivo . One interpretation is that SIGLEC1 ligands exert functions beyond 236 

binding SIGLEC1, potentially engaging additional glycan -binding receptors and influencing 237 

anti -tumour T cell functions 31 . Alternatively, SIGLEC1 blockade may interfere with other 238 

functions such as antigen uptake by macrophages, indirectly dampening T cell effector 239 

function 32,33 . Of note, we experimentally blocked a specific glycosylated motif by the use of a 240 

recombinant SIGLEC1 protein, but it is still not fully described which molecules carry this motif. 241 

The specific identity of the SIGLEC1 ligands remains unknown, and defining these ligands will 242 

be critical for understanding how glycan -mediated interactions regulate T cell responses in 243 

tumour s. 244

macrophages to favorable outcomes in other malignancies, including endometrial and 249

Although our analyze s focused on macrophages, SIGLEC1 expression on other APC subsets, 256

In summary, our study identifies the SIGLEC1 –ligand axis as a regulator of macrophage –T266 

cell interactions that coordinates spatial immune organization, T cell activation and exhaustion, 267 

and clinical response to ICB . By integrating single -cell, spatial, and functional analyze s, we 268 

reveal how glycan -mediated interactions contribute to the formation of immune niches that 269 

support anti tumour immunity while we may still need checkpoint -mediated reinvigoration. 270 

These findings highlight SIGLEC1 and its ligands as potential biomarkers and modulators of 271 

immunotherapy responsiveness in cancer , which merit further investigation .272

Spatial transcriptomic analysis 364 

We performed spatial transcriptomic analysis as described earlier ( Coccimiglio, Ibáñez -Molero 365 

et al., in revisions ). Briefly , we analyzed single -cell spatial transcriptomics data from archived 366 

FFPE tumour tissues of four patients with stage III melanoma treated with neoadjuvant 367 

immune checkpoint blockade at Amsterdam UMC. Two responders (with major pathological 368 

response) and two non -responders were selected. Tissue sections were processed using the 369 

Xenium platform (10x Genomics), including RNA extraction, probe hybridization, imaging, and 370 

signal decoding to generate spatial gene expression data. Data were analyzed in R using 371 

Seurat, with quality filtering, integration, clustering, and manual cell -type annotation based on 372 

lineage markers. Low -quality and mixed clusters were excluded, and spatial re -clustering was 373 

performed where necessary. 374
