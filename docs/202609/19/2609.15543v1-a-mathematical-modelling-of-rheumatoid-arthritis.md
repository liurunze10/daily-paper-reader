## 摘要
我们开发了一个数学模型来描述关节炎发生和消退过程中的炎症动力学，重点关注关节组织内三种关键免疫细胞群之间的相互作用，即中性粒细胞 (N)、滑膜下层组织驻留巨噬细胞 (SL-TRM, T) 以及被招募到炎症关节并分化为单核细胞衍生巨噬细胞 (MoMac, M) 的循环单核细胞。代表对外部致关节炎刺激反应的炎症信号 (S) 驱动中性粒细胞和 MoMac 的招募与激活，而 SL-TRM 则发挥调节和保护作用。我们提出了一个关节内急性炎症反应的极小常微分方程模型，该模型将炎症信号 S 与三种细胞群 (N, T, M) 耦合，并纳入了招募、刺激、非线性自我限制和相互调节机制。我们证明了平衡结构取决于三个无量纲参数 A、B、C，并推导出了表征非平凡稳态的三次方程。我们对平衡点及其稳定性进行了分析研究，并确定了 A = 1 处的交叉分岔，将无病状态与持续炎症状态区分开来。数值模拟说明了各种情景下的典型瞬态动力学，并展示了该模型捕捉从急性炎症到慢性炎症的转变以及在特定条件下炎症消退的能力。总之，我们的模型提供了一个探索急性与持续性关节炎症潜在机制的框架，并为基于实验数据的参数估计以及未来评估治疗策略的扩展奠定了基础。

## Abstract
We developed a mathematical model to describe the inflammatory dynamics during arthritis development and resolution, focusing on the interaction between three key immune cell populations within the joint tissue, namely neutrophils (N), sublining synovial tissue-resident macrophages (SL-TRM, T), and circulating monocytes that are recruited into the inflamed joint and differentiate into monocyte-derived macrophages (MoMac, M). An inflammatory signal (S), representing the response to an external arthritogenic stimulus, drives the recruitment and activation of neutrophils and MoMac, while SL-TRM exert a regulatory and protective role. We propose a minimal ordinary differential equation model of acute inflammatory response into a joint that couples an inflammatory signal S to the three cell populations (N, T, M) and incorporates recruitment, stimulation, nonlinear self-limitation and mutual regulation. We show that the equilibrium structure depends on three adimensional parameters A, B, C and derive a cubic equation characterizing nontrivial steady states. We perform an analytical study of equilibria and their stability, and identify a bifurcation at A = 1 separating a disease-free regime from a sustained inflammatory state. Numerical simulations illustrate typical transient dynamics under various scenarios and show the ability of the model to capture the transition from acute to chronic inflammation, as well as the resolution of inflammation under certain conditions. In conclusion, our model provides a framework to explore mechanisms underlying acute versus sustained joint inflammation and offers a basis for parameter estimation from experimental data, as well as future extensions toward evaluation of therapeutic strategies.
