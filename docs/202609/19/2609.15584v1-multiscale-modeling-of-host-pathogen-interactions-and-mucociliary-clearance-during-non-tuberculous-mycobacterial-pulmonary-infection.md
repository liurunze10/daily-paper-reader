## 摘要
非结核分枝杆菌（NTM）感染是囊性纤维化（CF）面临的临床挑战，尽管存在宿主免疫反应，但受损的黏液纤毛清除功能和改变的黏液流变学仍会促进细菌定植。了解细菌生长、免疫细胞动力学和黏液转运如何调节感染进展具有挑战性，因为这些过程在空间和时间尺度上相互作用。我们开发了一个计算框架，将NTM感染的机制性基于代理的模型（ABM）与空间分辨的偏微分方程（PDE）模型相结合。该PDE模型在代表黏液和肺组织的双室几何结构中，耦合了细菌增殖、巨噬细胞趋化、免疫介导的清除、黏液降解和粘弹性转运。参数使用已建立的ABM数据进行校准，在保留细胞机制的同时产生了一种高效的连续介质表示。该PDE模型重现了细菌和巨噬细胞的动力学，并能够分析与黏液相关的机制和疗法。敏感性分析确定了黏液粘度、细菌扩散率和巨噬细胞迁移率是通过黏液纤毛清除和组织定植影响细菌持久性的关键调节因子。模拟揭示了黏液溶解疗法的非线性效应：增强的清除能力减少了黏液中的细菌负荷，而过度的粘度降低可能会促进细菌向肺组织迁移，这支持了将其与抗菌治疗相结合。该框架为研究肺部感染、评估疗法和开发患者特异性数字孪生提供了定量平台。

## Abstract
Non-tuberculous mycobacterial (NTM) infections are a clinical challenge in cystic fibrosis (CF), where impaired mucociliary clearance and altered mucus rheology promote bacterial colonization despite host immune responses. Understanding how bacterial growth, immune cell dynamics, and mucus transport regulate infection progression is difficult because these processes interact across spatial and temporal scales. We develop a computational framework bridging a mechanistic agent-based model (ABM) of NTM infection with a spatially resolved partial differential equation (PDE) model. The PDE model couples bacterial proliferation, macrophage chemotaxis, immune-mediated clearance, mucus degradation, and viscoelastic transport in a two-compartment geometry representing mucus and lung tissue. Parameters are calibrated using data from the established ABM, yielding an efficient continuum representation while preserving cellular mechanisms.   The PDE model reproduces bacterial and macrophage dynamics and enables analyses of mucus-related mechanisms and therapies. Sensitivity analysis identifies mucus viscosity, bacterial diffusivity, and macrophage mobility as key regulators of bacterial persistence through mucociliary clearance and tissue colonization. Simulations reveal nonlinear effects of mucolytic therapies: enhanced clearance reduces bacterial burden in mucus, whereas excessive viscosity reduction may promote migration into lung tissue, supporting combination with antibacterial treatment. This framework provides a quantitative platform for studying pulmonary infections, evaluating therapies, and developing patient-specific digital twins.
