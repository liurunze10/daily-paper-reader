Abstract 32 

Bacterial effector proteins manipulate host signalling cascades, including immune 33 

responses, to facilitate infection. While most effectors of Gram-negative bacteria rely 34 

on a secretion system for intracellular delivery, some possess intrinsic cell-penetrating 35 

capabilities. Here, we characterize the Shigella flexneri LPX effector IpaH7.8, which 36 

combines autonomous cell entry with enzymatic modulation of immunomodulatory host 37 

signaling pathways through distinct structural domains. We show that recombinant 38 

IpaH7.8 (rIpaH7.8) enters human cells independent of Shigella ´s type III secretion 39 

system (T3SS) via lipid raft-mediated endocytosis and escapes the endosome through 40 

a conserved N-terminal domain composed of two  α-helices. In the cytosol, the C-41 

terminal E3 ubiquitin ligase domain of the cell-penetrating effector protein targets the 42 

pore-forming protein gasdermin D (GSDMD), suppressing inflammasome-induced IL-43 

1β release. Beyond inflammasome inhibition, integrated transcriptomic and kinome 44 

profiling in primary human monocytes revealed that IpaH7.8 induces a coordinated 45 

reprogramming of host signaling networks. Cluster-resolved gene expression analysis 46 

demonstrated selective suppression of immune effector pathways alongside induction 47 

of regulatory programs and interference with vesicular trafficking. These transcriptional 48 

changes converged with kinase activity remodeling, characterized by attenuation of 49 

PKC- and PKA-dependent signaling pathways. Notably, both datasets identified the 50 

NOX2 complex as a central target of IpaH7.8 activity. The NOX2 subunit NCF1 was 51 

downregulated at the transcriptional level and showed reduced phosphorylation at 52 

regulatory sites, indicating impaired activation. Consistently, IpaH7.8 significantly 53 

reduced reactive oxygen species production in primary human monocytes, 54 

demonstrating functional suppression of oxidative burst responses. 55

Author Summary 69 

Bacterial pathogens like Shigella flexneri manipulate host immune responses to 70 

survive and spread within human cells. The Shigella effector protein IpaH7.8 is known 71 

to block inflammatory cell death by targeting gasdermin D. Here, we show that IpaH7.8 72 

can enter human cells without a bacterial secretion system. It uses a specialized 73 

protein domain to cross the membrane and reach the cytoplasm. Once inside, IpaH7.8 74 

alters host cell signaling by both attaching ubiquitin to immune proteins and 75 

reprogramming phosphorylation pathways. This dual function allows Shigella to 76 

suppress inflammation and promotes its escape from immune defenses. Our findings 77 

reveal how IpaH7.8 combines cell entry, immune evasion, and cytoskeletal control in 78 

a single protein, and highlight its potential as a tool to modulate inflammation in disease 79 

contexts. 80

# Introduction 81 

Shigella flexneri is a Gram-negative, facultative intracellular pathogen and a major 82 

cause of bacillary dysentery (shigellosis), a severe intestinal infection characterized by 83 

inflammation, abdominal pain, diarrhea, and fever. Shigellosis accounts for over 270 84 

million infections worldwide annually, disproportionately affecting children in low-85 

income regions with limited access to clean water and sanitation [1,2]. The primary 86 

mode of transmission is through the ingestion of contaminated food or water, resulting 87 

in the colonization and invasion of the intestinal epithelium. After ingestion, S. flexneri 88 

translocates through specialized microfold (M) cells in the intestinal epithelium, where 89 

it is phagocytosed by macrophages and dendritic cells. Upon uptake, the bacterium 90 

rapidly escapes from the bacteria-containing vacuole (BCV) into the cytosol, where it 91 

replicates and directly interacts with the host immune system [1,2]. To evade host 92 

immune responses and establish an intracellular replicative niche, S. flexneri utilizes a 93 

type III secretion system (T3SS) to inject bacterial effector proteins into host cells, 94 

modulating immune signaling and cell death pathways [3-6]. Among these effectors, 95 

the IpaH proteins, a unique class of E3 ubiquitin ligases classified as LPX effectors 96 

(harboring a Leu-Pro-X motif), play a crucial role in subverting the host’s innate 97 

immune defenses by targeting key regulators of immune signaling, pyroptosis, and 98 

inflammasome activation [7]. Pyroptosis is a pro-inflammatory form of regulated cell 99 

death that serves as a crucial immune defense mechanism against intracellular 100 

bacterial infections. This process is primarily mediated by the gasdermin family of pore-101 

forming proteins, particularly gasdermin D (GSDMD), which, upon activation, 102 

oligomerizes and forms membrane pores, leading to host cell lysis and the release of 103 

inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) and interleukin -18 (IL-18) 104 

[4,8]. The Shigella effector IpaH7.8 ubiquitinates and targets GSDMD for proteasomal 105 

degradation, thereby inhibiting pyroptosis and enabling bacterial survival in the cytosol 106

GSDMD but not murine GSDMD due to structural differences. This specificity may 108 

contribute to the restricted host range of S. flexneri , which primarily infects humans 109 

and non-human primates but not mice [9]. Additionally, IpaH7.8 targets gasdermin B 110 

(GSDMB), another pore-forming cytolysin, preventing granzyme A-mediated activation 111 

in epithelial cells and thereby protecting Shigella from the bactericidal effects of natural 112 

killer cells [10,11]. Beyond gasdermin regulation, IpaH7.8 has also been implicated in 113 

inflammasome modulation through its interaction with glomulin (GLMN), a negative 114 

regulator of inflammasome activation. The degradation of GLMN by IpaH7.8 leads to 115 

the activation of NLRP3 and NLRC4 inflammasomes, promoting caspase-1-dependent 116 

pyroptosis in murine macrophages [3,4]. Additionally, IpaH7.8 triggers proteasome-117 

mediated degradation of the N-terminal domain of murine NLRP1B, releasing a C-118 

terminal fragment that is a potent activator of caspase-1 [12]. However, both NLRP1B 119 

and GLMN have thus far been identified only as targets of IpaH7.8 in murine cells, and 120 

their relevance in human cells remains unclear. In addition to its role in immune 121 

evasion, the Shigella effector protein IpaH7.8 has recently been shown to also regulate 122 

Rab13, promoting its retention on the membrane remnants of the Shigella -containing 123 

vacuole (SCV) [13]. This process facilitates efficient uncoating of SCV membranes, 124 

which is likely essential for IpaH7.8-dependent bacterial escape and successful 125 

intercellular spread [2]. The interaction between IpaH7.8 and Rab13 appears to be 126 

unique among the IpaH family members, with no functional redundancy observed for 127 

other bacterial effectors [13]. Despite extensive research, the precise molecular 128 

function and role of IpaH7.8 in different host cell types, as well as its broader 129 

implications for Shigella pathogenesis, warrants further investigation. Moreover, the 130 

precise mechanisms underlying their translocation and functional impact on host cell 131 

signaling remain unresolved. Our previous studies demonstrated that LPX effectors 132

endocytosis and exhibits pH-dependent membrane interaction [21,22]. Similarly, LPX 150

acidic conditions, suggesting that they function as dynamic, pH-sensitive modules 152 

facilitating cellular entry (Fig 1C). However, particularly for IpaH7.8, the molecular 153 

basis and functional consequences of this N-terminal domain mediating autonomous 154 

entry remain largely unresolved. To address this, the present study focuses on the 155 

Shigella effector IpaH7.8. We systematically characterize its T3SS-independent 156 

translocation into different host cell types using deletion constructs and recombinant 157

rIpaH7.8 is a cell-penetrating effector protein with a functional N-terminal protein 172 

transduction domain for cytosolic transport. 173 

To analyze the T3SS-independent uptake of the recombinant LPX effector protein, 174 

microscopy studies and a flow cytometry-based assay were performed. The latter used 175 

fluorescent dye-conjugated recombinant IpaH7.8 (rIpaH7.8), as previously established 176 

[14,15]. Upon incubation of HeLa cells with FITC-labeled rIpaH7.8, confocal 177 

microscopy visualized rIpaH7.8 within the cytosol of HeLa cells and revealed a 178 

predominant perinuclear accumulation pattern, suggesting endocytic uptake followed 179 

by intracellular redistribution (Fig 2A and S1B Fig). To specifically quantify intracellular 180 

fluorescence, trypan blue was employed to quench extracellular- and surface-181 

associated signals [16]. In this regard, a pronounced dose- and time-dependent 182 

increase in intracellular fluorescence was detected by fluorescence-based flow 183 

cytometry, comparable to that observed for the cell-penetrating effectors (CPEs) 184 

rYopM and rIpaH9.8 (Fig 2C, S1A Fig and S1C-D) [14,17]. Similar results were 185 

obtained in HEK293T epithelial cells and THP-1-derived macrophages, confirming that 186 

this T3SS-independent internalization mechanism is not restricted to a specific cell 187 

type (S1E-J Fig). To define the structural determinants responsible for cellular 188 

translocation, we next examined uptake of rIpaH7.8 deletion constructs (Fig 2B). The 189 

C-terminal truncation mutant (ΔC), which lacks E3 ubiquitin ligase (NEL) activity, was 190 

efficiently internalized, whereas the N-terminal deletion variant (ΔN) displayed 191 

markedly reduced uptake (Fig 2A and 2C, S1H Fig and S1J). These results identify the 192 

N-terminal α -helical region of IpaH7.8 as a functional protein transduction domain 193 

(PTD), as demonstrated for other CPEs of the LPX family [14,18,19]. To test whether 194 

this region alone can transport heterologous cargo, we generated a fusion construct of 195

Like other LPX effector proteins, rIpaH7.8 harbors a conserved C-terminal novel E3 229 

ubiquitin ligase (NEL) domain that mediates substrate polyubiquitination [25,26], by 230 

catalyzing the attachment of ubiquitin to a target protein’s lysine residue via an 231 

isopeptide bond with ubiquitin’s C-terminal glycine (Fig 3A; [27]). To confirm that this 232 

domain is enzymatically active in recombinant rIpaH7.8, we performed in vitro 233 

ubiquitination assays using purified proteins. The full-length rIpaH7.8 and its N-terminal 234 

deletion variant (ΔN) catalyzed the formation of polyubiquitin chains, whereas the 235 

catalytic C-terminal deletion mutant (ΔC) lacked this activity (Fig 3B), showing that the 236 

NEL domain of rIpaH7.8 is functional. Pull-down assays using THP-1 macrophage 237 

lysates further revealed that both GLMN, a known regulatory component of SCF-type 238 

E3 complexes [3], and Gasdermin D (GSDMD), a key executor of pyroptosis [9], 239 

physically interact with rIpaH7.8 independent of its catalytic activity (Fig 3C). FLAG-240 

tagged recombinant GFP was used as a negative control and didn’t show this 241 

interaction. To determine whether cytosolic rIpaH7.8 retains its ubiquitin ligase activity 242 

after T3SS-independent entry, we incubated THP-1 cells with rIpaH7.8 or its 243 

catalytically inactive ΔC variant and analyzed ubiquitinated targets by tandem 244

IpaH7.8, such as inflammasome rewiring, cytokine release, but also vacuolar escape 274 

and cell-to-cell spread [2,3,9,12,13], an unbiased transcriptomic profiling was 275

Discussion 549 

In this study, we demonstrate that the Shigella effector IpaH7.8 possesses an intrinsic, 550 

T3SS-independent capacity to enter eukaryotic cells , expanding the functional 551 

repertoire of cell-penetrating LPX effectors. Recombinant IpaH7.8 (rIpaH7.8) entered 552 

host cells via macropinocytosis and lipid raft-dependent endocytosis, in a temperature-553 

dependent manner consistent with an energy-requiring process (Fig 2, S1 Fig and S2). 554 

Chemical inhibition of macropinocytosis and actin dynamics significantly reduced 555 

uptake, highlighting the contribution of cytoskeletal remodeling. Similar to other LPX 556 

effectors (IpaH9.8, SspH1, YopM) [14,17,24,32], the N-terminal  α-helical region of 557 

IpaH7.8 functions as a protein transduction domain (PTD) enabling autonomous 558 

internalization. Following uptake, rIpaH7.8 localized to endo-lysosomal compartments 559 

but also reached the cytosol, as indicated by pH-sensitive fluorophore quenching and 560 

by polyubiquitination of cytosolic GSDMD, which required the intact NEL domain (Fig 561 

2 and Fig 3). This confirms that a fraction of internalized protein escapes endosomal 562 

degradation. While the precise escape mechanism remains unknown, the PTD exhibits 563 

pH-dependent modulation of net charge, becoming slightly more positive in acidic 564 

compartments (Fig 1C), a feature shared with other pH-sensitive anionic CPPs such 565 

as Sap(E) [33]. As described for Sap(E) and other negatively charged CPPs, uptake 566 

appears to rely on a multifactorial mechanism combining endocytosis, 567 

macropinocytosis, and local membrane interactions [34,35]. These similarities suggest 568 

that LPX PTDs form a distinct class of anionic, pH-responsive CPEs optimized for 569 

cytosolic delivery during infection. Whether T3SS-independent uptake contributes to 570 

Shigella virulence in vivo remains unresolved. Nonetheless, CPE-mediated delivery 571 

could theoretically enable effectors to act on bystander or distal host cells not directly 572 

contacted by bacteria, an advantage unavailable to classical T3SS injection. Using this 573

In conclusion, this study reveals that IpaH7.8 is a dual-function effector that 704 

combines autonomous cell entry with catalytic interference in host ubiquitin and 705 

phosphorylation networks. It suppresses pyroptotic signaling through direct 706 

ubiquitination of GSDMD, counteracts LPS-induced kinase responses, reprograms 707 

cytoskeletal signaling by inhibiting PKA-dependent pathways, and reduces NOX2-708 

mediated ROS production. These combined activities may facilitate Shigella flexneri 709 

vacuolar escape and intercellular spread while attenuating host inflammatory 710 

responses. The anti-inflammatory properties of rIpaH7.8, particularly its ability to 711 

suppress IL-1β release via GSDMD ubiquitination, highlight its potential for therapeutic 712 

exploitation. Recombinant IpaH7.8 or engineered PTD-fusion proteins could serve as 713 

targeted immunosuppressive tools in diseases characterized by excessive pyroptosis 714 

or IL-1-driven inflammation, such as autoinflammatory syndromes (e.g., familial 715 

Mediterranean fever), sepsis, or inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). However, the 716 

enzyme’s broad substrate spectrum and its ability to rewire host signaling networks 717 

also pose potential risks. Off-target ubiquitination or suppression of protective immune 718 

responses could increase susceptibility to infection or oncogenic transformation. 719 

Therefore, any therapeutic application would require precise control of delivery, 720 

activity, and tissue specificity. Beyond the direct translational potential, a better 721 

understanding of how microbial effectors such as IpaH7.8 manipulate host signaling 722 

may reveal an extensive natural repertoire of cell-penetrating immunomodulators. This 723 

shift in perspective - from viewing effectors solely as virulence factors to recognizing 724 

them as molecular tools - was previously articulated as the ‘drugs from bugs’ concept 725 

[59-62]. 726
