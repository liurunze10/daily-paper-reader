Abstract 20 

Peripheral blood (PB) is the predominant source of regulatory T cells (Tregs) for clinical applications, yet 21 PB Tregs have not been systematically compared with tissue Tregs and their defining properties remain 22 poorly understood. Here we identify a circulation-biased PB Treg population with reduced self-reactivity. 23 Although PB Tregs are less suppressive in vitro, they are hypersensitive to the danger-associated 24 nucleotides ATP and NAD +, linked to a high ARTC2.2/CD38 ratio. ATP/NAD +-triggered apoptotic 25 conversion of PB Tregs promotes macrophage efferocytosis and selectively dampens IFN-γ–driven 26 inflammatory polarization while preserving IL-10–associated programs and enhancing the scavenger 27 receptor Mertk, suggesting that efferocytosis-mediated macrophage reprogramming contributes to lesion 28 control. Consistent with this model, in atherosclerosis-prone mice lacking T cells, PB Treg transfer 29 effectively limits plaque growth and necrotic core formation. We identify an ATP/NAD +-hypersensitive 30 PB Treg fraction in both mice and humans and provide evidence consistent with preferential efferocytosis 31 of this subset within atherosclerotic plaques. 32

Introduction 36 

Regulatory T cells (Tregs) enforce immune tolerance and are increasingly pursued as cell therapies because 37 they can impose more antigen-selective immunosuppression than conventional immunosuppressive 38 drugs.(Sakaguchi et al , 2020; Tang & Bluestone, 2013) In most clinical settings, peripheral blood (PB) is 39 the dominant source of Tregs—either used directly or after ex vivo expansion—because it is readily 40 accessible. Despite this translational importance, PB Tregs have rarely been subjected to the kind of 41 systematic, cross-tissue comparisons that have illuminated specialized Treg programs in various other sites 42 including adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, and the central nervous system.(Burzyn et al , 2013; Feuerer et al ,43 2009; Ito et al , 2019) In humans, matched sampling of tissue Tregs at high quality is difficult; in mice, 44 blood Tregs have often been treated simply as cells in transit. Consequently, the defining properties and 45 physiological roles of blood-resident Tregs remain incompletely understood. 46 Tregs are distinguished from conventional CD4 T cells (Tconv) in multiple ways. In the thymus, 47 relatively strong recognition of self-antigen favors commitment to the Foxp3 + lineage, enriching the 48 peripheral Treg pool for self-reactive TCRs.(Hsieh et al , 2006; Jordan et al , 2001; Josefowicz et al , 2012; 49 Sekiya et al , 2013) Consistent with this concept, peripheral Tregs typically show higher tonic TCR 50 signaling than Tconv cells, a feature that contributes to immune homeostasis and autoimmunity 51 prevention.(Levine et al , 2014; Moran et al , 2011) Another reported hallmark—particularly in mice—is 52 heightened sensitivity to danger-associated nucleotides, most notably ATP and NAD +, which can provoke 53 P2X7R-dependent apoptotic death of Tregs.(Aswad et al , 2005; Hubert et al , 2010) This vulnerability has 54 been exploited experimentally to transiently deplete Tregs and enhance antitumor immunity, but its 55 physiological or disease-relevant meaning remains unclear.(Hubert et al. , 2010) 56 57 Tregs also constrain atherosclerosis, an inflammatory disease driven by both innate and adaptive immune 58 responses.(Hansson & Hermansson, 2011; Witztum & Lichtman, 2014) Macrophages play a particularly 59 central role in atherogenesis. IFN-γ–polarized inflammatory macrophages promote plaque growth and 60 destabilization by producing pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines (e.g., TNF-α, IL-1 β, and MCP-61 1/CCL2) and by inducing tissue-injurious mediators such as matrix metalloproteinases and iNOS, 62 whereas macrophages polarized by IL-10, TGF-β, or IL-13 are thought to exert reparative and anti-63 inflammatory functions through expression of scavenger receptors (e.g., CD163, CD206, and Mertk) and 64 anti-inflammatory mediators such as IL-10 and TGF-β, thereby contributing to plaque stabilization and 65 limiting lesion progression.(Chinetti-Gbaguidi et al , 2015; Moore et al , 2013; Ouyang & Liu, 2024) In 66 mouse models, Treg depletion accelerates plaque progression, whereas adoptive transfer of Tregs is 67 broadly protective.(Ait-Oufella et al , 2006; Klingenberg et al , 2013; Mor et al , 2007; Sharma et al , 2020) 68

4Proposed mechanisms include IL-10– and TGF-β–dependent modulation of macrophage polarization, IL-69 13–mediated enhancement of efferocytosis of dying cells, and suppression of pro-atherogenic T cell 70 responses, including Th1 differentiation and reactivity to specific antigens such as ApoB100-derived 71 antigens.(Herbin et al , 2012; Mallat et al , 2007; Ouyang & Liu, 2024; Proto et al , 2018) In addition, 72 Treg-derived exosomes have been reported to act directly on macrophages, suppressing expression of 73 M1-associated markers such as TNF-α and iNOS while inducing M2-associated markers including Arg1 74 and TGF-β, thereby conferring protection in murine models of acute myocardial infarction. (Hu et al ,75 2020) These observations suggest that Tregs contain molecular constituents capable of directly 76 reprogramming macrophage inflammatory states. However, the complete mechanistic repertoire of 77 atheroprotective Tregs is not yet fully defined. Of note, multiple clinical studies have reported a reduction 78 in circulating FOXP3 + Tregs in patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.(Wigren et al , 2012; 79 Yazdani et al , 2016) Comparable decreases in Treg abundance have also been observed during 80 atherogenesis in ApoE- and LDLR-deficient mice.(Maganto-Garcia et al , 2011; Mor et al. , 2007) 81 However, it remains unclear whether a discrete Treg subset is preferentially diminished in the context of 82 atherosclerosis and, if so, what the biological consequences of such selective loss are. 83 84 Here we focus on PB Tregs. We identify a circulation-biased Treg population that is enriched for low self-85 reactivity and exhibits attenuated suppressive function, yet displays pronounced nucleotide hypersensitivity. 86 We further show that ATP/NAD +-triggered apoptotic PB Tregs are efferocytosed by macrophages, which 87 in turn selectively dampen inflammatory polarization. In an atherosclerosis model, PB Tregs traffic 88 efficiently to plaques and suppress plaque development via an ARTC2.2-dependent program, supporting 89 nucleotide-driven Treg apoptosis and macrophage efferocytosis as a previously underappreciated 90 immunoregulatory axis. 91

PB Tregs are enriched for low ongoing TCR signaling and low self-reactivity 94 

We first surveyed eight tissues and confirmed that Foxp3 + Tregs can be readily detected in PB, although 95 they comprise a smaller fraction of CD4 + T cells than in most tissues ( Supplementary Fig. S1A, S1B ). We 96 then performed ATAC-seq on naïve CD4 T cells and Tregs from six tissues, including blood. Principal 97 component analysis revealed that PB Tregs segregate from other tissue Tregs and occupy an intermediate 98 position between naïve T cells and Tregs along PC1 ( Fig. 1A ), suggesting a distinct epigenomic state. 99 Because Tregs are typically sustained by ongoing self-antigen recognition, we next compared tonic T cell 100 receptor (TCR) signaling across tissues. We used a recently generated Nr4a1 (Nur77) reporter mouse 101 (Nr4a1-dsGFP mice) in which a destabilized GFP (half-life of a few hours) reports recent TCR signaling, 102 enabling detection of ongoing stimulation in situ.(Sekiya et al , 2024) Strikingly, the fraction of Nr4a1-103 dsGFP + Tregs was markedly lower in blood than in other tissues ( Fig. 1B, 1C ). In contrast, in conventional 104 Nur77 reporter mice (Nur77-GFP mice), in which EGFP is used as a reporter molecule and is relatively 105 stable (half-life >24 h),(Moran et al. , 2011) a non-trivial fraction of PB Tregs remained GFP + (Fig. 1B, 1C ). 106 These results collectively suggest that PB Tregs are composed of a mixture of long-lived blood-biased 107 Tregs and recently emigrated tissue Tregs. 108 To test whether PB Tregs merely recirculate or preferentially persist in blood, we adoptively transferred 109 total CD4 T cells (CD45.2, 1 ×10 6 ) together with a small number of purified Tregs (CD45.1, 1 ×10 4 ) from 110 either lymph nodes or blood into Rag2 −/− recipients. One week later, PB-derived Tregs showed significantly 111 greater enrichment in the circulation relative to LN-derived Tregs ( Fig. 1D ), supporting the presence of a 112 circulation-biased Treg population. 113 We next assessed self-reactivity of PB Tregs at the TCR level employing BALB/c mice that express 114 endogenous superantigens encoded by Mtv-8 and Mtv-9, that react with specific subsets of V b chains (e.g., 115 Vb5, V b11, and V b12). Superantigen-reactive V β usage is known to be typically enriched among Tregs 116 relative to Tconv cells.(Tanaka et al , 2010) In this experiment, superantigen-reactive V β usage has been 117 estimated as a surrogate for heightened self-antigen recognition. Using the Nr4a1-dsGFP mice, it was 118 validated that tissue Tregs with superantigen-reactive V β subsets indeed display stronger tonic TCR 119 signaling ( Supplementary Fig. S1C, S1D ). As shown in Fig. 1E , we found that superantigen-reactive V β120 subsets were under-represented among PB Tregs, whereas non-reactive V β subsets were comparable across 121 tissues. This tissue bias was not observed in C57BL/6J mice, which lack I-E and thus cannot present the 122 relevant superantigens ( Supplementary Fig. S1E ). 123

During assay development we noticed that PB Tregs differed dramatically depending on how red blood 138 cells (RBC) were removed. Compared with density gradient separation, hypotonic RBC lysis induced 139 robust apoptosis in PB Tregs, as indicated by phosphatidylserine exposure ( Fig. 2A, 2B ). This effect did 140 not seem to be caused by an artificial toxicity of RBC lysis, because PB Tconv cells as well as the majority 141 of cells in PB were far more resistant to the treatment ( Fig. 2A-2D ). In addition, RBC removal from PB 142 was performed by density-gradient separation throughout this study, unless otherwise indicated. 143 Next, to elucidate the mechanism underlying the Treg-selective induction of apoptosis by RBC lysis 144 procedure, we focused on the known property that Tregs are more sensitive than Tconv cells to stimulation 145 by the danger-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) nucleotides ATP and NAD +.(Aswad et al. , 2005; 146 Hubert et al. , 2010) Both ATP and NAD + are released predominantly from dying cells in inflamed tissues 147 and function as danger-associated signals. We therefore performed the RBC lysis in the presence of the 148 P2X7R inhibitor A438079, which blocks ATP-driven signaling, and a neutralizing antibody against 149 ARTC2.2, a key mediator of NAD +-dependent signaling. Under these conditions, both reagents markedly 150 suppressed apoptosis in Tregs, indicating that the hemolysis-induced apoptosis of Tregs is driven by ATP 151 and/or NAD + released from RBCs ( Fig. 2E, 2F ). 152 To define the relative contribution of ATP and NAD + to Treg apoptosis, we next performed in vitro 153 stimulation assays using Tregs prepared by density gradient separation. Low tonic TCR signaling associates with a high ARTC2.2/CD38 ratio and heightened nucleotide 166 

sensitivity of PB Tregs 167 

Next, we investigated the mechanism underlying the heightened sensitivity of PB Tregs to ATP/NAD +.168 Given our observation that PB Tregs experience weaker self-antigen–driven stimulation, we asked whether 169 tonic TCR signaling modulates ATP/NAD + responsiveness. To this end, LN Tregs from Nur77-GFP 170 reporter mice were stratified into four equal fractions based on GFP intensity and challenged with 171 ATP/NAD +. Strikingly, GFP intensity inversely correlated with ATP/NAD + sensitivity ( Fig. 3A–3C ), 172 strongly suggesting that tonic TCR signaling dampens nucleotide-induced susceptibility in Tregs. In 173 addition, Nur77-GFP intensity in Tregs was not altered by ATP/NAD + treatment ( Fig. 3D ), showing that 174 the reporter signal reflects antecedent TCR stimulation rather than an acute response to nucleotide exposure. 175 Given the inverse correlation between tonic TCR signal strength and ATP/NAD + sensitivity, we next sought 176 to define the underlying molecular mechanism. Because NAD + is the dominant trigger of Treg apoptosis, 177 we quantified expression of ARTC2.2—an NAD +-dependent ecto-ADP-ribosyltransferase that functions as 178 the key receptor/effector upstream of P2X7R activation—and CD38, an ectoenzyme that degrades 179 extracellular NAD +.(Rivas-Yanez et al , 2020) We analyzed these molecules across the four Nur77-GFP– 180 stratified LN Treg fractions described above and compared them with PB Tregs. ARTC2.2 expression 181 inversely correlated with Nur77-GFP intensity, whereas CD38 expression showed a positive correlation 182 (Fig. 3E ). Notably, PB Tregs exhibited a markedly higher ARTC2.2/CD38 ratio than LN Tregs, providing 183 a mechanistic basis for their enhanced nucleotide sensitivity. Furthermore, PB Tregs exhibited the highest 184 ARTC2.2/CD38 ratio among tissues ( Fig. 3F ). 185

PB Tregs have reduced suppressive capacity in vitro 191 

Next, we compared the in vitro suppressive capacity of PB Tregs and LN Tregs in standard T cell 192 proliferation–suppression assays. PB Tregs suppressed responder T cell proliferation significantly less 193 effectively than LN Tregs ( Supplementary Fig. 3A, 3B ). To investigate the basis for this functional 194 difference, we leveraged our observation that PB Tregs receive weaker self-antigen–driven stimulation and 195 tested whether tonic TCR signaling influences suppressive potency. LN Tregs from Nur77-GFP reporter 196 mice were stratified into four equal fractions according to GFP intensity, and their suppressive activity was 197 assessed in parallel ( Supplementary Fig. S3C ). Suppressive capacity positively correlated with Nur77-198 GFP intensity, strongly suggesting that tonic TCR signaling enhances the ability of Tregs to suppress T cell 199 proliferation ( Supplementary Fig. S3D, S3E ). To gain mechanistic insight, we quantified expression of 200 key mediators implicated in in vitro Treg suppression—CD25, CTLA-4, and IL-10—in the Nur77-GFP– 201 stratified LN Treg fractions and in PB Tregs. CD25 and IL-10 expression positively correlated with Nur77-202 GFP intensity, and both were reduced in PB Tregs ( Supplementary Fig. S3F, S3G ). CTLA-4 expression 203 did not correlate with Nur77-GFP intensity across LN fractions, but was nonetheless lower in PB Tregs 204 than in LN Tregs ( Supplementary Fig. S3F ). Collectively, these findings suggest that the weak tonic TCR 205 stimulation experienced by PB Tregs is associated with reduced expression of CD25 and IL-10 (and lower 206 CTLA-4 overall), which likely contributes to their weakened capacity to suppress T cell proliferation in 207 vitro. 208

In parallel with this decline, PB Tregs 255 exhibited increased CD38 mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) and decreased ARTC2.2 MFI around 18 256 weeks of age ( Fig. 5B, 5C ). We next compared ATP/NAD + sensitivity of PB Tregs from wild-type and 257 ApoE-deficient mice at 6 weeks of age and at the experimental endpoint of 26 weeks. Whereas no difference 258 was evident at 6 weeks, PB Tregs from 26-week-old ApoE-deficient mice showed a marked reduction in 259 sensitivity to ATP/NAD + stimulation (Fig. 5D, 5E ). Collectively, these findings suggest that, as 260 atherosclerosis progresses in ApoE-deficient mice, the ATP/NAD +-hypersensitive fraction of PB Tregs 261 may be selectively eliminated. 262 These observations further raise the hypothesis that PB Tregs are recruited to atherosclerotic plaques, where 263 they undergo apoptosis in response to DAMP nucleotides (ATP/NAD +) and are subsequently engulfed by 264 macrophages, a major cellular component of plaques. To test this model, we adoptively transferred Tregs 265 isolated either from PB or from LN, derived from either wild-type or Art2-deficient donors, into 266 ApoE −/−TCR β−/− recipients, which develop atherosclerosis despite lacking αβ T cells, and then examined 267 donor Tregs within aortic sinus plaques ( Fig. 5F ). Notably, 269 deletion of Art2—which abolishes ATP/NAD + responsiveness—resulted in a pronounced increase in the 270 number of PB-derived donor Tregs detected in plaques ( Fig. 5G, 5H ). In contrast, Art2 deficiency did not 271 substantially increase the number of plaque-detected LN-derived Tregs ( Fig. 5G, 5H ). 272 Together, these data indicate that PB Tregs display enhanced tropism for atherosclerotic plaques. PB Tregs traffic to plaques and suppress plaque growth 281 

We next assessed disease outcomes using a longer transfer regimen (weekly transfers for 14 doses starting 282 at 9 weeks of age; Fig. 6A ). Histologic analysis of the aortic sinus demonstrated that WT or Art2-KO PB 283 Treg transfer significantly suppressed plaque formation compared with no-transfer controls, whereas LN 284 Treg transfer provided weaker or no significant protection under these conditions ( Fig. 6B, 6C ). Notably, 285 suppression of necrotic core formation was observed specifically in recipients of WT PB Tregs ( Fig. 6B, 286 

6C ). None of the transfer conditions produced a significant change in circulating cholesterol levels 287 (Supplementary Fig. S5B ), indicating that the observed effects on plaque pathology were not attributable 288 to systemic lipid lowering. 289 Immunofluorescence of plaques revealed that WT PB Treg transfer reduced macrophage iNOS staining and 290 increased Mertk staining in CD68 + regions, consistent with suppression of inflammatory macrophage 291 polarization and enhancement of efferocytosis-related programs ( Fig. 6D , 6F ). Suppression of iNOS 292 expression was also observed following transfer of Art2-KO PB Tregs; however, the magnitude of 293 suppression was attenuated compared with that mediated by wild-type PB Tregs. ( Fig. 6D, 6F ). Together, 294 these in vivo results indicate that PB Tregs suppress atherosclerotic plaque progression via an ARTC2.2-295 dependent program associated with nucleotide sensitivity and macrophage reprogramming. 296

Although bulk Tregs expressed higher CD38 than bulk Tconv cells 316 (Supplementary Fig. S5G ), the CD38-low Treg subset expressed less CD38 than the average Tconv 317 population ( Supplementary Fig. S5H ). 318 Altogether, despite overall nucleotide resistance, human PB Tregs share key features with mouse PB Tregs: 319 preferential susceptibility of a CD38-low fraction and the capacity to reprogram macrophages after 320 efferocytosis. 321 

The ATP/ NAD + -sensitive fraction of human PB Tregs is reduced in plaque rupture–type acute 322 

myocardial infarction 323 

Collectively, our analyses in the murine atherosclerosis model revealed that progression of plaque 324 development is accompanied by a decline in an ATP/NAD +-hypersensitive Treg subset, and suggested that 325 this subset may suppress plaque formation by inducing macrophage reprogramming via efferocytosis. We 326 therefore asked whether a comparable depletion of ATP/NAD +-sensitive Tregs occurs in human 327 atherosclerotic disease. Because our experiments indicated that the CD38-low fraction of human PB Tregs 328 exhibits increased ATP/NAD + sensitivity, we stratified human PB Tregs into four quartiles based on CD38 329 expression ( Fig. 7H ), performed RNA-seq, and identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between 330 fractions ( Supplementary Table S2 ). We defined the gene set preferentially expressed in the CD38-low 331 Fraction 1 as the “CD38-low Treg module” ( Fig. 7I ). We then leveraged this CD38-low Treg module to 332 mine a public single-cell RNA-seq dataset from patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI).(Qian et 333 

al , 2022) In this study, PB cells were profiled from patients presenting with acute myocardial infarction 334 due to plaque rupture (PR, n=5) or non–plaque rupture (NPR; presumed plaque erosion, n=5). Because 335 ruptured plaques are generally more macrophage-rich and necrosis-rich than eroded plaques, they are 336 expected to exhibit heightened efferocytosis activity. Using Azimuth(Hao et al , 2021) to annotate and 337 extract Tregs, we quantified the CD38-low Treg module score in PB Tregs for each sample 338 (Supplementary Fig. S5I-S5K ). Notably, the module score tended to be lower in PB Tregs from PR 339 patients ( p = 0.0556) ( Fig. 7J ), consistent with the possibility that the ATP/NAD +-sensitive, CD38-low PB 340 Treg fraction is preferentially depleted in the context of plaque rupture, potentially through enhanced 341 efferocytosis within advanced atherosclerotic lesions. 342

Discussion 344 

This study reveals that the PB Treg compartment is not simply a transient conduit between tissues. Instead, 345 blood contains a circulation-biased Treg population enriched for low self-reactivity, low tonic TCR 346 signaling, and a distinct epigenomic and transcriptional program. Although these PB Tregs show attenuated 347 canonical suppressive capacity in vitro, they are uniquely poised to sense danger-associated nucleotides 348 and to couple nucleotide-triggered apoptotic conversion to macrophage reprogramming via efferocytosis. 349 Tregs across tissues acquire specialized phenotypes shaped by local environmental cues, including 350 cytokines, metabolites, and cellular interactions.(Burzyn et al. , 2013; Feuerer et al. , 2009; Ito et al. , 2019) 351 Our data suggest that weak tonic TCR signaling itself may be an important determinant of the PB Treg state. 352 Stratification by Nur77 reporter intensity revealed a continuum in which lower tonic signaling associates 353 with reduced CD25/IL-10 and a higher ARTC2.2/CD38 ratio, culminating in heightened ATP/NAD +354 sensitivity. This coupling provides a conceptual framework: blood-biased, self-antigen–poor Tregs may be 355 selected or maintained under low antigenic drive, while simultaneously becoming specialized to respond to 356 damage-associated nucleotide cues encountered in circulation or at inflamed vascular interfaces. 357 A practical implication emerges from our initial observation: hypotonic RBC lysis can artifactually induce 358 selective death of nucleotide-sensitive Treg subsets. Because ATP/NAD + treatment has minimal impact on 359 many other immune populations, this confound may have been overlooked in prior studies. Our results 360 argue that PB immune phenotyping—especially for Tregs and other potential nucleotide-sensitive 361 subsets—should consider RBC depletion method as an experimental variable. 362 Atherosclerosis engages both adaptive and innate immune responses, and prior work has established an 363 atheroprotective role for Tregs by both depletion and adoptive transfer approaches.(Ait-Oufella et al. , 2006; 364 Bonacina et al. , 2021; Klingenberg et al. , 2013; Mor et al. , 2007; Sharma et al. , 2020) Mechanisms 365 described to date largely reflect functions of viable Tregs, including modulation of macrophage polarization, 366 promotion of efferocytosis, and, in some settings, effects on systemic lipid metabolism.(Bonacina et al. ,367 2021; Proto et al. , 2018; Sharma et al. , 2020) Our study adds a distinct, non-canonical mechanism: PB 368 Tregs sense plaque-associated DAMP nucleotides (ATP/NAD +), undergo apoptotic conversion, and 369 reprogram macrophages upon engulfment. In ApoE −/− mice, the ATP/NAD +-hypersensitive CD38 low ,370 ARTC2.2 high PB Treg fraction declines after plaque onset, consistent with preferential consumption of this 371 subset; notably, the decrease begins around the reported window of aortic sinus plaque initiation and 372 precedes accelerated plaque growth. (Reddick et al., 1994) Thus, our data raise the possibility that, 373 during early atherogenesis, PB Tregs—particularly the ATP/NAD +-hypersensitive CD38 low , ARTC2.2 high 374 subset—protect against plaque formation through a mechanism that involves their own cell death, and that 375

Together, these findings support a potentially conserved PB 380 Treg–efferocytosis axis that may shape plaque inflammation and stability. 381 Efferocytosis is well known to promote anti-inflammatory and tissue-reparative macrophage programs, and 382 accumulating evidence indicates that the identity of the engulfed cell can imprint distinct macrophage 383 states.(Bosurgi et al , 2017; Elliott & Ravichandran, 2016; Liebold et al , 2024; N et al , 2017) Proposed 384 mechanisms for such prey-specific imprinting include differences in the scavenger receptor pathways 385 engaged by particular cargo and delivery of cargo-derived metabolites or other intracellular constituents 386 that reshape macrophage signaling and transcription.(Haldar et al , 2014; Liebold et al. , 2024) Using a tri-387 culture system that isolates phagocytic and non-phagocytic macrophages from the same microenvironment, 388 we found that efferocytosis of apoptotic Tregs selectively dampens IFN-γ–driven inflammatory 389 polarization while preserving IL-10–associated programs. The molecular basis for this Treg-specific 390 suppression remains to be defined. It may involve obligate cell-surface interactions during efferocytosis— 391 particularly because ATP/NAD +-treated Tregs expose phosphatidylserine yet remain viable at the time of 392 contact ( Supplementary Fig. S4E, S4F ) —and/or delivery of Treg-enriched mediators after engulfment. 393 For example, both mice and human Tregs contain high levels of cAMP and can transfer cAMP to other 394 immune cells to suppress activation,(Bodor et al , 2012; Bopp et al , 2007) and cAMP signaling in 395 macrophages can restrain inflammatory cytokine production and support alternative activation.(Luan et al ,396 2015; Wall et al , 2009) Consistent with the notion that Tregs harbor molecular constituents capable of 397 directly reprogramming macrophages, Treg-derived exosomes have been reported to act directly on 398 macrophages, suppressing M1-associated markers including iNOS while inducing M2-associated markers, 399 with protective effects in murine models of AMI. Together, these results suggest that 437 manipulating nucleotide sensing, Treg subset composition, or efferocytosis pathways could be leveraged 438 therapeutically—not only in atherosclerosis but potentially across inflammatory settings where 439 extracellular ATP/NAD + and macrophages are prominent. 440

Study limitations 442 

To focus on macrophage efferocytosis as a mechanistic endpoint, we performed in vivo Treg transfer 443 experiments in ApoE −/−TCR β−/− recipients lacking conventional T cells. Because suppression of adaptive 444 immune responses is an established component of atheroprotective Treg function, the relative contribution 445 of PB Tregs in immune-intact atherosclerosis remains to be determined. In addition, IL-2 is likely limiting 446 in T cell–deficient plaques; because IL-2 promotes Treg survival, Treg apoptosis may have been enhanced 447 relative to immune-intact settings. Furthermore, several mechanistic questions remain open, including the 448 molecular mediators by which engulfed Tregs selectively dampen IFN-γ polarization and the extent to 449 which distinct PB Treg subsets are selectively recruited, converted, or depleted during disease progression. 450 Moreover, our mining of scRNA-seq data from patients with AMI suggested a depletion of the ATP/NAD +-451 sensitive Treg subset—potentially consistent with enhanced efferocytosis in advanced plaques—but this 452 signal did not reach statistical significance ( p > 0.05) and was derived from a retrospective analysis with a 453 limited sample size. Larger, prospective studies will therefore be required to validate this association in 454 human disease. 455 456
