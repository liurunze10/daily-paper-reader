# Abstract 59 

60 

Phagosome maturation arrest (PMA) imposed by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb ) is a 61 

classic tool that helps Mtb evade macrophage anti-bacterial responses. The exclusion of RAB7, 62 

a small GTPase, from Mtb -phagosomes causes PMA. Here, we report an unexpected 63 

mechanism that triggers crosstalk between the mitochondrial quality control (MQC) and the 64 

phagosome maturation pathways that reverses the PMA. CRISPR-mediated p62/SQSTM1 65 

depletion ( p62 KD ) does not appear to impact mitochondrial quality. The p62 KD cells are 66 

restrictive to Mtb growth, triggered by an increasingly oxidative environment and increased 67 

lysosomal targeting. The lysosomal targeting of Mtb is facilitated by enhanced TOM20 +

68 

mitochondria-derived vesicles (MDVs) biogenesis, a key MQC mechanism. In p62 KD cells, 69 

TOM20 +-MDVs biogenesis is MIRO1/MIRO2-dependent and gets delivered to lysosomes for 70 

degradation in a RAB7-dependent manner. Upon infection in p62 KD cells, TOM20 +-MDVs get 71 

extensively targeted to Mtb -phagosomes, inadvertently facilitating RAB7 recruitment, PMA 72 

reversal and lysosomal targeting of Mtb ; the phenotype also replicated in p62/SQSTM1 73 

knockout cells. Triggering MQC collapse in p62 KD cells further diminishes Mtb survival, 74 

signifying cooperation between redox- and lysosome-mediated mechanisms. The MQC-anti-75 

bacterial pathway crosstalk could be exploited for host-directed anti-tuberculosis therapies. 76

# Introduction 79 

80 

Tuberculosis (TB), caused by the pathogen Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb ), continues to 81 

pose challenges to global communicable disease management and is one of the leading 82 

causes of mortality due to infectious diseases. The ability of the bacteria to alter and/or hijack 83 

various host cellular responses in its favour is one of the major factors contributing to the 84 

success of Mtb as a human pathogen. Arguably, understanding the host physiological 85 

processes hijacked or altered by Mtb also presents an opportunity to develop unconventional 86 

strategies to target and control the infection. In the past decade, several host-dependency 87 

factors have been identified, through large genome-wide screens, high-throughput 88 

transcriptome and systematic analysis of bacterial trajectory inside the host macrophages, the 89 

primary cells infected by Mtb (1). 90 

The role of lysosomes in killing bacterial pathogens, including Mtb is well-known (2). 91 

Macrophages phagocytose bacterial pathogens, forming phagosomes, which mature from 92 

early to late phagosomes, eventually fusing with the lysosomes, where the pathogen is 93 

degraded (3) . The phagosome maturation involves RAB5 (early phagosome) to RAB7 (late 94 

phagosome) exchange as a critical rate-limiting step (4). Mtb is known to block this RAB-95 

conversion by excluding RAB7 recruitment, which helps them evade the lysosomal killing 96 

(5). Several mechanisms, including the role of lipid mediators and other signalling events, 97 

have been shown to contribute towards the phagosome maturation arrest (PMA) caused by 98 

Mtb (6). 99 

Autophagy is another important anti-bacterial process that kills bacteria through a lysosomal 100 

targeting mechanism (7, 8). Autophagy performs crucial housekeeping functions like 101 

degradation of misfolded proteins or damaged organelles like mitochondria, peroxisomes, 102 

Endoplasmic Reticulum etc., in addition to its role in response to stresses like oxidative, 103 

nutritional or infection-induced stress (9). Understanding how autophagy machinery 104

distinguishes and segregates its homeostatic cargos, as mentioned above, from those like 105

The cargos targeted for autophagic degradation are usually ubiquitinated, followed by their 107 

recognition by the autophagy adaptors, which interact with the ubiquitinated cargos and 108 

ATG8 or LC3B to capture the cargos into the growing autophagosomes (11). Autophagy 109 

adaptors are a family of proteins with ubiquitin-binding domains and LC3/ATG8 interaction 110 

domains. These adaptors primarily include p62/SQSTM1, NDP52, NBR1, OPTN and 111 

TAX1BP1 (12). Each of these adaptors is reported to have its roles defined in cargo 112 

selectivity. However, some reports suggest functional redundancy among them (13, 14). 113 

While autophagic targeting of bacteria via xenophagy is part of the defence arm of 114 

autophagy, degradation of cellular cargos like mitochondria via mitophagy constitutes the 115 

homeostatic arm (10, 15). Mitophagy is a key pathway for mitochondrial quality control 116 

(MQC) through which damaged mitochondria are removed from the cell (16). Since 117 

mitochondrial damage is a normal consequence of respiration and other oxidative stresses, 118 

their quality control through mitophagy and other pathways is fundamental to cellular 119 

survival (17, 18). 120 

In this study, while studying the effect of depletion of autophagy adaptor proteins like 121 

p62/SQSTM1 on intracellular Mtb survival, we unravel an unexpected MQC that facilitated 122 

Mtb targeting to the lysosomes by reversing the PMA. Our data provide mechanistic insights 123 

on the crosstalk between MQC and conventional host anti-bacterial pathways that are of both 124 

fundamental and biomedical relevance. 125

Since the autophagy adaptors target the autophagic cargo during selective autophagy, we 131 

investigated how the expression and recruitment of these adaptors to the phagosomes and 132 

mitochondria are affected upon Mtb infection. We first analysed for any perturbation in the 133 

expression of the major autophagy adaptors, such as p62/SQSTM1 , OPTN , NBR1 , NDP52 134 

and TAX1BP1 , in THP-1 macrophages upon infection with the Mtb strain, H37Rv (MOI 1:10, 135 

see methods). For all the infection experiments in this study, the time 0 hpi (hours post-136 

infection) includes incubation with H37Rv for 4 hours followed by 2 hours of amikacin 137 

treatment to kill extracellular bacteria (detailed in the methods section). At the transcript 138 

level, OPTN and p62/SQSTM1 showed increased expression at 0-, 24- and 48-hpi (Fig. S1A). 139 

However, immunoblotting indicated higher expression of only p62/SQSTM1 proteins upon 140 

infection (Fig. S1B-C). To target the bacteria for autophagic degradation, autophagy adaptors 141 

need to get recruited onto the bacteria or bacterial phagosomes. Therefore, we evaluated the 142 

recruitment of autophagy adaptors on mCherry-H37Rv phagosomes by immunostaining and 143 

confocal microscopy at 0-, 6-, 12-, 24- and 48-hpi (see methods). While all of the five 144 

autophagy adaptors being studied were recruited onto the bacterial phagosomes (Fig.1A-E), 145 

we did observe some selectivity in the extent of colocalisation. For instance, OPTN showed 146 

the least recruitment to bacterial phagosomes, ranging from 2-4% colocalisation across all the 147 

time points (Fig. 1B), whereas p62/SQSTM1 showed maximum recruitment starting at ~12% 148 

colocalisation at 0 hpi and increasing to ~40% colocalisation at 48-hpi (Fig. 1A). The other 149 

three adaptors showed intermediate levels of recruitment: NBR1 5-20%, NDP52 10-15% and 150 

TAX1BP1 5-15% (Fig. 1C-E). Notably, only p62/SQSTM1 and NBR1 showed increased 151 

recruitment to the bacterial phagosomes over time as the infection progressed (Fig. 1A, 1C). 152

# Discussion 686 

Autophagy adaptors recognise and target the cargos for autophagy-mediated degradation (12). 687 

The damaged mitochondria are one of the significant cellular cargos that must get degraded via 688 

mitophagy to ensure mitochondrial quality and cellular health (51). Since the autophagy 689 

adaptors also play a crucial role in targeting the bacterial pathogens for lysosomal degradation, 690 

in this study, we wanted to study the distribution of the autophagy adaptors between 691 

mitochondria, the cellular cargo and Mtb , the pathogenic cargo, in the infected macrophages. 692 

Understanding this functional segregation of the autophagy adaptors is crucial since several 693 

host-directed therapies against tuberculosis envisage targeting the autophagy (52). However, 694 

most autophagy regulators do not distinguish between the homeostatic and defence arm of 695 

autophagy, potentially limiting the evolution of these regulators to clinically acceptable uses. 696 

Our data suggest p62/SQSTM1 as the major autophagy adaptor recruited to both Mtb and 697 

mitochondria in the infected macrophages. Contrary to our hypothesis, infection with Mtb did 698 

not significantly alter, except for a minor change in OPTN, recruitment and the distribution of 699 

autophagy adaptors on mitochondria. However, irrespective of their high or low association 700 

with the Mtb or mitochondria, individual depletion of each autophagy adaptor, except for 701 

NDP52, resulted in reduced bacterial survival in THP-1 macrophages. These early results 702 

showed that autophagy adaptors in the human macrophages regulate some housekeeping 703 

functions that crisscross with the anti-bacterial pathways. Moreover, it was also apparent that 704 

p62/SQSTM1 regulates some distinct downstream pathways in humans versus other 705 

vertebrates like mice and zebrafish. That is because, in the zebrafish model, p62/SQSTM1 and 706 

OPTN were previously shown to play a critical role in host resistance to mycobacterial 707 

infections (22). In mice, there are reports of an inverse correlation between Mtb -p62/SQSTM1 708 

colocalisation and the bacterial CFU in the BMDMs (53). In yet another study, the absence of 709 

p62/SQSTM1 in mouse BMDMs did not affect Mtb CFU (54). The same study reported that 710

Autophagy, through mitophagy, ensures the maintenance of mitochondrial quality -a serious 720

under check (56). The damaged mitochondria can also cause bacterial killing, primarily 724

mitochondria in the macrophages, we did not observe any loss to mitochondrial quality in 726

aberrant mitochondrial functionality (58). In HeLa cells, only OPTN, NDP52, and, to some 728

the Mtb phagosomes. Thus, MDV recruitment to Mtb phagosomes facilitated bacterial 753

Staphylococcus -containing phagosomes, which helps in the bacterial killing (46). 756
