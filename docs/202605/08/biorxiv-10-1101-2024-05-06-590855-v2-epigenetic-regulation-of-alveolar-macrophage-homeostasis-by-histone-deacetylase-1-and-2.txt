a pivotal role in pathogen clearance and surfactant homeostasis. Although the 26 

molecular regulation of AM development at the transcriptional level has been more 27 

widely studied, the epigenetic mechanisms that maintain AM homeostasis remain 28

Introduction 40 

Alveolar macrophages (AMs) are the major macrophage population residing on the 41 

luminal surface of the alveoli. As key immune sentinels in the lung, AMs maintain 42 

pulmonary homeostasis by clearing incoming pathogens and airborne pollutants 1 .43 

Their dysfunction causes pulmonary surfactant accumulation, leading to pulmonary 44 

alveolar proteinosis (PAP) 2 . AMs primarily originate from fetal monocytes that 45 

colonize the lungs before birth and achieve a stable phenotype postnatally 3-6 . Under 46 

steady-state conditions, AMs self-maintain through local proliferation without input 47 

from bone marrow (BM) precursors 7. Their identity is heavily shaped by the alveolar 48 

microenvironment, particularly GM-CSF secreted by alveolar epithelial cells, which 49 

guides their differentiation and maturation 3,5 . Impaired GM-CSF signaling (e.g., in 50 

Csf2 −/− , Csf2rb −/− , or Csf2ra −/− mice) leads to AM deficiency 8,9 and surfactant 51 

accumulation 8-12 . GM-CSF drives expression of peroxisome proliferator-activated 52 

receptor-gamma (PPAR-γ), a key regulator of lipid metabolism that defines AM 53 

identity 5 . Other crucial factors, including mTOR 13,14 , PU.1 15 , C/EBP β16 and STAT5 17 ,54 

are also essential for AM differentiation and function under steady-state conditions. 55 

Additionally, autocrine TGF-β signaling enhances PPAR-γ expression and promotes 56 

AM homeostasis 18 .57 

Although the roles of cytokines and transcription factors in regulating AM 58 

homeostasis are increasingly understood, the epigenetic mechanisms involved remain 59

poorly understood. Histone acetylation plays a central role in transcriptional 60 

regulation, which is dynamically regulated by histone acetyltransferases (HATs) and 61 

deacetylases (HDACs) 19 . HDACs remove acetyl groups from histones, leading to 62 

chromatin condensation and transcriptional repression 20 . Mammalian genomes encode 63 

18 HDACs, which are categorized into four classes based on their structure, 64 

enzymatic mechanism and intracellular localization. HDAC1 and HDAC2 belong to 65 

class I HDACs, and have a wide tissue distribution and share ~83% sequence 66 

similarity 21 . Both HDAC1 and HDAC2 are critical for immune cell development and 67 

function. Their inactivation impairs hematopoiesis 22-24 , lymphopoiesis 25-27 , microglia 68 

maintenance 28 , and interferon production by macrophages 29,30 . Notably, in human 69 

chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), reduced HDAC2 expression in AMs 70 

is associated with disease severity 31,32 , suggesting a vital role for epigenetic 71 

regulation. 72 

In this study, we utilized a genetic approach to conditionally delete both Hdac1 73 

and Hdac2 in AMs. Our findings reveal that HDAC1 and HDAC2 are indispensable 74 

and exhibit functional redundancy in maintaining AM homeostasis. Deficiency in 75 

either Hdac1 or Hdac2 alone did not significantly affect AM homeostasis, however, 76 

combined deletion resulted in profound defects in AM development, culminating in 77 

PAP and significantly enhanced susceptibility to viral infection in mice. We first 78 

confirmed that loss of HDAC1 and HDAC2 did not significantly affect GM-CSF 79 

signaling or PPAR γ expression. We next demonstrated that the deacetylase activity of 80 

HDAC1 and HDAC2 was required to repress the expression of pro-apoptotic Bim ,81 

thereby promoting AM survival. Furthermore, we found that HDAC1 and HDAC2 82 

interacted with the histone demethylase LSD1, enabling coordinated regulation of a 83 

transcriptional program essential for AM differentiation. Taken together, these 84 

findings reveal HDAC1 and HDAC2 as master epigenetic regulators for AM 85 

homeostasis and function through coordinating transcriptional programs governing 86 

cell survival and differentiation. 87

HDAC1 and HDAC2 deficiency in AMs impairs surfactant clearance and 139 

antiviral response 140 

AMs are central to pulmonary homeostasis by clearing surfactant secreted by alveolar 141 

type II epithelial cells. Deficiency of AMs leads to PAP. To evaluate pulmonary 142 

pathology in Hdac1 and Hdac2 double deficient mice, we analyzed Itgax Cre -DKO 143 

mice and observed a marked accumulation of total protein levels in bronchoalveolar 144 

lavage fluid (BALF), accompanied by intense periodic acid–Schiff (PAS) staining 145 

(Fig. 2A-C). Immunoblotting further revealed elevated levels of surfactant proteins in 146 

lung tissues (Fig. 2D). Moreover, increased neutrophil counts in BALF (Fig. S3A, B) 147 

and elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Fig. S3C) were detected, 148 

indicating that AM deficiency induces low-grade pulmonary inflammation. 149

Given that Bim knockdown only partially rescued apoptosis in Hdac1/2 -deficient 224 

AM-like cells, we next tested whether it could also rescue differentiation defects. 225 

Using an AM-like culture system with Bim -specific shRNA knockdown, we observed 226 

no significant improvement in AM differentiation (Fig. S6), indicating that HDAC1 227

Discussion 245 

The lung is constantly exposed to inhaled microbes and pollutants, with AMs serving 246 

as essential effectors for host defense, surfactant clearance, and lung development. In 247 

this study, we show that Hdac1 and Hdac2 double-deficiency led to severe AM 248 

defects, pulmonary alveolar proteinosis, and dramatically increased mortality after 249 

influenza infection. We further uncover that HDAC1 and HDAC2 maintained AM 250 

survival by repressing the pro-apoptotic protein Bim and promoted AM differentiation 251 

through interaction with LSD1. These findings define HDAC1 and HDAC2 as critical 252 

epigenetic regulators of AM homeostasis. 253 

Epigenetic mechanisms, including histone modifications, DNA methylation, and 254 

noncoding RNAs, are central to AM development and function, modulating cellular 255 

responses to both developmental cues and environmental stimuli 35 . One 256 

well-established epigenetic mechanism is PRC2-mediated H3K27 trimethylation 257 

(H3K27me3). Loss of the PRC2 component SUZ12 impairs AM self-renewal and 258 

dramatically reduces AM numbers, highlighting the importance of histone 259 

methylation in AM biology 36 . Histone deacetylases (HDACs) constitute another major 260 

class of epigenetic regulators, among which HDAC3 has been shown to be essential 261 

for AM embryonic development, postnatal homeostasis, and maturation 37 . Yao et al. 262 

demonstrated that HDAC3 binds Pparg enhancers, and HDAC3 deficiency leads to 263 

mitochondrial oxidative dysfunction, impaired AM maturation, and disrupted AM 264 

homeostasis 37 . Our unpublished work also suggests that HDAC3 promoted AM 265 

proliferation via GM-CSF signaling, and maintained lipid metabolism through 266 

CPT1A, highlighting the critical role of HDAC3 in coordinating metabolic and 267 

developmental programs in AMs. 268 

HDAC1, HDAC2, and HDAC3 all belong to class I HDACs, yet the roles of 269

HDAC1 and HDAC2 in AM biology remain poorly defined. Despite their high 270 

sequence similarity (83%) and redundant functions in other cell types 23,26,38,39 , our 271 

data demonstrate that HDAC1 and HDAC2 are indispensable for AM survival and 272 

pulmonary homeostasis. Particularly, HDAC1, HDAC2, and HDAC3 regulate AM 273 

homeostasis by distinct mechanisms, indicating non-redundant functions within class 274 

I HDACs. HDAC3 drives AM development and maturation via PPAR γ signaling 37 .275 

However, in this study we showed that deficiency of HDAC1 and HDAC2 did not 276 

affect GM-CSFR expression, PPAR γ activation, or STAT5 signaling, but instead 277 

primarily triggered extensive apoptosis during early AM establishment, accompanied 278 

by Bim upregulation and widespread transcriptomic changes enriched in apoptosis 279 

pathways. HDAC1 and HDAC2 act as catalytic cores within co-repressor complexes 280 

such as Sin3 40 , NuRD 41 , and CoREST 42 , which likely confer distinct transcriptional 281 

repression programs compared with HDAC3-associated complexes. 282 

Different epigenetic mechanisms often cooperate to fine-tune gene transcription. 283 

For instance, HDAC-mediated deacetylation and LSD1-mediated demethylation can 284 

act synergistically within shared co-repressor complexes to define cell fate decisions 285 

and maintain cellular fitness in response to diverse stimuli 43,44 . In this study, we 286 

validated a physical interaction between HDAC1/2 and LSD1. Moreover, both 287 

pharmacological inhibition and genetic knockdown of LSD1 partially rescued the AM 288 

defects resulting from HDAC1 and HDAC2 deficiency. These findings support a 289 

model in which HDAC1/2 and LSD1 function cooperatively to guide AM 290 

development and homeostasis. We propose that, in the absence of HDAC1 and 291 

HDAC2, the HDAC-LSD1 complex becomes destabilized, leading to dysregulated 292 

transcriptional programs at LSD1 target loci required for AM development, ultimately 293 

resulting in AM loss. 294
