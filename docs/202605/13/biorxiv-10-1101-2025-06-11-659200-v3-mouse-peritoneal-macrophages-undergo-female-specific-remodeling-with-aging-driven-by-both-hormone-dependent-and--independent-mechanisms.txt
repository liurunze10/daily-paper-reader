Abstract 29 Aging is a complex process characterized by a progressive decline in physiological functions. 30 Immune function is strongly influenced by biological sex, affecting both innate and adaptive responses. 31 Here, we investigated the effects of age and sex on mouse peritoneal immune cells and identified 32 macrophages as the top affected cell type. Macrophages, as central components of the innate immune 33 system, play critical roles in homeostasis maintenance and infection response. We found that aging 34 induces sex-specific remodeling of mouse peritoneal macrophage ‘omic’ landscapes, which is 35 accompanied by female-specific age-related functional remodeling ( i.e . decreased phagocysis, increased 36 glycolysis). We show that age-related changes in circulating estrogen levels likely drive aspects of 37 female-specific macrophage age-related changes, specifically age-related phagocytosis decline. By 38 leveraging our multi-omic dataset, we identify transcription factors whose female-specific age-regulation 39 may drive female-specific age-related changes in peritoneal macrophage phenotypes. Interestingly, Irf2 40 downregulation was sufficient to recapitulate aspects of female peritoneal macrophage aging phenotypes, 41 including increased glycolysis. Mechanistically, decreased Irf2 expression leads to decreased binding to 42 its target genes, including Hk3 , which encodes hexokinase (the rate-limiting enzyme of glycolysis). Our 43 findings support the notion that aging leads to female-specific remodeling of mouse peritoneal 44 macrophages through hormone-dependent and -independent mechanisms. 45

Introduction 47 Aging is a complex process characterized by a progressive decline in physiological functions, 48 with notable differences between sexes 1 . This decline is driven by a combination of biological factors and 49 extrinsic factors 2 . Life expectancy of females is, on average, longer than that of males 3 . However, despite 50 increased longevity, females tend to experience higher levels of comorbidities and disability than males 51 (i.e . the mortality-morbidity paradox) 4-6 . Additionally, females face an elevated risk of developing age-52 related chronic conditions ( e.g . coronary heart disease, osteoarthritis, Type 2 diabetes, neurodegenerative 53 disorders) 7 . Chronic sterile inflammation, a mechanism dubbed “inflamm-aging”, is a hallmark of aging 54 and is associated with more general immune dysfunction or “immunosenescence” 8,9 . Despite well-55 documented sex-differences in aging and disease susceptibility, mechanisms driving sex-based aging 56 disparities are poorly understood. 57 Immune function is heavily shaped by biological sex, influencing both innate and adaptive 58 immune cells 10,11 , including macrophages 12 . Females generally exhibit a more robust immune response 59 compared to males ( e.g . quicker pathogen clearance, higher antibody response, higher cytokine secretion, 60 and less infection-related mortality) 10,11,13-15 . However, females are also more susceptible to autoimmune 61 diseases 16 . During aging, hematopoietic stem cells (HSC) skew production toward myeloid cells ( e.g .62 monocytes, neutrophils) at the expense of lymphoid cells ( e.g. B- and T-cells), a shift more pronounced in 63 males than females, thereby impacting leukemogenesis and hematopoiesis 17,18 . Importantly, lifelong sex 64 differences in immune function can be shaped by differential ‘omic’ regulation in immune cell types 19-24 .65 Despite the potential significance of such sex-differences in immune aging, how biological sex shapes the 66 impact of age on ‘omic’ and functional landscapes of innate immune cells is largely unexplored. 67 Macrophages are key components of the innate immune system, functioning as both regulatory 68 and effector cells in maintaining tissue homeostasis and responding to infections. Tissue-resident 69 macrophages ( e.g . large peritoneal macrophages), which originate primarily from embryonic precursors, 70 exhibit specialized, organ-specific functions shaped by the local microenvironment. In contrast, bone 71 marrow-derived macrophages differentiate from circulating monocytes and typically adopt more pro-72 inflammatory phenotypes during immune responses 25,26 . Macrophages have a repertoire of functions: 73 phagocytosis of pathogens and other foreign materials, clearance of dead/dying and senescent cells, 74 chemotaxis and migration, and cytokine production 27 . During aging, macrophages have been reported to 75 exhibit altered phagocytosis, metabolic changes, and cytokine secretion, leading to poor inflammation 76 control 28-36 . In addition to age-related differences, sex-differences in young macrophages have also been 77 reported 10 , where young females exhibited greater phagocytic activity and more efficient antigen 78 presentation 37,38 . Interestingly, microglia, the brain’s resident macrophages, undergo a female-specific 79 shift in glycolytic output with age dependent on C3a signaling 39 . Macrophages are the immune cell type most impacted by aging in the peritoneal cavity niche 88 The peritoneal cavity hosts a rich and accessible immune niche, housing B-cells, T-cells, 89 macrophages, neutrophils, and monocytes 41 . This immune niche is easily accessible through post-mortem 90 peritoneal lavage 42,43 , making it tractable for studies without the need for mechanical tissue dissociation 91 (which compromises both cell viability and phenotypes 44 ). We used this niche to examine how biological 92 sex may impact the mice’s aging immune system. Peritoneal macrophages show sex-dimorphic ‘omic’ phenotypes with aging 133 To better understand the impact of aging and sex on aging peritoneal macrophages, we performed 134 a bulk multi-omic profiling approach using MACS-purified macrophages from young adults (4-5 months) 135 and old (20-21 months) female and male C57BL/6JNia mice. We opted to use a bulk approach due to its 136 greater sensitivity and robustness compared to single-cell profiling, particularly for well-defined purified 137 cells. For this study, we profiled transcriptional and epigenomic features, specifically (i) transcriptome 138 profiling by bulk sequencing of ribosomal-RNA depleted RNA (RNA-seq), (ii) chromatin accessibility 139 landscape through the Assay for Transposon-Accessible Chromatin followed by sequencing (ATAC-140 seq) 49,50 , and (iii) promoter activity/priming using CUT&RUN profiling for the H3K4me3 histone post-141 translational modification 51 (Figure 2a ). These 'omic' assays were each performed on an independent 142 cohort of C57BL/6JNia animals (n = 5 animals per group; Extended Data Table 1A ). After accounting 143 for premature death before the experimental day ( Extended Data Table 1A ) and retaining only samples 144 with > 90% macrophage purity ( Extended Data Figure 4a, b ), final sample numbers ranged from 3-5 per 145 group and ‘omic’ layer. 146 The peritoneal macrophage population could be comprised of both large peritoneal macrophages 147 (LPM; with high surface levels of F4/80) and small peritoneal macrophages (SPM; with lower surface 148 levels of F4/80) 52 . LPMs are the tissue-resident macrophages of the peritoneal cavity derived from the 149 fetal yolk sac, and constitute the overwhelming majority of peritoneal macrophages in naïve mice 53-55 . In 150 contrast, SPMs are constantly derived throughout life from blood monocytes, which are recruited to the 151 peritoneal cavity in response to inflammatory signals, and represent a minority of peritoneal macrophages 152 in naïve mice 55,56 . Discussion 649 

A multi-omic resource to study murine tissue-resident macrophages as a function of age and 650 

biological sex. 651 To understand how sex may influence aging of the immune system, specifically macrophages, we 652 generated a multi-omic resource focusing on primary resident peritoneal macrophages from young and 653 old, female and male C57BL/6J[Nia] mice, with high-quality transcriptomic (single-cell and bulk RNA-654 seq) and epigenomic (chromatin accessibility by ATAC-seq and H3K4me3 genomic distribution by 655 CUT&RUN) maps. To our knowledge, our dataset is the largest multi-omic dataset for the study of 656 primary macrophages during aging, and we believe it will be a rich resource to the scientific community 657 in understanding the influence of biological sex on age-related immune remodeling. Analysis of these 658 datasets revealed that, not only is there a baseline difference in genomic regulation of primary 659 macrophages at a young age, but also that aging differentially impacts ‘omic’ profiles in female vs . male 660 animals. Although there were shared features of molecular aging in female and male macrophages, we 661 observed much larger sex-dimorphic age-related changes in gene expression and chromatin organization 662 (Figure 2 ). 663 Notably, genes and genomic regions showing age-related sex-dimorphic regulation were enriched 664 for processes key to macrophage function, including phagocytosis regulation, metabolic wiring, and 665 inflammatory phenotypes ( Figure 3 ). Although aging phenotypes of macrophages across various niches 666 have been reported ( e.g . visceral adipose macrophages, alveolar macrophages, Kupffer cells, etc .), 667 previous studies largely focused on using animals of one sex, or mostly reported results combining sexes 668 (reviewed in 36 ). A notable exception includes a series of recent studies on aging murine microglia, the 669 brain resident macrophage, which also reported large sex-differences in molecular landscapes with 670 aging 39,107 .671 672

To understand the molecular mechanism driving female-specific regulation of phagocytosis, we 683 tested candidate regulator genes of phagocytosis from CRISPR/Cas9 screens that were specifically 684 downregulated in aged female macrophages through a mini shRNA knockdown screen ( Figure 4h ). Our 685 results identified two phagocytosis regulators ( Magt1 and Rps6ka3 ) whose knockdown mimicked the age-686 related decrease in phagocytosis of female macrophages, consistent with the notion that their 687 downregulation with aging may explain mechanistically our observed female-specific age-related 688 decrease in macrophage phagocytic capacity. Although its impact during aging has not been evaluated 689 before, Magt1 is a Magnesium transporter located in the endoplasmic reticulum involved in protein 690 glycosylation with a critical function in immune cells 108,109 , which was identified as required for proper 691 phagocytosis of Zymosan in a whole genome CRISPR/Cas9 screen of human phagocytes 71 . Rps6ka3 encodes a member of the S6 kinase family, whose activity is regulated by mTOR 110 , which is a 693 key regulator of aging 111 , autophagy 112 , phagosome maturation 113 , and macrophage polarization 114 .694 

Rps6ka3 was also shown to play a direct role in innate immune responses 115 and was found to be required 695 for phagocytosis of Zymosan in human phagocytes 71 . Thus, our 704 observations are consistent with a potential shift to a more dysfunctional state in female macrophages 705 with aging. 706
