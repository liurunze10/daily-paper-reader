# Abstract 7

Numerous studies have highlighted  the crucial roles of cytosolic calcium transients mediated by inositol 8

1,4,5 -trisphosphate receptor (IP3R) channels in intracellular signaling, as well as the significant modulatory 9

effects of cytosolic redox state on th ese dynamics . Accordingly, developing a mathematical model that 10 

captures the core dynamics by which redox state of IP3Rs influence calcium signaling would provide a 11 

valuable foundation for simulating and predicting experimental observations. In this study, we developed a 12 

dynamic system , based on ordinary differential equations that incorporates a kinetic model for the redox 13 

dynamic of the IP3R and the role of this dynamic on the channel’s gating parameters . Using this framework, 14 

we simulated both the static and dynamic effects of mitochondria -derived  superoxide fluctuations on local 15 

calcium oscillations in non -excitable cells . Numerical solutions demonstrate d that the model quantitatively 16 

reproduce d experimentally observed changes in local calcium oscillation frequency in response to 17 

mitochondria -derived superoxide variations. Overall, the proposed model offers a predictive tool for 18 

exploring how redox perturbations in non -excitable cells affect cellular signaling pathways through calcium 19 

frequency modulation .20

Dynamic model, calcium oscillation, reactive oxygen species, mitochondria, cell signaling 22 

# Introduction 23 

The dynamics of free calcium in the cytosol of cells —appearing as global oscillations, propagating waves, 24 

or localized oscillations —has significant physiological importance across a wide range of cell types, 25 

including excitable cells such as neurons and cardiac cells, and non -excitable cells (many of other somatic 26 

cell types ) (1 –3) . Under conditions of cellular activity, these dynamics dominantly appear as global 27 

oscillations or propagating waves in the cytosol, controlling vital signaling processes such as cell 28 

differentiation, gene expression and secretion of hormones or other substances (4 –6) . In contrast, in resting 29

conditions, calcium dynamics in the cytosol often appear as localized fluctuations —known as calcium 30 

flashes, sparks, or puffs —and play a critical role in maintaining the stability of home -keeping functions, 31 

particularly mitochondrial function and thus energetic stability of cell s (7 –9) . This role has been observed 32 

in both excitable and non -excitable cells (10) . Meanwhile , numerous studies have demonstrated that the 33 

frequency of calcium oscillations, rather than solely their amplitude, is the key determinant of their 34 

physiological functions (11,12) .35 

On the other hand, several studies have indicated that cellular reactive oxygen species (ROS), especially 36 

mitochondria -derived ROS , exhibit localized oscillations under pathological or even physiological 37 

conditions (13 –15) . For instance, research on a variety of cell types revealed that a single mitochondrion 38 

generate d pulses of superoxide in the surrounding cytosol under resting cellular conditions (16) .39 

Parallel to above studies, the relationship between ROS and calcium dynamics has been investigated for 40 

decades (17) . For example, it has been shown that calcium dynamics is influenced by mitochondrial ROS 41 

dynamics or the redox state dynamics of the cytosol, and alterations in the concentration of ROS or changes 42 

in the reducing capacity of the cytosol modify calcium dynamics (18) . Particularly, observations in this 43 

context have shown that an increase in cytosolic ROS, under resting conditions that concentration of inositol 44 

1,4,5 -trisphosphate (IP3 )—which stimulates calcium channels in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER )45 

membrane known as inositol 1,4,5 -trisphosphate receptors (IP3R) —is at basal levels, can initiate abnormal 46 

global calcium oscillations or increase the frequency of calcium flashes (19 –22) . Interestingly, a recent 47 

study on local mitochondria l calcium dynamics demonstrate d triggering mitochondrial superoxide 48 

production modulat ed the frequency of calcium oscillation s, and influenced mitochondrial quality control 49 

and mitophagy in cellular models of Parkinson’s disease (23) .50 

Indeed, studies of the molecular structure of IP3R s have reveal ed that these channels, as key components 51 

of the calcium dynamics toolbox, have numerous thiol groups (in the structure of cysteine residues ) on their 52 

cytosolic side and thus can be sensitive to redox changes (24 –29) . In fact, oxidation of these groups by a 53 

wide range of oxidants, increases the open probability of IP3Rs at a constant IP3 concentration or the 54 

duration of open state of the channels in each pulse (30,31) .55 

Considering the aforementioned studies, which highlight the importance of calcium dynamics, various 56 

mathematical models based on systems of differential equations (ODE/PDE), or agent -based models have 57 

been developed to deepen our understanding of this dynamics and simulate both global calcium oscillations 58 

or local calcium flashes (32 –46) . Among these, some have specifically aimed to simulate the effect of 59 

mitochondrial ROS dynamics on the frequency of calcium flashes (45,46) . However, dynamical models 60 

based on compartmentalization of different cytoplasmic regions face two fundamental issues: First, the 61

experimental observations. Consequently, a physical basis for determining their quantitative values is 65 

lacking. The second and more important issue is that in models developed to simulate the effect of ROS on 66 

calcium dynamics, the effect of ROS is mainly applied directly to the open probability of IP3R channels 67 

(45,46) . Whereas, the open probability of IP3R channels, according to existing models, is itself a variable 68 

dependent on several parameters and has a complex dynamic (47) . Therefore, the rational role of IP3R 69 

channel redox dynamics on the parameters of the open probability of IP3Rs is also missing. 70 

Therefore, in the present study, we pursue two main objectives: 71 

First, to adapt existing models for calcium and mitochondrial superoxide dynamics with the geometry 72 

obtained from microscopic images of these dynamics and images of the microdomains of cytosol near single 73 

mitochondria (especially the cytosolic region between a single mitochondrion and ER mitochondria -74 

associated membranes abbreviated as MAMs) (48) , and by considering a physical basis for the quantitative 75 

values of diffusion rates between different compartments. 76 

Second, and more importantly, to develop a kinetic model for the redox dynamics of IP3R channels and a 77 

model for the dependence of IP3R channel open probability and dynamics on the redox state of these 78 

channels. 79 

Achieving these two goals enables us to construct a more realistic computational model of the effects of 80 

ROS on calcium dynamics under both physiological and pathological conditions in non -excitable cells , and 81 

ultimately to predict how changes in key chemical or geometrical parameters may influence calcium 82 

signaling. 83

## Compartmentalization 85 

Our modeling was based on a system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs). The model was developed 86 

with six compartments: 1) the matrix of a single mitochondrion (V M), 2) the cytosolic region between a 87 

mitochondrion and adjacent ER membrane known as MAM (V MAM ), which we refer to briefly as the MAM 88 

cytosol, 3) the peripheral cytosolic microdomain of a single mitochondrion (V N) excluding V MAM , which 89 

hereafter we refer to briefly as the near cytosol, 4) the bulk cytosol (V B), 5) the lumen of endoplasmic 90 

reticulum adjacent to a mitochondrion (V EN ), and 6) the lumen of endoplasmic reticulum within the bulk 91 

cytoplasm (V EB ) (Fig 1b). 92

## Numerical Solution and Sensitivity Analysis of the Model 258 

The numerical solution of the system of ordinary differential equations was carried out using the SciPy 259 

package in Python3. Due to the inherent stiffness of the system —resulting from the oscillations in calcium 260 

and superoxide concentrations —an implicit integration method, namely the Radau algorithm, was 261 

employed to ensure numerical stability. The complete simulation script, along with the code for plotting 262 

calcium and superoxide oscillations, is publicly available on GitHub:  https://github.com/Ali -Same -263 

Majandeh/Calcium -Frequency -Modulation .264 

To conduct a global sensitivity analysis (GSA) of the model parameters, we employed the Regional 265 

Sensitivity Analysis (RSA) framework, based on Monte Carlo Filtering (MCF). Accordingly, 100,000 266 

parameter sets encompassing all 52 parameters were generated via Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS), 267 

assuming uniform prior distributions within a ±40% variation range. Following the classification of model 268 

outputs into behavioral and non -behavioral sets (discussed in Results & Discussion) , the parameter 269 

sensitivities were scored using the Kolmogorov -Smirnov (KS) statistic. Both the LHS sampling procedure 270 

and the subsequent RSA/KS statistical evaluations were implemented utilizing the SALib package in 271 

Python. 272

To simulate experimental observations indicating the influence of superoxide on calcium oscillations, the 329 

range of the superoxide production rate in the mitochondrial matrix (k shunt ) was divided into two regions. 330 

The first region, analyzed initially, includes values of k shunt for which, due to the higher degradation rate of 331 

superoxide in the cytosol compared to its leakage from a single mitochondrion, superoxide does not 332 

accumulate in the mitochondrion adjacent regions (i.e., MAM cytosol and near cytosol). As a result, 333 

superoxide oscillations driven by the ROS -induced ROS release (RIRR) mechanism (15) do not occur. 334

In general, modeling the modulation of IP3R gating dynamics under the influence of superoxide —rather 404 

than other oxidants —is supported by two key rationales: First, among the thiol -oxidizing agents whose 405 

effects on calcium dynamics via IP3Rs have been studied —such as thimerosal (30,51) , diamide (27) ,406 

superoxide (26) , peroxide (19) , vanadate (29) , chloramine T (29) , etc. —only superoxide and peroxide are 407 

naturally occurring compounds endogenously produced within cells, particularly by mitochondria. Second, 408 

the diffusion coefficient of peroxide in aqueous solutions has been reported to be 10 to 100 times greater 409 

than that of superoxide (55) . Therefore, given the highly localized nature of calcium flashes (typically 410 

within a radius of 1 –2 micrometers), modeling a superoxide -mediated effect is more plausible and 411 

physiologically consistent than one mediated by peroxide. 412

# Sensitivity Analysis 623 

Given that the majority of parameter values are derived from four distinct, previously developed models, 624 

concerns may arise regarding the biological compatibility and physiological plausibility of this complex 625 

model and parameter integration. However, two of the reference models employed in the present study —626 

namely, the calcium dynamics model by Sneyd et al. (44) and the model by Moshkforoush et al. (38) —627 

serve as generic frameworks applicable to a broad spectrum of non -excitable cells. Furthermore, the model 628 

proposed by Wacquier et al. (36) was specifically formulated for HeLa cells, which are widely regarded as 629 

typical non -excitable cells in the context of calcium dynamics. Moreover, although the model by Yang et 630 

al. (13) was initially developed to describe mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) dynamics in 631 

excitable cardiac cells, it has previously been successfully applied to non -excitable cancer cells conserving 632 

the most of original parameter set (53) , demonstrating a high degree of consistency in simulating ROS 633 

dynamics within these cells. 634

# Conclusion 693 

The mathematical model developed in this study successfully capture d and simulate d the influence of 694 

superoxide dynamics on local calcium oscillations as observed in experimental studies. Compared to 695 

previous models addressing the impact of reactive oxygen species (ROS) on calcium dynamics, our 696 

approach adopts a more rational framework by incorporating the influence of ROS through modulation of 697 

the redox state governing IP3 receptor s (IP3R) gating dynamics, rather than assuming a direct and 698 

phenomenological effect of ROS on these calcium channels. This highlights how existing models of IP3R 699 

dynamics can be extended to account for redox -dependent modifications under varying cellular conditions. 700 

Nonetheless, the current model has two limitations. First, it considers only the unidirectional interaction 701 

between ROS and calcium —specifically from mitochondrial superoxide to IP3R -mediated calcium 702 

dynamics —whereas this relationship is inherently bidirectional. The second is that the model contains only 703 

the interactions between calcium and superoxide in the vicinity of a single mitochondrion, without 704 

accounting for the role of other mitochondria as part of the broader mitochondrial network. 705 

Therefore, integrating the bidirectional relationship of ROS and calcium and the structural and functional 706 

dynamics of the mitochondrial network into the proposed model in future studies , can provide a more 707 

comprehensive and physiologically realistic representation of calcium and ROS signaling under both 708 

normal and pathological cellular conditions. 709 

Ultimately, beyond elucidating fundamental biophysical interactions, the proposed framework holds 710 

significant practical applications for advancing both basic and translational research. First, by establishing 711 

a mechanistic link between localized oxidative stress and calcium frequency decoding, the model provides 712 

a crucial computational tool for investigating pathological conditions characterized by altered redox 713 

homeostasis, such as neurodegenerative diseases and aging. Furthermore, as explicitly demonstrated by our 714 

simulation of ELF -PMF exposure, the model serves as a robust in silico testbed. It can reliably predict how 715 

external physical stimuli or pharmacological interventions that modulate mitochondrial ROS will 716 

subsequently alter vital downstream calcium signaling networks. Such predictive capabilities can 717 

significantly accelerate hypothesis generation, minimize trial -and -error, and guide future experimental and 718 

therapeutic designs. 719
