Abstract 24 

Orientia tsutsugamushi (Ot ) is an obligatory intracellular bacterium that can cause 25 scrub typhus, an emerging but severely neglected disease with high mortality rates. Ot 26 Karp and Gilliam strains account for most reported cases in Southeast Asia. Our group 27 has reported that Karp-infected outbred and inbred mice exhibit more severe disease 28 outcomes than their Gilliam-infected counterparts, likely due to their excessive 29 inflammation and tissue injury. Macrophages (MΦs) serve as the main target cells for Ot 30 replication and host defense against the infection; they also are key players in immune 31 modulation and cytokine/chemokine production. However, it remains unclear as to how 32 MΦ-Ot interactions impact disease outcomes. In this study, we focused on RNAseq from 33 C57BL/6 mouse-derived MΦs to reveal Ot infection - and strain-related immune 34 signatures. While Ot infection modulated several common canonical pathways/genes, we 35 identified unique pathway/gene signatures that were highly selective for Karp or Gilliam 36 strain, respectively. Karp infection uniquely upregulated proinflammatory signaling and 37 pattern recognition receptors, including C-type lectin receptors (CLRs), including 38 Mincle/ Clec4e and Dectin-2/ Clec4n . In contrast, Gilliam infection enhanced MΦ 39 proliferation and DNA replication, and Gilliam strain grew better in M0-like MΦs than Karp 40 strain. In IFN 𝛾 -primed (M1-like) MΦs, however, Karp exhibited significantly greater 41 resistance against host killing than Gilliam, suggesting its superior ability to evade host 42 immune responses. Overall, Karp strain preferentially upregulated CLRs and activated 43 type 1-skewed inflammatory responses, but it is also relatively resistant to IFN 𝛾 -mediated 44 killing. This study provides new insights into potential mechanisms underlying Ot strain-45 associated immune responses and disease outcomes. 46

Author Summary 47 

Orientia tsutsugamushi (Ot ), the causative agent of scrub typhus, is an 48 understudied, life-threatening pathogen endemic to Southeast Asia. Among its various 49 pathogenic strains, Karp and Gilliam are clinically dominant and exhibit distinct disease 50 severity. Building on previous reports that Karp-infected mice display more severe 51 disease than those infected with Gilliam, we explored how strain-specific interactions with 52 macrophages (MΦs), a central cell type in both Ot replication and host immunity, may 53 contribute to these outcomes. Using RNAseq of primary MΦs from C57BL/6 mice, we 54 characterized transcriptional profiles of both strains. Karp infection was uniquely 55 associated with increased proinflammatory signaling and the selective induction of 56 bacterial-sensing molecules. In contrast, Gilliam infection promoted host cell proliferation 57 and supported more efficient bacterial replication in naïve MΦs. Notably, Karp 58 demonstrated increased resistance to host killing in stimulated (proinflammatory) MΦs 59 compared to Gilliam. These findings suggest that the Karp strain can drive strong 60 inflammatory responses but is adept at evading IFN 𝛾 -mediated immune defenses. Our 61 study uncovers key immunological distinctions between Ot strains and offers novel 62 insights into the mechanisms of strain-specific pathogenesis in scrub typhus, with 63 potential to uncover biomarkers for disease severity. 64

Results 239 

Karp Infection Strongly Activates Inflammatory MΦ-related Genes in Lung Tissues. 240 

To define disease outcomes and early immune responses during infection, we 241 infected B6 mice with a similar infectious dose of Karp or Gilliam strain (5.6 × 10 4 FFU, 242 i.v.). Consistent with our previous report (17), Karp-infected mice had increased disease 243 scores and progressive body weight loss around 8 dpi (Fig. 1A-B), as well as consistently 244 high lung bacterial burdens at 4, 8, and 12 dpi (Fig. 1C). In contrast, Gilliam infection was 245 self-limited with no disease signs. To further test our hypothesis that such distinct disease 246 outcomes are regulated by the innate immunity, especially MF-related immune 247 responses (17), we examined mRNA levels of MΦ-associated proinflammatory markers 248 in lung tissues via qRT-PCR. As shown in Fig. 1D, while both Il6 and Mincle/ Clec4e 249 mRNA levels were significantly higher in Karp than in Gilliam infection at 4 dpi, 250 Mincle/ Clec4e levels continually increased in Karp group at 8 and 12 dpi; however, their 251

Discussion 358 

Despite being an important and emerging neglected tropical disease, scrub typhus 359 remains understudied, particularly with respect to how strain-level variation in Ot shapes 360 host innate immune responses and disease severity. Our previous reports have revealed 361 that Karp and Gilliam strains differ markedly in pathogenicity in B6 and CD-1 mice, with 362 Karp causing more severe disease and stronger systemic inflammatory responses than 363 Gilliam (17, 34). In this study, we provided new evidence that these divergent disease 364 phenotypes are associated with fundamentally distinct MΦ transcriptional programs. 365 Specifically, Karp infection drives a coordinated proinflammatory innate immune 366 response characterized by broad activation of PRR pathways; however, Gilliam infection 367 induces a comparatively attenuated inflammatory phenotype accompanied by 368 unexpected enrichment of host cell cycle and DNA replication pathways. These findings 369 of Ot strain-specific innate immune programming as a central determinant of Ot 370 pathogenesis provide a framework linking differential immune sensing to divergent 371 disease outcomes. 372
