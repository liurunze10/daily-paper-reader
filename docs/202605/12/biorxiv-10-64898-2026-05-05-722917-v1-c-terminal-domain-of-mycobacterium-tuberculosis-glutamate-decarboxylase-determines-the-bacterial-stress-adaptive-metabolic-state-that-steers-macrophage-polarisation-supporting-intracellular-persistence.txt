Abstract: 13 

Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb ) adapts metabolically to survive hostile conditions within 14 

alveolar macrophages, but whether specific enzyme -driven metabolic states of Mtb link stress 15 

adaptation to host immuno modulation remains unclear. Using LC -MRM/MS metabolite profiling 16 

and metabolic modeling , we previously identified elevated γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA) 17 

production via the GABA shunt during adaptation to oxidative and acidic stress. Here, we show 18 

that stress -responsive enzymatic efficiency of glutamate decarboxylase (GadB) in producing 19 

GABA , not only mitigates acidic and oxidative stress but also drives macrophage polarisation 20 

towards the M2 phenotype, extending its role beyond bioenergetics into host –pathogen cross -talk. 21

Introduction 55 

Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb ), the causative agent of tuberculosis (TB), survives for 56 

prolonged periods within macrophages despite exposure to multiple host -imposed stresses, 57 

including acidic pH, oxidative stress, nutrient limitation, and hypoxia (1 –3) . Among these, 58 

phagosomal acidification and reactive oxygen species (ROS) represent dominant early 59 

antimicrobial pressures encountered during infection (4 –6) . Notably, acidic and oxidative stresses 60 

are functionally interconnected, as proton accumulation can influence redox reactions, while 61 

oxidative stress can disrupt proton gradients and intracellular pH homeostasis, together imposing 62 

a combined metabolic burden on the bacterium (7, 8) . These coupled stresses perturb central 63 

metabolism and redox balance, necessitating coordinated adaptive responses for the survival of 64 

intracellular bacteria (9 –11) . Consequently, successful persistence of Mtb within macrophages 65

relies on metabolic pathways that simultaneously maintain cytosolic pH, redox homeostasis, and 66 

energy production. 67 

Metabolic remodeling during infection has emerged as a critical determinant of mycobacterial 68 

survival (12) . Systems -level studies, including metabolomics and infection models, have revealed 69 

stress -induced reprogramming of central carbon metabolism, amino acid utilization, and redox 70 

buffering pathways that enable adaptation to hostile intracellular environments (13, 14) .71 

Importantly, these metabolic adaptations extend beyond bacterial physiology and intersect with 72 

host immune responses , thereby modulating macrophage activation states and inflammatory 73 

signaling (15 –17) . Given that macrophage polarisation is tightly linked to metabolic programs, 74 

pathogen -derived metabolites represent a potential mechanism by which Mtb may reprogram host 75 

immunity to favor its persistence. 76 

Our previous metabolomic analysis identified increased accumulation of γ-aminobutyric acid 77 

(GABA) and glutamate in Mtb exposed to acidic and oxidative stress conditions (18) . GABA is 78 

produced from glutamate by the enzyme Glutamate Decarboxylase (GadB), a pyridoxal -5′ -79 

phosphate (PLP) –dependent enzyme that catalyzes a proton -consuming decarboxylation reaction 80 

(19) . In several bacterial systems, this reaction functions as an acid resistance mechanism by 81 

consuming intracellular protons and contributing to pH homeostasis (19, 20) . In addition, the 82 

GABA shunt feeds into central metabolism and can influence redox balance through 83 

NAD⁺/NADH -linked reactions (21 –24) . Recently, various studies have suggested the potential role 84 

for GABA in immunomodulation (25 –28) ; however, whether Mtb GadB -mediated metabolism can 85 

influence host immunometabolic pathways during infection remains an open question. 86 

Furthermore, recent studies using non -pathogenic mycobacteria have suggested a role for 87 

mycobacterial GadB in intracellular survival (29, 30) ; however, the biochemical regulation of 88

In this study, we investigated the biochemical regulation and functional role of Mtb GadB under 92

enhanced catalytic efficiency. Together, our findings position Mtb GadB as a pivotal metabolic 97 

node that links bacterial stress adaptation with host immunomodulation. This underscores the 98

Mtb and the host, thereby shaping host –pathogen interactions and revealing the intricate interplay 100
