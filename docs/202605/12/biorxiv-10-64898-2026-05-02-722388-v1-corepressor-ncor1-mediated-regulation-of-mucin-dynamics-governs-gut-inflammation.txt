Abstract 13 

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) , comprising Ulcerative colitis (UC) and Crohn’s Disease , is a 14 

chronic relapsing immune -mediated inflammatory disorder of the gut . The intestinal mucus layer is 15 

a protective barrier that safeguards direct exposure of epithelium to luminal microbes and antigens. 16 

A prolonged disruption of the mucus layer may contribute to the development of IBD . Lo ss of 17 

mucin -producing goblet cells is a hallmark of UC. The underlying molecular mechanism controlling 18 

goblet regulation remains poorly understood. In the current work, we show a key role for NCoR1 19 

(Nuclear corepressor 1) in goblet cell regulation . A specific downregulation of NCoR1 in intestinal 20 

crypts and goblet cells was observed in human UC and mice models. While NCoR1 was upregulated 21 

during goblet cell differentiation, inflammatory cues downregulated its expression . Experimental 22 

loss of NCoR1 resulted in exacerbated disease in a murine model of colitis , w hereas its upregulation 23 

via Vitamin D le d to a rescue. ChIP -seq led to the identification of KLF -16, a transcription factor ,24 

as a target of NCoR1 . NCoR1 -KLF16 regulatory axis regulate d key goblet cell proteins , including 25 

MUC2 . Mechanistically, t he regulation of M UC2 is modulated by the NCo R1-KLF16 axis, via 26 

mTOR signalling. In conclusion , this work shows a critical involvement of NCoR1 -KLF16 in 27 

governing goblet cell function and intestinal homeostasis. 28

Introduction 32 

Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD) is a chronic, immune -hyper active disease broadly 33 

classified into Ulcerative colitis (UC) and Crohn’s Disease (CD).  The symptoms, d iarrhoea, 34 

abdominal cramps, rectal bleeding, and weight loss manifest in cycles of remission and relapse ,35 

progressively leading to tissue damage and deleterious outcomes 123. In case of UC, disease 36 

pathophysiology  is limited to the mucosal layer and  is an idiopathic inflammatory condition 37 

primarily restricted to the colon,  causing superficial damage to the bowel wall 3 . Owing to a 38 

lack of cure, IBD patients live a very compromised lifestyle, and the disease poses a major 39 

health challenge 45.40 

At the cellular level, IBD is due to failed relationship between gut epithelium and luminal 41 

microbes, leading to a heightened immune response. The exaggeration of immune response is 42 

triggered by dysregulated epithelial signalling , often arising due to altered microbial 43 

composition, defective antigen sensing, or mucus layer abnormality . In healthy tissue, luminal 44 

microbes are prevented from directly contacting host epithelium by a protective mucus layer. 45 

Loss of goblet cells (GCs) , the cells responsible for the production of mucin, is a key hallmark 46 

of UC pathogenesis 67.47 

GCs are specialised epithelial cells, originate from crypt resident stem cells , to secrete mucin 48 

(predominantly MUC2 in the colon) and maintain the mucus layer 8. A healthy colonic 49 

epithelium is composed of 16% of G Cs, which is significantly reduced in UC 910 . Also, GCs50 

are involved in cytokine secretion and antigen presentation, thereby contributing to GCs -51 

immu nocyte cross -talk 11 . While , several transcription factors are involved in cell fate 52 

determination in the gut, the detailed mechanism of GCs differentiation remains poorly 53 

understood . Transcription factors , KLF4 and SPDEF are involved in goblet cell differentiation, 54 

mediating mucin MUC2 regulation 12 .55 

In a recent work, we showed the involvement of SENP7, a deSUMOylase enzyme, as a 56 

mediator of epithelial signalling in IBD. Enhanced SENP7 expression led to signalling events 57 

culminating in pathogenic gamma -delta T-cell infiltration and tissue inflammation. 58 

Interestingly, NCoR1, Nuclear Receptor Corepressor 1 protein , was among the several other 59 

interactors, identified to physically interact with SENP7 1 .60 

NCoR1 is a scaffolding protein involved in modulating gene repression via forming large 61 

protein complexes and recruitment of HDACs 13 14 . NCoR1 involvement in the maintenance of 62 

gut homeostasis has been cited for its importance in protection during DSS -mediated 63

Discussion 294 

In recent developments modulating IBD Pathogenesis, molecular mechanisms driven by 295 

hyperactive immune regulation have been well reported. A constant epithelial -immune cell 296 

crosstalk is required for gut homeostasis via a proper mucosa . Impaired mucus barrier and 297 

epithelium exacerbate inflammation due to infiltrated microbiome -mediated dysbiosis, 298 

eventually leading to immune recruitment triggering inflammation 4. Understanding the goblet 299 

cell mechanism that regulates mucus homeostasis, particularly during ulcerative colitis 300 

pathogenesis 30,31 , becomes important. In this study, we identify a distinct role for NCoR1 in 301 

maintaining mucosal barrier integrity by regulating crypts and goblet cells in the colon, which 302 

was previously known to regulate the immune cells 32 -33 .The role of NCoR1 in goblet cell 303 

regulation has not yet been reported. Here, we highlight the importance of crypt residential 304 

NCoR1 in maintaining the goblet cells and preventing inflammation. 305 

NCoR1 is classically characterized as a transcriptional repressor known to repress genes via 306 

recruitment of histone deacetylases 13 . The role of NCoR1 in combating intestinal inflammation 307 

is not well - understood 15 . NCoR1 modulat es several pathophysiological conditions 34 ,13 ,308 

indicating its diverse functions. Our study reveals a dynamic expression of NCoR1 and its 309

1. Human colon biopsy samples 376 

Human samples were collected from the Department of Gastroenterology at the Al l India 377

collection protocol and with the required ethical clearance. These included Non -IBD (IBD 379 

suspects) (n= 33 ) and UC(n= 46 ) groups with ages between 18 and 60 years, with inclusion and 380 

exclusion criteria specifie d49 . Two to four biopsies from each human subject were collected for 381 

qRT -PCR, Western blotting, and sectioning. Informed consent forms were acquired from all 382 

the patients. The influence of gender was not considered in the present study. UC severity was 383 

determined based on the ulcerative colitis endoscopic index of severity (UCEIS). Ethics 384 

approval for the use of human samples was obtained from both the institutes, the Regional 385 

Centre for Biotechnology (RCB) (IEC -141/05.03.2021, RP -34/2021) and AIIMS (IEC/NP -386

2. Animal Strains and colitis models 388 

The C57BL/6 mouse strain was used for the study. Mice were bred and housed in pathogen -389 

free conditions and provided with sterilized food and water at 25°C with a 12 -hour light and 390 

12 hrs dark cycle in the Small Animal Facility . For inducing colitis, female C57BL/6 mice, 6 -391 

8 weeks old, weighing 18g -20g were used. Mice were fed with 2.5% DSS (w/v) for 3, 5 and 7 392 

days in autoclaved water. Only autoclaved water was given to control mice. To knock down 393

10 mg/kg (20nM/mice). Different conditions included in the study were: (i) Ctrl - mice were 397 

given non -target morpholino, (ii) Ctrl NCoR1KD mice treated with NCoR1 Vivo morpholino, (iii) 398 

DSS mice along with non -target morpholino and (iv) DSS NCoR1KD NCoR1 knockdown mice 399 

along with DSS treatment for 7 days. For upregulation of vitamin D -mediated NCoR1 in 400

CaCl2 mixed with feed for 3 weeks (Ctrl VD ). Animals were fed with 2% DSS (w/v) for 1 week 402 

(3rd week) (DSS). A group of animals were treated with both vitamin D throughout 3 weeks, 403 

along with 2% DSS (only for 3rd week of the experiment) (DSS VD ), called the rescue group. 404

3.Crypt isolation 409 

Frozen colon samples were thawed and cut into 2 -3 pieces of 1 -2cm each and washed in 15ml 410

pieces were washed in 15ml chelation buffer (2% sorbitol, 1%sucrose, 5%FBS in DPBS) once. 412 

Colon were incubated in chelation buffer with 10mM EDTA for 20 mins under constant shaking 413 

at 4°C. The crypts were vortexed for 30 seconds, followed by 30 sec rest for five times. The 414 

supernatant was filtered using 100μm filter and collected, and the remaining crypts were 415 

scraped to detach them and filtered into the respective tubes. The tubes were centrifuged at 416 

800g for 10 mins at 4 degrees to collect the crypts. The crypts were washed in PBS and 417 

collected in 2X Lamillie buffer and denatured at 95oC for 10 mins and quantified further using 418 

the CBX kit. Lysates were subjected to Western blot. 419

Fig 10: Representative Image: (created with BioRender.com)770 

Nuclear Co -repressor1 or NCoR1 is predominantly present in the crypts of the colon in healthy 771 

conditions, but during ulcerative colitis, its expression is reduced in the crypts , and its 772 

expression increases in the infiltrated immune cells. Due to the varied expression of NCoR1, 773 

functions of crypt residential cells alter during the onset of inflammation. In the goblet cell, a 774 

balanced regulation of NCoR1 and its target gene KLF16 is maintained, pursuing a regulated 775 

mTOR pathway and a regulated mucus secretion is attained during healthy conditions. But, 776 

upon inflammation, loss of NCoR1 and KLF16 in the goblet cell tends to activate the mTOR 777 

pathway. This upregulated mTOR pathway leads to reduced mucin production , affecting the 778 

goblet cell function during UC pathogenesis. 779
