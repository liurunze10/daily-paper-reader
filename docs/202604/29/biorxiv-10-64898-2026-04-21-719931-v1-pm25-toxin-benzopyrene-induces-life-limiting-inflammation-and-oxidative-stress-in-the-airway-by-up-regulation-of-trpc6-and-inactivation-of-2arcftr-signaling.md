## 摘要
背景：人群规模长期暴露于高水平大气PM2.5与肺部炎症性疾病风险增加相关。这些疾病的特征是瞬时受体电位通道6（TRPC6）钙通道激活、活性氧（ROS）增加和氧化应激。长期暴露还与寿命缩短，以及心血管疾病、痴呆、帕金森病、阿尔茨海默病、自闭症及自闭症谱系障碍的发病率增加有关。已有研究提出，吸附在PM2.5颗粒表面的苯并[a]芘（B[a]P）是PM2.5中的毒素。但B[a]P驱动肺部炎症性疾病或其他上述适应症的具体机制尚不清楚。假设：近期有报道称B[a]P能与肺部的β2肾上腺素受体（β2AR）发生不可逆且破坏性的结合。因此，我们假设B[a]P是PM2.5中吸附的毒素，且β2AR是导致肺上皮细胞中TRPC6激活的B[a]P受体。结果：为验证该假设，我们将正常人肺上皮极化类器官模型、极化肺上皮16HBE14o-细胞以及雪貂肺气管支气管切片培养物暴露于PM2.5或B[a]P。我们发现PM2.5和B[a]P均能：（i）通过Gi到PI3K/AKT途径不可逆地激活β2AR信号；（ii）通过PI3K/AKT激活IKKβ，进而增加NFκB激活的促炎细胞因子释放，该过程可被PI3K抑制剂LY 294002抑制；（iii）通过内吞回收使激活的β2AR受体脱敏并被破坏；（iv）通过相同过程破坏β2AR的信号复合物伙伴CFTR；（v）由于抑制性CFTR的缺失，激活与CFTR结合的钙通道蛋白TRPC6；从而导致（vi）胞质[Ca2+]浓度升高；（vii）由于线粒体解偶联导致ROS增加；以及（viii）氧化应激表达增加。使用TRPC6抑制剂BI 749327处理可阻断步骤（vi-viii），并保护CFTR免受内吞损失。用PM2.5或B[a]P处理雪貂肺气管支气管切片培养物会导致IL-6分泌增加、TRPC6表达增加，以及β2AR和CFTR表达减少。最后，我们发现肺类器官暴露于B[a]P会显著降低五种微RNA（miR-126a-3p、miR-30b-5p、miR-103a-3p、miR-26a-5p和miR-766-3p）的表达，这些微RNA此前在部署于伊拉克和阿富汗期间暴露于焚烧坑排放PM2.5的服役人员血清中被鉴定出来。结论：PM2.5及其毒素苯并[a]芘（B[a]P）通过增加TRPC6表达和使β2AR/CFTR信号失活，诱导气道炎症和氧化应激。这些发现首次确定了暴露于PM2.5或PM2.5毒素B[a]P本身诱导肺上皮炎症和TRPC6依赖性氧化应激的机制。

## Abstract
Abstract2O_ST_ABSBackgroundC_ST_ABSSustained exposures to high atmospheric levels of PM2.5 at population scale are associated with increased risks for pulmonary inflammatory diseases. These are marked by activation of the TRPC6 (Transient Receptor Potential Canonical type 6) calcium channel, increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) and oxidative stress. Long term exposures are associated with reduced life span, and increased incidences of cardiovascular diseases, dementia, Parkinsons and Alzheimer disease, and increased risk of autism and autism spectrum disorders. It has been proposed that the PM2.5 toxin is benzo[a]pyrene (B[a]P) that is adsorbed to the surface of the PM2.5 particle.. But the mechanism by which B[a]P might drive pulmonary inflammatory diseases, or any other of the indications above, are not known.

HypothesisB[a]P was recently reported to bind irreversibly and destructively to the {beta}2 Adrenergic Receptor ({beta}2AR) in the lung. We have therefore hypothesized that B[a]P is the adsorbed PM2.5 toxin, and that {beta}2AR is the B[a]P receptor responsible for TRPC6 activation in lung epithelial cells.

ResultsTo test this hypothesis, we exposed a polarized organoid model of normal human lung epithelia, polarized lung epithelial 16HBE14o-cells, and tracheobronchial slice cultures from ferret lung to either PM2.5 or B[a]P. We found that both PM2.5 and B[a]P: (i) irreversibly activated of {beta}2AR signaling via Gi to PI3K/AKT; (ii) increased NF{kappa}B-activated release of proinflammatory cytokines through IKK{beta} activation by PI3K/AKT, which was suppressed by the PI3K inhibitor LY 294002 (iii) desensitized and destroyed the activated {beta}2AR receptor by endocytic recycling; (iv) also destroyed {beta}2ARs signalplex partner CFTR by the same process; (v) activated the CFTR-bound calcium channel protein TRPC6 due to loss of inhibitory CFTR; leading to (vi) increased cytosolic [Ca2+] concentration; (vii) increased ROS due to mitochondrial uncoupling; and (viii) increased expression of oxidative stress. Treatment with the TRPC6 inhibitor BI 749327 blocked steps (vi-viii), and preserved CFTR from endocytic loss. Treatment of tracheobronchial slice cultures of ferret lung with either PM2.5 or B[a]P resulted in increased secretion of IL-6, increased expression of TRPC6, and reduced expression of {beta}2AR and CFTR. Finally, we found that exposure of lung organoids to B[a]P significantly reduced expression of the same five microRNAs (miR-126a-3p, miR-30b-5p, miR-103a-3p, miR-26a-5p, and miR-766-3p) previously identified in sera from service members exposed to PM2.5 from burn pit emissions during deployment to Iraq and Afghanistan.

ConclusionPM2.5 and the PM2.5 toxin benzo[a]pyrene (B[a]P) induce inflammation and oxidative stress in the airway by increased expression of TRPC6 and inactivation of {beta}2AR/CFTR signaling. These discoveries mark the first identification of a mechanism by which exposure to PM2.5 or the PM2.5 toxin B[a]P itself can induce inflammation and TRPC6-dependent oxidative stress in lung epithelia.
